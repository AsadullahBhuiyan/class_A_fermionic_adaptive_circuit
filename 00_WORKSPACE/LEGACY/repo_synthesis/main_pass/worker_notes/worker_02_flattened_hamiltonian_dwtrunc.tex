\section{Findings}

\subsection{Flattened Hamiltonian Construction}

The local model is a two-band Chern insulator with mass profile $\alpha(x)=\alpha_1$ in the central topological strip and $\alpha_2$ in the flanks. The benchmark uses $\alpha_1=1$, $\alpha_2=30$, and two domain walls. OW spinors are generated from upper/lower-band projectors and two trial spinors, giving four rank-one projector families per lattice site. The flattened Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm flat}=\sum_{\bm R}\left(P_{A+}(\bm R)+P_{B+}(\bm R)-P_{A-}(\bm R)-P_{B-}(\bm R)\right),
\end{equation}
with numerical Hermitization. The half-filled occupied subspace defines the OW target covariance $G_{\rm OW}=2P_{\rm occ}-I$.

Finite shell radius $n_{\rm shell}$ truncates each OW spinor spatially; \texttt{None} denotes full support. The extra \texttt{dw\_truncation} mask confines each OW spinor to the topological or trivial sector containing its center, imposing hard OW-function boundaries at the two domain walls.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$n_{\rm shell}$}{nshell} and Domain-Wall Truncation}

The Chern diagnostic is sensitive to both OW support and sector leakage. At $N_x\times N_y=20\times40$, $\alpha_1=1$, $\alpha_2=30$, the no-truncation variants saturate near $\mathcal C\simeq0.796$, while full-support DW-truncated variants reach $\mathcal C=0.99999895$. For $n_{\rm shell}=1$, $\mathcal C\simeq0.795$, so the one-shell state captures much of the topology but not full Chern quantization.

Entanglement is more robust: the wall-window log-chord slope is already near $1/6$ at $n_{\rm shell}=1$, and the full-$x$ slope is near $1/3$, consistent with two domain-wall edge modes and total effective $c=1$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\alpha_2$}{alpha2} Sweep}

The focused alpha sweep fixes $\alpha_1=1$, $DW=\mathrm{True}$, $n_{\rm shell}=1$, trial orbital $X$, and scans $\alpha_2\in\{1,1.2,1.4,1.6,1.8,2,2.2,3\}$ with and without DW truncation. The manuscript interpretation is sharp: with \texttt{dw\_truncation=True}, the truncation itself exposes topological edges whenever a sector is topological. If both sides are topological, the extracted central charge is $c_{\rm est}\approx2$; when the $\alpha_2$ flank becomes trivial, the result drops to a robust $c_{\rm est}\approx1$. Thus the sweep counts the chiral channels supplied by the flank sector and locates the phase boundary near $\alpha\simeq2$.

\subsection{Post-Selected Frobenius Comparison}

The Frobenius comparison uses the canonical CPU dynamics entry point \texttt{classA\_U1FGTN.run\_markov\_circuit}, consistent with the repository policy. For $16\times20$, $\alpha_2=30$, $n_{\rm shell}=1$, and 40 cycles, the normalized distance to $G_{\rm OW}$ drops quickly: without DW truncation, $d(0)=1.4132$, $d(10)=0.7529$, $d(40)=0.7529$; with DW truncation, $d(0)=1.4161$, $d(10)=0.7215$, $d(40)=0.7210$. The circuit therefore approaches the OW target rapidly but saturates at a nonzero fixed-point offset.

\section{Interpretation}

The local evidence supports the main repo message: the OW flattened Hamiltonian is a theoretical bridge between adaptive measurement dynamics and the class-A topological target state. DW truncation is not a minor implementation detail; it is a physically active boundary condition in OW-function space. It can expose edge channels even without a mass discontinuity, and in the physical sweep its central-charge floor distinguishes ``topological on both sides'' from ``topological strip against trivial flank.'' The Markov circuit result is best read as approximate preparation of the OW target rather than exact convergence at these sizes and shell radius.
