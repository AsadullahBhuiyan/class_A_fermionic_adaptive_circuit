\section{Findings}
The benchmark constructs an exact Gaussian ground state for a real-space
domain-wall Chern-insulator Hamiltonian.  The model uses two orbitals per unit
cell and the flattening convention
$i(\mu,x,y)=\mu+2x+2N_x y$; the domain-wall slab is implemented by switching
the mass parameter from $\alpha_{\rm triv}$ to $\alpha_{\rm top}$ between two
stored wall locations.  The real-space hopping blocks correspond to the usual
lattice Dirac-Chern Bloch Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
h(\bm{k})=\sin k_x\,\sigma_x+\sin k_y\,\sigma_y+
(\alpha-\cos k_x-\cos k_y)\sigma_z .
\end{equation}
The notebook default benchmark uses $N_x=20$, $N_y=40$,
$\alpha_{\rm triv}=30$, $\alpha_{\rm top}=1$, periodic boundary conditions,
and domain walls at $x=6,14$.

The exact state is obtained by diagonalizing the one-body Hamiltonian, forming
the negative-energy projector $P_-$, and setting
\begin{equation}
G=2P_- - \mathbf{1}.
\end{equation}
For the default periodic benchmark, the stored notebook output reports
$800$ negative and $800$ positive modes and a near-zero finite-size minimum
$|E|=7.313048\times 10^{-15}$, consistent with domain-wall spectral crossings.

The strongest universal edge diagnostic is the fixed-$x$ squared correlator.
The benchmark expects a chiral free fermion edge field to give
$|\langle \psi^\dagger(y)\psi(0)\rangle|^2\sim
\sin^{-2}(\pi y/L)$, hence slope $-2$ versus log chord length.  The exact
domain-wall slices give slopes $-2.02464139$ at $x=6,14$ with
$R^2=0.99983525$; the trivial-side neighbors give slopes $-2.06888554$ at
$x=5,15$ with $R^2=0.99877589$.  Interior neighbors $x=7,13$ instead fall with
slope about $-13.13363802$, showing that the algebraic edge signal is spatially
localized.

The integrated entanglement-contour analysis is also consistent with two
domain-wall edge contributions.  The full-width strip contour has fitted
log-chord slope $m=0.343924$ on both lower and upper branches, while each
three-site wall-centered window has slope $m\simeq 0.1686$.  The near equality
$0.3439 \simeq 2\times 0.1686$ is the cleanest quantitative statement that the
full-width signal is approximately the sum of two equal wall contributions.

The notebook and note emphasize an important convention: when scanning an
$x$ interval, the subsystem is usually not redefined in $x$.  One first computes
the entanglement contour on a full-width strip in $x$, then restricts only the
final contour sum to a diagnostic $x$ window.  This convention applies both to
integrated contour fits and contour-restricted mutual-information diagnostics.

The overcomplete-Wannier flattened Hamiltonian benchmark supports the same
message at a surrogate level.  The note reports that $X$ and $Y$ trial orbitals
produce nearly identical flattened spectra and reproduce exact integrated
contour slopes extremely well: full-width exact $m\approx 0.3439$ versus
$X/Y$ $m\approx 0.3448$, and wall-window exact $m\approx 0.1686$ versus
$X/Y$ $m\approx 0.1691$.  The $Z$ trial orbital remains qualitatively correct
but has larger quantitative shifts.

\section{Interpretation}
These sources support the main repo narrative that the domain-wall geometry is
a controlled exact benchmark for chiral Chern-edge physics.  The exact
Gaussian ground state produces edge-localized algebraic correlations, local
topological response, and conformal log-chord entanglement scaling.  The
contour diagnostics further show that the observable edge signal can be
localized after the full subsystem contour is computed, giving a physically
clean way to assign full-strip entanglement to the two domain walls.

The benchmark also gives evidence for the repository's broader flattened-H or
post-selected-generator picture: the overcomplete-Wannier flattened
Hamiltonian, especially with $X/Y$ trial orbitals, reproduces the exact
entanglement-contour scaling closely enough to be a credible surrogate for
long-time or slow-purification structure.  This is strongest for integrated
entanglement observables and weaker for trial-orbital-dependent spectral
details.

\section{Caveats}
The notebook contains executed textual outputs, but I did not rerun it, so I
treat the stored results as source evidence rather than independently
recomputed facts.  I did not inspect image pixels or load arrays; figure
content is inferred from captions, code, and stored text outputs.  Some
flattened-Hamiltonian details in the live notebook include both truncated and
untruncated branches, while the polished note emphasizes the simpler trial
orbital comparison; this does not change the main conclusion but should be
tracked if the synthesis discusses truncation specifically.

\section{Confidence}
Confidence is high for the exact benchmark's qualitative message and for the
quoted stored numerical slopes, because the RevTeX note, notebook source, and
notebook outputs agree.  Confidence is moderate for broader implications about
adaptive dynamics or post-selected relaxation, because those are interpretive
links made by the benchmark note rather than directly established by the exact
ground-state calculation alone.
