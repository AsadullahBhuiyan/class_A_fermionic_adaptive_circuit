\begin{abstract}
I summarize the text-visible evidence in the small-system and partial-postselection
subtrees, excluding paths containing \texttt{haining}, \texttt{haoyu}, or
\texttt{erroneous}, and excluding prior synthesis notes.  The small-system data
support a conservative statement: the canonical GPU dynamics and downstream CPU
analyses reproduce robust qualitative distinctions between perfect-correction,
deterministic postselection, and intermediate partial-postselection protocols.
However, the same evidence also shows appreciable dependence on initial state,
domain-wall truncation, shell number, geometry, and observable.  The most secure
claim is therefore not a thermodynamic phase statement, but a finite-size
confirmation of protocol-dependent purification and late-time covariance
behavior, with partial postselection interpolating operationally but not yet
establishing a sharp scaling theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Findings}

\subsection{Small-System Campaign Structure}

The small-system bundle is explicitly organized around canonical GPU generation
and local/CPU characterization.  The pure-state snapshot campaign uses
$N_x=16$, $N_y=30,40$, $n_{\rm shell}=1,2$, $\alpha_2=30$,
domain-wall truncation enabled, default initialization, perfect correction,
ten samples, and cycles through $50$; it saves selected covariance snapshots
rather than a full dense history.  The purification campaign starts from the
maximally mixed state and compares perfect correction to deterministic
postselection, with postselection represented by one deterministic sample in the
GPU driver.  The README also distinguishes the current intended
$N16\times 32$, $C=100$, no-domain-wall-truncation purification campaign from
comparison/legacy truncated campaigns.

\subsection{Protocol Dependence in Covariance Histories}

The finite-size covariance-characterization summaries show that perfect
correction and postselection are not merely statistically different samples of
the same late-time process.  In the $N20$ streaming characterization, late
entropy-slope windows are close but distinguishable: for cycles $21$--$50$ the
mean slope is $0.3549$ for $N20\times40$ perfect correction and $0.3419$ for
postselection.  At the same time, the deterministic postselected data have only
one sample, so the comparison is qualitative rather than an uncertainty-resolved
ensemble comparison.

The older $N16$ covariance-history characterization shows a stronger early-time
protocol separation.  For example, at $N16\times30$, the cycle-$10$ entropy
slope is $0.5087\pm0.0482$ for perfect correction but $0.5520$ for the single
postselected trajectory.  The Frobenius successive delta also separates:
for $N16\times20$, perfect correction remains near $6$--$7$ in the displayed
late early cycles, whereas postselection decays from $10.96$ at cycle $2$ to
$0.787$ at cycle $10$.

\subsection{Purification Entropy Contours}

The maximally mixed purification contour evidence is the cleanest control.  In
the current $N16\times32$, $C=100$, \texttt{dw\_truncation=False} campaign,
perfect correction purifies the total entropy essentially to zero:
$S_{100}=4.37\times10^{-4}$ for $n_{\rm shell}=1$ and
$1.32\times10^{-3}$ for $n_{\rm shell}=2$.  Deterministic postselection instead
settles near $2\log 2$: the final totals are $1.3862869$ and $1.3862943$ in
natural-log units for $n_{\rm shell}=1,2$ respectively.

The comparison table in entropy units normalized by $\ln 2$ makes the same
statement sharper.  The postselected final entropy is essentially $2$ bits for
$N16\times32$ with or without domain-wall truncation and for the larger
$N30\times40$ truncated, $n_{\rm shell}=1$ comparison.  Perfect correction,
by contrast, ends far below $2$ bits but with non-negligible sensitivity to
truncation and geometry: for $N16\times32$ the final entropy is
$6.3\times10^{-4}$ bits without truncation and $2.79\times10^{-2}$ bits with
truncation for $n_{\rm shell}=1$, while the $N30\times40$ truncated case ends
near $0.234$ bits.

The contour validation report supports internal consistency of the derived
observables: the stored contour shapes are $(101,16,32)$ for $N16\times32$ runs
and $(101,30,40)$ for the $N30\times40$ comparison, with sum/total consistency
errors at machine precision to roughly $10^{-13}$ depending on geometry.

\subsection{Initial-State and Observable Dependence}

Trace and flattened-energy controls show that initial state and observable
choice matter.  The trace-real campaign explicitly includes both \texttt{maxmix}
and \texttt{default} initial states and both perfect-correction and postselected
protocols.  For $N20\times40$, $C=50$, \texttt{dw\_truncation=True}, the
sample-averaged final $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,G$ under perfect correction is
about $1.55$ for maxmix in one trace campaign and $6.20$ for default
initialization, while deterministic postselection ends numerically near zero in
both cases.  In the higher-sample flattened-energy/charge-variance campaign,
perfect correction reaches a strongly negative flattened energy near
$-1256$, while postselection reaches about $-1273$; the maximally mixed charge
variance falls from $400$ to $0.181$ under perfect correction and to $0.502$ in
the deterministic postselected run.  These controls support protocol dependence,
but also warn against reducing the results to a single scalar order parameter.

\subsection{Partial Postselection Sweep}

The partial-postselection notebook defines a $N20\times40$, $C=40$,
$n_{\rm shell}=1$, \texttt{dw\_truncation=True}, $\alpha_2=30$ sweep over
$postselect\_probability$
\[
0,\;0.1,\;0.25,\;0.5,\;0.75,\;0.9,\;0.95,\;0.99,\;1.
\]
For $p<1$, the runs are trajectory-resolved perfect-correction dynamics with
ten samples and a per-site forced postselection coin; the $p=1$ endpoint is the
deterministic postselected control with one actual sample.  The run index records
the same: lines for $p=0$ through $0.99$ have
\texttt{protocol=perfect\_correction}, ten actual samples, and measurement
frustration payloads, whereas $p=1$ has \texttt{protocol=postselect}, one actual
sample, and no measurement-frustration payload.

At the final displayed cycle and final sample, the Frobenius successive delta
falls from about $11.03$ at $p=0$ to $2.24$ at $p=0.99$, then to $0.0609$ at the
deterministic $p=1$ endpoint.  The real-space Chern marker remains near unity
for the shown $p=0$ and $p=0.99$ final-sample rows, but the deterministic
postselected endpoint is lower, about $0.769$.  This is evidence for a smooth
operational interpolation in the measured dynamics, not by itself evidence for a
sharp transition.

\section{Interpretation}

The strongest synthesis is that the small-system suite confirms the machinery
and exposes a physically meaningful protocol axis.  Perfect correction,
deterministic postselection, and partial postselection produce visibly different
late-time covariance motion, purification entropy, trace observables, flattened
energy, and charge variance.  The purification-contour data are particularly
diagnostic because they start from a maximally mixed state and track a full
spatial entropy contour with stringent internal consistency checks.

The conservative physical reading is the following.  Perfect correction tends to
drive the finite systems much closer to a pure state in the maxmix purification
test than deterministic postselection does.  Deterministic postselection leaves a
stable residual of approximately $2\log 2$ in the tested geometries, suggestive
of a small protected or protocol-induced residual sector.  Partial
postselection suppresses the large trajectory-to-trajectory covariance update
scale as $p$ approaches one, but the available sweep is still a finite
$N20\times40$ study with ten samples for $p<1$ and one deterministic endpoint.

\section{Caveats}

The postselected runs in these GPU conventions are deterministic single-sample
controls.  They are therefore excellent for a protocol endpoint but not an
ensemble uncertainty estimate.

The partial-postselection sweep is finite and narrow: one geometry
($N20\times40$), one shell number, one truncation convention, and ten samples for
$p<1$.  It should not be used to claim a thermodynamic critical point or a
universal interpolation law.

The purification residual near $2\log2$ is robust across the inspected
postselected maxmix cases, but its interpretation requires more theory and
larger-size scaling.  The comparison data show quantitative sensitivity to
domain-wall truncation and geometry, especially for perfect correction.

Several observables were inspected only through summaries and captions/manifests,
not through raw binary arrays.  This is appropriate for synthesis but means no
new independent numerical reductions were performed here.

\section{Confidence}

Confidence is high for the descriptive statements about what campaigns exist,
their parameters, and the qualitative protocol distinctions in the inspected
summaries.  Confidence is moderate for physical interpretation of the residual
postselected purification entropy as a meaningful finite-size sector, because
the pattern is clean but not yet supported here by a systematic scaling collapse.
Confidence is low for any stronger claim of a sharp partial-postselection
transition, universality class, or thermodynamic phase boundary from the present
small-system evidence alone.
