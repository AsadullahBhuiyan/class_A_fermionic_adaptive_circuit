\begin{abstract}
I inspected the lightweight provenance chain for the synthesis figures and tables supporting
\texttt{summary\_of\_results/summary\_of\_results.tex}.  The primary synthesis artifacts are
seven PDF figures under \texttt{summary\_of\_results/figures/}, all regenerated by a single
script, \texttt{summary\_of\_results/scripts/make\_summary\_figures.py}.  They combine
schematic content, CSV summaries, small-to-moderate \texttt{npz} products, and one hard-coded
fallback table copied from an executed notebook.  The strongest provenance is for the
streaming, purification, size-scaling, correlator, and modular-charge CSV-backed panels.  The
weakest provenance is the $\alpha_2$ truncation figure, because the script explicitly falls
back to embedded numerical tables rather than a stable exported CSV.  I did not edit files,
execute notebooks, or load binary arrays.
\end{abstract}

\section{Findings}

\subsection{Primary Synthesis Artifacts}

The figure-generation script identifies itself as a synthesis layer: it reads explicit data
products and performs no circuit simulation.  It also has a guard refusing
an explicitly excluded GPU-stuff directory.  The seven figures are all produced by the script's
\texttt{main()} routine.

The figure provenance separates into four classes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Schematic-only: \texttt{protocol\_taxonomy.pdf}.
\item CSV/manifest backed: \texttt{init\_default\_scaling.pdf},
\texttt{purification\_protocol\_comparison.pdf},
\texttt{critical\_scaling\_summary.pdf}, and \texttt{modular\_charge\_chirality.pdf}.
\item Binary-derived controls: \texttt{trajectory\_vs\_mean\_channel.pdf}, which uses two
\texttt{npz} inputs named in the script.  I verified only their paths and sizes.
\item Notebook-output fallback: \texttt{alpha2\_truncation\_limit.pdf}, whose plotted tables
are embedded directly in the script, with a comment saying the executed notebook contains the
relevant tables but no separate CSV.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Caveats Already Stated in the Synthesis Note}

The note is unusually explicit about ensemble ordering.  It distinguishes
$\overline{O[G_\xi]}$, $O[\overline{G_\xi}]$, and $O[G_{\rm ps}]$, and warns that nonlinear
observables such as entropy, contours, Chern-marker diagnostics, and squared correlations
cannot generally be averaged at the covariance level.  It treats post-selection, the Markov
channel, and the Lindblad limit as controls rather than the physical perfect-correction
ensemble.

The note also states that the current correlator evidence is weaker than the entanglement
evidence.  In the critical-scaling section it says the correlation fits are ``less settled'',
wall-position dependent, and not yet a final universality extraction.  It similarly frames the
$\alpha_2$ sweep as a diagnostic proxy for a hard-wall limit, not a controlled extrapolation.

\subsection{Data-Availability and Claim-Matching Issues}

The main mismatch is in \texttt{purification\_protocol\_comparison.pdf}.  The caption and text
discuss perfect-correction and post-selection charge fluctuations, but the two charge-statistic
manifests used by the plotting script select only the perfect-correction configurations.  The
purification campaign manifest also records that post-selection requested 100 samples but the
canonical GPU driver forced \texttt{samples=1}.  Thus post-selection charge-fluctuation
comparisons in that panel are not supported by an equal-sample charge-statistics table; they
are at best inferred from scalar metrics or from a single conditioned branch.

A second mismatch is the skipped \texttt{N20x60} streaming run.  The scaling-fit log and
characterization manifest report the run was skipped because required derived arrays were
missing.  Therefore the init-default scaling summary is a selected $N_y=30,40,50$ story, not a
complete use of all discovered streaming runs.

A third caveat is the $\alpha_2$ figure.  The figure is useful as a synthesis diagnostic, but
its numerical source is hard-coded fallback data inside the plotting script.  This should not
be treated as archival table provenance until the notebook values are exported into a stable
CSV or JSON table.

\section{Interpretation}

The provenance supports the qualitative narrative: trajectory-resolved perfect correction is
the physical ensemble; post-selection and mean-channel objects are controls; entropy scaling
is the most robust chiral-critical diagnostic; correlator exponents and modular-charge
chirality are consistent but less mature.

For manuscript claims, I would use the following hierarchy.  Claims based on entropy slopes
near $1/3$ and the ensemble distinction are currently high-confidence within the inspected
data products.  Claims based on squared-correlation exponents should remain caveated because
fixed-$x$ fits are strongly unstable while $x$-averaged fits are near the expected exponent.
Claims based on modular-charge chirality should be phrased as signed-drift evidence, not a
precision velocity extraction, since some single-launch velocity fits have low $R^2$ even
though the averaged displacement signs are clear.  Claims involving the $\alpha_2$ hard-wall
limit should cite the diagnostic character of the sweep and the need for an exported table.

\section{Caveats}

I did not inspect notebook outputs directly, execute notebooks, regenerate figures, or load
large binary arrays.  I therefore assess provenance and internal consistency, not numerical
reproducibility from raw covariance histories.

The post-selection charge-fluctuation comparison should be tightened before final use.  The
scalar purification campaign contains post-selected scalar metrics, but the charge histogram
and variance summary manifests used by the synthesis figure select only perfect-correction
runs, while post-selection has actual sample count one.

The $\alpha_2$ truncation plot needs a stable exported table.  Until then it is a useful
author-synthesis figure but has weaker archival provenance than the CSV-backed panels.

The correlator evidence should remain a consistency check rather than a precision exponent
claim.  The inspected summary table shows the same tension stated in the note: fixed-$x$
extractions are not stable, while $x$-averaged diagnostics are much closer to the expected
chiral value.

\section{Confidence}

Confidence is high that the seven \texttt{summary\_of\_results/figures} PDFs are the primary
synthesis figure artifacts and that their immediate provenance is the single summary plotting
script.  Confidence is high for the CSV-backed provenance statements and for the explicit
caveats quoted from the synthesis note and manifests.  Confidence is moderate for numerical
interpretation of the two \texttt{npz}-backed panels because I verified only paths, sizes, and
script usage, not array contents.  Confidence is lower for the $\alpha_2$ panel as an archival
data product because its plotted numbers are embedded fallback values rather than exported
table data.
