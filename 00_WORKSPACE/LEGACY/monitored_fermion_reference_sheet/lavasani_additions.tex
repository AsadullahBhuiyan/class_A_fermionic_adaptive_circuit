{\large\bfseries V. TOPOLOGY AND MONITORED DYNAMICAL PHASES---ADDITIONS}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{41a.} A. Lavasani, Y. Alavirad, and M. Barkeshli,
``Measurement-Induced Topological Entanglement Transitions in Symmetric Random
Quantum Circuits,'' \textit{Nature Physics} \textbf{17}, 342--347 (2021).
[NEW] [\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07243}{arXiv}]
[\href{https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-020-01112-z}{DOI}]

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Summary.} Competing measurements and symmetry-preserving
random unitaries produce distinct symmetry-protected topological and trivial
area-law phases, together with a volume-law phase. In the measurement-only
limit, the transition maps to two copies of two-dimensional percolation; sparse
unitaries split this tricritical point into two critical lines. The work is a
foundational example in which topology belongs to the ensemble of monitored
trajectories and is visible in trajectory entanglement rather than ordinary
linear expectation values.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{41b.} A. Lavasani, Y. Alavirad, and M. Barkeshli,
``Topological Order and Criticality in $(2+1)$D Monitored Random Quantum
Circuits,'' \textit{Phys. Rev. Lett.} \textbf{127}, 235701 (2021).
[NEW] [\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06595}{arXiv}]
[\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.127.235701}{DOI}]

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Summary.} Random Clifford unitaries, single-qubit
measurements, and toric-code stabilizer measurements generate topologically
ordered, trivial area-law, volume-law, and critical trajectory phases in two
spatial dimensions. The phase diagram contains a percolation tricritical point
and a distinct anisotropic self-dual tricritical point. This is the direct
higher-dimensional monitored-circuit precedent for asking how a spatially
patterned dynamical phase terminates at an interface.
