\title{Unequal-time density correlations in Gaussian adaptive circuits}
\author{Working derivation}
\date{September 27, 2026}
\begin{abstract}
We distinguish Gaussian states at individual times from a jointly Gaussian
space-time process. We derive the usual Wick expression when the latter applies
and an exact Gaussian-probe representation of ordered density correlations for
an adaptive Markov circuit. Neither endpoint covariance nor the modular
Hamiltonian alone determines correlations in physical circuit time.
\end{abstract}

\section{Observable and convention}
Let $\hat n_i=\hat c_i^\dagger\hat c_i$ and
$G_{ij}(s)=\operatorname{Tr}[\hat\rho_s\hat c_i^\dagger\hat c_j]$.
Here $i=(x,y,\mu)$ includes the orbital index, and $G$ denotes the ordinary
occupation correlator, not the centered covariance called \texttt{G} in saved files.
For a fixed initial state at $s$ and the subsequent trace-preserving circuit
channel $\mathcal E_{t:s}$, define the ordered, connected correlator
\begin{align}
K_{ij}(t,s)&=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\hat n_i
  \mathcal E_{t:s}(\hat n_j\hat\rho_s)\right],\\
C_{ij}(t,s)&=K_{ij}(t,s)-\langle\hat n_i(t)\rangle\langle\hat n_j(s)\rangle.
\end{align}
The map is extended linearly to the generally non-Hermitian insertion
$\hat n_j\hat\rho_s$. This is the circuit regression definition for fresh
ancillas/Markov dynamics; any feedback memory must be retained or fixed by
conditioning on the past record. It is not the correlation of two invasive
projective density measurements. The real part is the symmetrized connected
correlator. A cell-density correlation sums $C_{ij}$ over both orbital indices.

\section{Jointly Gaussian dynamics: Wick reduction}
For a number-conserving jointly Gaussian process, introduce
\begin{equation}
F_{ij}(t,s)=\langle\hat c_i^\dagger(t)\hat c_j(s)\rangle,
\qquad H_{ij}(t,s)=\langle\hat c_i(t)\hat c_j^\dagger(s)\rangle.
\end{equation}
The anomalous contractions vanish. Wick's theorem gives
\begin{equation}\boxed{C_{ij}(t,s)=F_{ij}(t,s)H_{ij}(t,s).}\end{equation}
This is an elementwise product, not a matrix product or generally a modulus
squared. In particular,
$C_{ij}(s,s)=G_{ij}(\delta_{ij}-G_{ji})$:
the diagonal equals $G_{ii}(1-G_{ii})$, whereas distinct-mode correlations equal
$-|G_{ij}|^2$.

For quadratic unitary evolution write
$\hat{\bm c}(t)=U(t,s)\hat{\bm c}(s)$. Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\boxed{
C_{ij}(t,s)=[U^*G(s)]_{ij}
 [U(\mathbf 1-G(s)^{\mathsf T})]_{ij}.}
\end{equation}
The same expression with $U$ replaced by $X$ applies to a number-conserving
Gaussian dilation $\hat{\bm c}(t)=X\hat{\bm c}(s)+\hat{\bm\eta}$
with independent, zero-mean Gaussian incoming noise and a Gaussian joint input.
Dynamic correlations in quadratic open fermionic systems with linear
Lindblad operators provide a standard setting for this construction
\cite{KosProsen}.

Gaussian conditional states alone do not guarantee these assumptions after
averaging a measurement record. For example, a single mode with occupation
$p$ subjected to an equally weighted identity/parity unitary has vanishing
averaged one-body propagator. Nevertheless its density is conserved and
$C_{nn}=p(1-p)$. Multiplying the separately averaged one-body contractions would
incorrectly give zero. Gaussianity also does not fix a power-law exponent:
the same Gaussian initial state can be evolved under different dynamics.

\section{Exact Gaussian probes for the adaptive circuit}
The following identities do not assume the nonselective channel is Gaussian.
Define two perturbed initial states at $s$:
\begin{align}
\hat\rho_\epsilon^{\rm f}
 &=\frac{e^{\epsilon\hat n_j/2}\hat\rho_s e^{\epsilon\hat n_j/2}}
 {\operatorname{Tr}(e^{\epsilon\hat n_j}\hat\rho_s)},\\
\hat\rho_\epsilon^{\rm p}
 &=e^{-i\epsilon\hat n_j}\hat\rho_s e^{i\epsilon\hat n_j},\qquad
f_i^{a}(\epsilon)=\operatorname{Tr}[\hat n_i\mathcal E_{t:s}(\hat\rho_\epsilon^{a})].
\end{align}
Both inputs remain Gaussian when $\hat\rho_s$ is Gaussian. Differentiating
the normalized filter and the unitary phase rotation yields
\begin{equation}\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}C_{ij}=\left.\partial_\epsilon f_i^{\rm f}\right|_0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Im}C_{ij}=\frac12\left.\partial_\epsilon f_i^{\rm p}\right|_0.}
\end{equation}
Indeed the two state derivatives are
$\{\hat n_j,\hat\rho_s\}/2-G_{jj}\hat\rho_s$ and
$-i[\hat n_j,\hat\rho_s]$, respectively. Hermiticity preservation relates
the two operator orderings by complex conjugation.

These probes have explicit single-particle formulas. With
$P_j=|j\rangle\langle j|$, $u=e^\epsilon-1$, $p=G_{jj}$, and
$D=e^{\epsilon P_j/2}$,
\begin{align}
G^{\rm f}_\epsilon
 &=D\left[G-\frac{u}{1+up}GP_jG\right]D,\\
G^{\rm p}_\epsilon&=e^{i\epsilon P_j}G e^{-i\epsilon P_j},\\
\left.\partial_\epsilon G^{\rm f}_\epsilon\right|_0
 &=\tfrac12(P_jG+GP_j)-GP_jG.
\end{align}
The first identity follows by Wick contraction and
$e^{\epsilon\hat n_j}=\hat{\mathbf 1}+u\hat n_j$; it also holds for pure
Gaussian states by continuity.

For each probe, evolve the \emph{same circuit rule} with its own correct Born
probabilities, not a frozen sequence of outcomes without likelihood weights.
The difference quotient at $\pm\epsilon$ approximates the derivatives above;
finite-$\epsilon$ convergence and Monte Carlo uncertainty must be tested.
Common random numbers can couple runs, but outcome probabilities must still
be recomputed. At $s>0$, one may condition on a Gaussian past trajectory.
To obtain the full ensemble connected correlator, average the unconnected
correlators first and subtract ensemble means: averaging conditional connected
correlators alone misses the covariance of conditional means.

\section{Implications for the present data}
The stored spectra, entropy contours, and final covariance determine equal-time
observables but not the two-time propagator or the operator-insertion channel.
The physical-time propagator is not $e^{-i h_{\rm modular}(t-s)}$ unless that
evolution is explicitly chosen. New probe evolution or a faithful recorded
instrument with insertion propagation is required for the circuit correlator.
The scalar entropy histories can instead supply classical trajectory-level
two-time statistics; those are different observables and are nonstationary
during purification. Power-law-looking purification is not by itself evidence
of a stationary power-law quantum correlator.

\paragraph{Validation.}
\texttt{test\_unequal\_time\_gaussian.py} checks the Wick expression, both
single-particle probe updates, and the complete complex density correlator
against exact three-mode Fock-space calculations for quadratic unitary evolution
and a mixture of Gaussian unitaries. It also checks the dephasing counterexample.
