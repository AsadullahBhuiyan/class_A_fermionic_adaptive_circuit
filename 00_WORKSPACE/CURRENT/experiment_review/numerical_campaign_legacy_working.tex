\begin{abstract}
This document is a preregistered numerical specification for the proposed PRX Quantum
manuscript on a postselection-free adaptive class-A fermion circuit.  Its stochastic
campaigns remain prospective and license no present-tense production claim.  Completed
deterministic calibration or reconstruction results are labelled explicitly and are not
pooled with production data.  The contract fixes the physical
ensemble, system-size sequences, sample counts, fixed-duration logic, saved data, estimator order,
controls, uncertainty analysis, acceptance tests, claim downgrades, and five-figure
architecture.  Every GPU circuit trajectory must be generated by the canonical
\path{classA_U1FGTN_gpu.run_markov_circuit} entry point on an A100-class 40~GB
runtime.  The central acceptance standard is deliberately conjunctive: quantitative
revalidation of the inherited BPJ bulk, a $c=1$ and level-one $U(1)$ wall state, physical chiral response, an integer
twist spectral flow, a wall-localized tangent sector controlling purification, and
universal information recoverable from trajectory-resolved records must all survive thermodynamic,
transverse, temporal, and protocol controls.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and evidentiary rules}

All stochastic production campaigns below retain status \planned.  The deterministic B0
review calibration and the compact M1/L1 atlas reconstruction are completed and recorded
explicitly; the full M1/L1 production matrix remains planned.  No
saved plot, notebook output, or previous fit
is admissible as production evidence.  Repository artifacts may be cited only in a field
labelled ``legacy comparison,'' and only when the observable and protocol have been checked
at the path level.  Such a comparison is never pooled with production data.

The physical object is the Born ensemble of adaptive trajectories.  For a record
$\xi=(m_1,\ldots,m_T)$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 K_\xi&=K_{m_T|\xi_{<T}}\cdots K_{m_1},
 &p_\xi&=\qTr(K_\xi\rho_0K_\xi^\dagger),\\
 \rho_\xi&=K_\xi\rho_0K_\xi^\dagger/p_\xi .
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Perfect correction samples every $m_t$ from its conditional Born probability and then
applies the prescribed feedback.  Forced postselection is a control.  The mean channel is
a second, inequivalent control.  For every nonlinear observable,
\begin{equation}
 \E_\xi[O(C_\xi)]\ne O(\E_\xi[C_\xi])
\end{equation}
in general.  The estimator must therefore be evaluated per trajectory before equal-weight
averaging.  Since the trajectories are Born sampled, no second probability weight is
applied.

Table~\ref{tab:gates} pass.  A campaign may produce an interesting result while failing its
predeclared gate; in that case the corresponding weaker wording, not a modified estimator,

\section{PRX Quantum thesis and inherited BPJ baseline}

The numerical program is not designed to re-establish the premises already supplied by
the Bhuiyan--Pan--Jian (BPJ) construction~\cite{BhuiyanPanJian2026}.  BPJ already provides the mEO/sTM setting, a
postselection-free finite-range class-A adaptive instrument, Chern trajectory ensembles,
programmable dynamical domain walls, finite-range parallelization, an ancilla--fSWAP
implementation route, and quantum-simulation prospects on encoded-qubit or native-fermion
hardware.  It also reports coherent-noise robustness and distinct trajectory and averaged
thresholds.  Accordingly, this manuscript shall not advertise postselection freedom,
ancilla feedback, Gaussian simulability, finite-range state preparation, or the mere
existence of a domain-wall slow mode as its standalone novelty.

The proposed advance is the conjunction of results absent from that baseline.  The
perfect-correction instrument should program a \emph{universal chiral defect} across
typical retained Born trajectories: each trajectory may occupy a different definite
system-charge sector, but the ensemble shares $c_{\rm edge}=1$ and level-one $U(1)$ wall data,
its sector wandering is localized at the walls, its response and flux flow are oriented,
and its slow purification is controlled by a singular conditional tangent sector.  The
strongest version further recovers defect data from the fixed-word trajectory-operator
spectrum and shows
precisely what survives or disappears in the unconditional channel.  This is the unique
intersection to which every headline panel must contribute.

The quantum-simulation proposition is therefore operational rather than computational.
It is not a quantum-advantage claim, nor a claim that an adaptive platform can prepare a
Chern state for the first time.  It is a protocol for realizing and interrogating a
programmable topological defect ensemble without rejecting records, with a hierarchy of
record-only, fixed-word operator, number-measurement, and covariance-tomography observables.  A result
that cannot be placed in this hierarchy or tied to one of Gates G1--G5 is supplemental or
deferred even if it is numerically interesting.

\section{Quantum-simulation implementation and operational observables}

The primary model is the fresh-ancilla, perfect-correction idealization of the BPJ adaptive
circuit.  For a controller mode $\chi_j$ with target occupation $s_j\in\{0,1\}$, measure
$n_{\chi_j}$ and retain the Born outcome $m_j$.  If $m_j\ne s_j$, prepare a fresh local
ancilla mode in occupation $s_j$, fSWAP it with $\chi_j$, and trace out or reset the outgoing
ancilla.  Thus the system charge transfer is
\begin{equation}
 Y_j=s_j-m_j,
\end{equation}
and every outcome is a valid retained run.  No success flag, trajectory rejection, or
reweighting is permitted in the primary data.  The older dynamical-bath implementation,
where correction succeeds only when a reused ancilla happens to have the needed
occupation, is an imperfect-feedback control rather than the definition of perfect
correction.

For $n_{\rm shell}=1$, an instrument block may be compiled by a number-conserving Gaussian
basis rotation on the $3\times3$-cell patch that maps $\chi_j$ to a readout mode, followed
by occupation readout, classical feedforward, target-occupation ancilla preparation,
fSWAP, and the required inverse basis rotation.  The hardware report shall give the
two-mode-gate count and depth of this compilation as $d_{\rm inst}$, the measurement and
feedforward latency, the number of concurrently live ancillas, reset fidelity, and the
colored-sweep bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:colored-depth}.  The same ancilla pool can be recycled
between color/channel blocks.  Fermionic locality does not automatically imply qubit
locality in two dimensions: a qubit implementation must state its Jordan--Wigner,
Bravyi--Kitaev, swap-network, or auxiliary-gauge encoding and include that mapping
overhead.  A native fermionic processor may use the fermionic compilation directly.

Because controllers within each conflict-graph color have disjoint support, their local
instruments commute and admit simultaneous execution on a quantum simulator.  A numerical
evaluation of such a color block may nevertheless apply its instruments sequentially,
which samples the identical joint Born law while avoiding a separate correlated multi-mode
sampler.  This optional finite-color compilation is a quantum-simulation resource
advantage, not a requirement of the production numerics.  Reordering noncommuting color
blocks relative to the production random-serial word would define a different microscopic
schedule and is not claimed to leave finite-size trajectories unchanged.

The operational hierarchy is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the measurement/correction record, activity, transfer FCS, and total system-charge
      history require no state tomography;
\item boundary charge profiles, Eq.~\eqref{eq:total-charge-variance}, and the real-time
      response require repeated occupation measurements and local source control;
\item the one-body covariance, local marker, and occupation spectrum require a
      correlation-matrix tomography protocol;
\item R\'enyi entropies require replicas, randomized measurements, or an equivalent
      interferometric protocol, while modular-Hamiltonian evolution may remain a
      trajectory-reconstruction diagnostic rather than a direct hardware primitive.
\end{enumerate}
The simulation package shall attach shot-complexity and calibration estimates to each
observable proposed as operational.  Since Gaussian circuits are classically
simulable, the manuscript does not claim computational quantum advantage.  The quantum
simulation value is controlled realization of a programmable topological defect and access
to typical conditioned states and their associated trajectory records, without conditioning on an
exponentially unlikely favorable record.

This implementation inherits rather than supersedes the BPJ hardware proposal.  Its concrete
new simplification is that a fresh ancilla is prepared in the target occupation $s_j$ for
each wrong branch, so the correction is deterministic once the Born outcome is known; no
ancillary redistribution layer or finite bath-occupation success probability is part of
the default model.  Production resource tables must compare this fresh-ancilla cost with
the reused-bath BPJ primitive instead of presenting the whole measurement--fSWAP idea as
new.

The ensemble language must also remain precise.  Starting from a definite total charge,
each conditioned trajectory lies in a definite system-charge sector after every recorded
gain/loss event, while the ensemble spans different sectors.  The intended result is
therefore a Born-sampled ensemble of boundary states with shared infrared data, not one
deterministically prepared ket and not an individual charge-indefinite pure state.  Campaign
S1 must demonstrate that the trajectory-to-trajectory charge wandering is localized at the
walls and separately extract the within-trajectory current-algebra coefficient.

\section{Reproducibility, sharding, and output schema}

Each campaign has an immutable JSON manifest containing a schema version, git commit,
canonical entry point, environment versions, device, dtype, complete geometry and protocol,
observer definitions, root seed, global sample-index allocation, shard RNG seed, and
analysis version.  The present GPU engine uses one exact generator stream per immutable
five-trajectory shard and checkpoints its pre-run and post-run states.  It does not claim
partition-invariant counter-based randomness: changing the shard partition defines a new
versioned ensemble rather than an exact rearrangement of the same 100 random words.

A production directory contains
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \code{manifest.json},\quad \code{shards/},\quad \code{merged/},\\
 \code{analysis/},\quad \code{figures/},\quad \code{logs/}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Sample indices are assigned to shards before execution.  The merger rejects duplicate or
missing indices and any manifest hash mismatch.  Atomic permanent checkpoints are written
after each observation block and contain the record cursor, RNG state, accumulated
$-\log p$, compact observer accumulators, and, when present, tangent QR log sums and
declared low-subspace products.  A covariance can exist only in live memory or local
scratch during the active stage; it is excluded from the permanent checkpoint schema.
Replaying any immutable shard from its saved pre-run RNG state and frozen word must agree
within declared tolerances.  Arbitrary repartitioning of the current stateful RNG stream is
not an exact-reproducibility operation.

Required raw products include per-trajectory scalars, not only ensemble averages; bit-packed
replay records for every stochastic family;
conditional log probabilities or lossless per-cycle sums; numerical residuals;
censor/null masks; and a completion table.  Covariances are evaluated on the fly.  A parent
covariance needed by H1--H3 is a transient shard-local input, not a permanent ensemble
product: H1/H2 consume it during the live parent process, while H3 reconstructs it from the
saved initial RNG state, ordered word, outcomes, and engine hash.  Analysis writes
figure-ready CSV/NPZ files and a fit JSON containing the input data hash, fit window,
covariance model, bootstrap distribution, and sensitivity fits.  Plotting scripts do not
perform unrecorded fits.

Every observable declared anywhere in this contract is retained before ensemble reduction
with logical axes \code{(sample,cycle,...)} at cycle 0 and after every completed cycle
through the declared final cycle.  This includes scalar, vector, profile, spectrum, and
marker outputs with their natural trailing axes.  Means, bootstrap intervals, fits, and
plotting-window extracts are derived products and never replace the complete raw array.
The merger validates the exact sample IDs and the complete cycle coordinate
$0,1,\ldots,T$ for every declared observable, rejecting missing or duplicate entries.
This retention rule does not require a dense covariance at every cycle: observers consume
the live covariance and save their outputs, while full covariances remain restricted to
explicitly declared checkpoints or exact replay.  Any intentionally sparse state product
must be called \emph{checkpoint-resolved}, with its schedule recorded in the manifest; it
must never be described as cycle-resolved.

\section{Master trajectory families and reuse rule}

A new independent trajectory family is required only when at least one component of the
following key changes:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak K=(&N_x,N_y,\text{controller construction and parameters},\\
              &\text{initial ensemble},\text{serial schedule law},\\
              &\text{feedback/noise law},n_{\rm shell}).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:trajectory-family-key}
\end{equation}
Changing the observer, fit, source position, analysis window, or deterministic replay does
not define a new Born family.  All campaigns sharing $\mathfrak K$ must consume one common
set of independently seeded trajectories rather than launch campaign-specific copies.
One trajectory remains the independent sampling unit even when many cycle snapshots,
periodic origins, subsystem positions, sources, or twists are derived from it.

For the primary pure-state wall and its matched wall-off control, run one master family per
accepted size for exactly $2N_y$ complete cycles from an independently sampled product
state.  No physical cycles are discarded as burn-in.  The same family supplies S1, T1,
B2, the late-time states for H1,
the unperturbed parents and time origins for H2, and the frozen words for H3.
Response continuations in H2 and twist replays in H3 are derived jobs
attached to a parent trajectory ID; they are not new independent samples.

For the redesigned G4, run an independent $S=25$ max-mix family per wall/control geometry
and size from $t=0$ through exactly $2N_y$.  For redesigned G5, run a distinct $S=25$
half-filled Haar--Slater pure family per wall/control geometry and size over the same
duration.  These versioned families are not pooled with the legacy P2 or T1 masters.
Uniform bulk, secondary-wall, changed-$\alpha$, changed-noise, imperfect-feedback,
postselected, changed-$n_{\rm shell}$, and changed serial-word controls have different keys
and therefore cannot be manufactured from a baseline trajectory.  B0 is deterministic;
the required B1 surrogate-attraction test is a separate two-family $S=25$ calculation
executed last.  H1 modular evolution and H3 fixed-record flux evolution are deterministic
descendants of saved states or records.  M1/L1 is instead a sample-free autonomous CPU
campaign with its own immutable analytic-channel configuration.

Each pure-state master trajectory saves:
\begin{enumerate}
\item its sample seed, initialization hash, controller hash, complete ordered site/channel
word, bit-packed outcomes, per-event conditional log probability, $X_j$, $Y_j$, and the
system charge after every event or a losslessly reconstructible block representation;
\item every declared observable evaluated from the live state and saved separately for
every sample at every cycle, including cycle 0; transient parent covariances at the
declared diagnostic, tangent-alignment, late-time S1, H2-source, and final checkpoints are
consumed shard-locally by H1/H2 and then discarded, while H3 regenerates them recordwise and the permanent
archive retains the initial RNG state, ordered record, engine hash, checkpoint cursor, and
replay hashes needed to regenerate them;
\item per trajectory, cycle, and R\'enyi index, the $y_0$-averaged full-strip entropy
curve, selected $y_0$-resolved validation curves, unnormalized wall-window contour sums,
subsystem spectra, charge-susceptibility spectra $q_a=\nu_a(1-\nu_a)$, matched
entropy--variance residual curves, interval-charge cumulants, density and charge profiles, structure
factors, the full fixed-slice array $C_{G,\xi}(x,r_y)$, its wall/bulk derived curves,
topology and purity diagnostics, and the simultaneous
$N_{\mathcal D,\xi}$ needed for sector-resolved fits;
\item tangent QR log increments, null masks, aligned low-subspace projectors and densities,
block-$R$ factors at declared checkpoints, and the initial frame seed; and
\item observer residuals, censor masks, completion status, and enough accumulated score data
or replay information to reconstruct the H2 conditional and score terms.
\end{enumerate}
The max-mix master obeys the same complete per-sample, per-cycle observable contract and
additionally saves its complete start-to-time record and physical tangent QR products on
the same realized trajectory.  Dense Choi covariance tracking is disabled: no reduced
Choi block is propagated, diagonalized, or archived.  The purification and tangent
products are compared statistically at the trajectory and finite-size levels rather than
through an exact Choi Gram identity.  Scan-only families may declare a smaller observable
set, but every observable they do declare is still saved for every included sample and
cycle; they must not discard either axis or save only ensemble means.  Neither family
archives a dense covariance history.

\subsection{Optional B1: controller-frame surrogate-attraction diagnostic}
\label{camp:B1}
\paragraph*{Claim and status.}
Status \planned\ final-priority appendix diagnostic.  This calculation asks one question:
whether the adaptive circuit approaches the artificial ground-state manifold of the
signed controller-frame operator over the finite interval $0\leq t\leq2N_y$.  It is not a
manuscript gate, an equilibrium model of the circuit, evidence for topology or a wall CFT,
or a claim about infinite-time relaxation.

\paragraph*{Formal object.}
For controller targets $s_j\in\{0,1\}$ and normalized rank-one projectors $P_j$, define
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm frame}=\sum_j(1-2s_j)P_j .
 \label{eq:controller-frame-operator}
\end{equation}
The static target cost over rank-$N$ Slater projectors is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F[C]
 =\sum_js_j+\qTr(H_{\rm frame}C),\qquad C^2=C,\quad\qTr C=N.
 \label{eq:static-target-cost}
\end{equation}
Ky Fan minimization therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 C_\star=\sum_{a=1}^{N}|u_a\rangle\langle u_a|,\qquad
 F_\star=\sum_js_j+\sum_{a=1}^{N}\epsilon_a^\uparrow(H_{\rm frame}).
 \label{eq:static-frame-minimum}
\end{equation}
This evaluates all constraints on one common covariance.  The actual circuit is instead a
stochastic ordered sequence of Born measurements and feedback corrections and is not
generated by $H_{\rm frame}$.  The object called the flattened Hamiltonian in the legacy
notebooks is therefore only a static surrogate; its spectrum is not generally $\pm1$, and
it is neither the conditioned tangent operator nor the mean channel.

\section{Topology and wall construction}

\paragraph*{Logic of the first gate.}
Campaigns P1 and W1 jointly constitute Gate G1.  They answer two logically separate
questions that must not be inferred from one another.  First, P1 asks whether the
finite-range controllers actually retain the intended bulk topology: the practical
$n_{\rm shell}=1,2$ instruments are compared with the untruncated
$n_{\rm shell}=\texttt{None}$ reference on uniform periodic topological and trivial
systems.  A chiral-looking wall observable alone cannot establish this bulk premise.
Second, after that premise is verified, W1 asks whether two microscopically different
ways of programming the wall converge to the same long-distance defect theory.

\paragraph*{Two programmable wall constructions.}\mbox{}\linebreak
For the \emph{explicit-interface} construction,
\code{dw\_truncation=False}: the physical lattice remains periodic, controller orbitals
centered on both sides retain their finite-range tails across the interface, and a wall is
created by changing the controller from the topological parameter
$\alpha_{\rm top}=1$ to the trivial parameter $\alpha_{\rm triv}=30$.  The interface is
therefore the dynamical analogue of a coupled topological--trivial material boundary.
For the \emph{support-terminated} construction, \code{dw\_truncation=True}: each
controller Wannier orbital is masked to the domain-wall sector containing its center and
renormalized, while the excluded exterior is prepared according to the active-slab
convention.  This terminates the one-particle controller frame at the interface.  It is
therefore analogous to imposing open boundary conditions on the controller/Wannier
orbitals, even though the underlying physical lattice is still a torus.  On a periodic
geometry the support mask necessarily creates a pair of such boundaries.

\paragraph*{Meaning of construction independence.}
The two prescriptions are not identical circuits and need not agree at the lattice scale.
If, after the transverse-width and opposite-wall leakage tests, they give compatible bulk
markers, wall localization lengths, entanglement coefficients, charge data, correlators,
and tangent observables, the result is evidence that the emergent chiral wall theory is
independent of whether the defect was produced by a topological mass interface or by an
OBC-like termination of controller support.  This is the programmable-wall universality
claim tested by W1.  If they do not converge, the correct conclusion is that the circuit
supports two distinct programmable wall realizations, not that one dataset may be silently
substituted for the other.

\paragraph*{Existing methodology comparison.}
The executed notebook
\path{notebooks/flattened_hamiltonian_analysis/flattened_hamiltonian_analysis.ipynb}
already compares both constructions at $N_x\times N_y=20\times40$ for
$n_{\rm shell}=0,1,2,\texttt{None}$.  For the topological--trivial benchmark at
$n_{\rm shell}=1$, the untruncated and support-terminated full-width log-chord slopes are
$0.333938$ and $0.333983$, while the corresponding one-wall-window slopes are $0.166756$
and $0.166784$.  At $n_{\rm shell}=2$ the full-width slopes are $0.340314$ and $0.340096$
and the one-wall-window slopes are $0.168203$ and $0.168129$.  These near-equalities are
strong static evidence for a common infrared wall theory once $n_{\rm shell}\geq1$.
The focused notebook
\path{notebooks/flattened_hamiltonian_analysis/flattened_hamiltonian_alpha2_sweep.ipynb}
provides the clean OBC test: it sets $\alpha_1=\alpha_2$ so that no mass interface exists,
retains \code{dw\_truncation=True}, and finds $c_{\rm est}\simeq2$ throughout the uniform
topological regime before the signal collapses toward zero in the uniform trivial regime.

These are flattened-Hamiltonian/controller-frame comparisons, not fresh-ancilla Born
production.  Moreover, their finite-$n_{\rm shell}$ static Chern estimator is approximately
$0.795$ for both constructions at $n_{\rm shell}=1,2$, rather than quantized to one; only
the full-support support-terminated case reaches one in that diagnostic.  The notebooks

\begin{align}

\end{align}

\end{equation}
Only the logarithmic coefficient of $\E_\xi[F_\xi^{\rm q}(A)]$ is used to infer the

 &\quad+\Var_{N_{\mathcal D}}\!\left(
   \E_{\xi|N_{\mathcal D}}[\langle N_A\rangle_\xi]\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:classical-charge-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The first term is fixed-sector shape variation and the second is the contribution from
sector wandering.  Max-mix trajectories enter this decomposition only after both the
global purity deficit and the within-trajectory quantum variance of $N_{\mathcal D}$ fall

 \label{eq:G5-tangent-dictionary}

 \label{eq:G5-radial-gaps}
\end{equation}

The deterministic M1/L1 campaign is not a Colab package; it lives under
\path{00_WORKSPACE/CURRENT/mean_channel_lindblad_cpu_campaign/}.  Two or three stochastic
claims are bundled only when they consume the same trajectory family or a
deterministic descendant of it.  Bundling never licenses pooling different initial
ensembles, controllers, schedule laws, or noise values.
The \code{maxmix\_master} and \code{scans\_and\_controls} runners also require a
checksummed schema-versioned launch-decision file.  P2 cannot launch until T1 is marked
viable; each S2 or M2--M3 case checks only the P1, S1, or T1 decisions on which that case
actually depends.  A template remains \code{pending} by default, so copying a later bundle
to Drive cannot silently bypass the campaign order.

The three production lanes contain 148, 50, and 677 shards respectively, for 875 before
strict reuse; \code{scans\_and\_controls} contributes 653.  The speed-optimized execution
unit is one live five-trajectory shard.  S1, T1, and B2 are
streamed during its single physical evolution; H1 consumes the declared late covariances
before they are released, and H2 forks its finite-difference continuations from the four
live origin states instead of replaying the common prefix for every source.  Each stage
writes an atomic compact receipt, so a later-stage failure does not invalidate completed
parent products.  H3 is intentionally not fused into this all-or-nothing invocation: its
65--129 full twist replays dominate runtime and use independent restart checkpoints based
on the compact RNG state and frozen record.

Google Drive is a staging area rather than the permanent raw-data archive.  At most one
geometry--size--control arm is live at a time.  The $S=25$ sample list is assigned before
execution to deterministic five-trajectory shards.  A shard runs first in Colab local
storage, computes all streaming observables, writes an atomic manifest and checksums, and
only then copies one compact archive to Drive.  The permanent shard contains ensemble-
resolved observables, replay-capable ordered controller/outcome records, the pre-run RNG
state, provenance, and replay hashes.  Per-event float64 probabilities are used live where H2
requires them but are reduced to per-cycle sums before Drive archival.  A dense parent
covariance may exist only as local in-memory or scratch state while observables or immediate
descendants are evaluated.  It is never an ensemble-level archival product: descendants
that cannot run in the same process regenerate the parent exactly from the pre-run RNG
state and frozen record and must pass replay-residual and checksum tests.  Dense covariance
histories are never saved merely for convenience.

The 15~GB Drive allocation may be filled deliberately: after one experiment arm is
complete, its checksummed archives are downloaded to the cluster and the staging area is
cleared before the next arm.  Compact replay records are therefore retained whenever they
make an experiment independently reproducible; Drive scarcity is not used to weaken a
record.  The routine ceiling for any one staged arm or live auxiliary bank is 12~GiB, with
12--14~GiB allowed only as an explicitly reported edge case.  A preflight above 14~GiB
must split the arm or immediate descendant stages.  These storage limits are separate from
the 40~GB A100 device-memory contract and do not license sample covariance archives.

The canonical engine has $N_{\rm layer}=2N_xN_y$.  At $N_x=20,N_y=80$, one dense
$N_{\rm layer}\times N_{\rm layer}=3200\times3200$ complex128 one-particle covariance
therefore occupies $163.84$~MB (decimal units), not $41$~MB.  Three such checkpoints for
25 trajectories require $12.288$~GB for one arm before records, tangents, or controls;
even exact Hermitian-triangle storage requires approximately $6.146$~GB.  Consequently
wall and matched-control arms are processed separately, and a five-trajectory shard is
reduced to compact observables before the next shard is admitted.  Parent covariances are
not copied to Drive, including as a nominally temporary production artifact.  H3 keeps
every record's complete twist circle on one shard; T1 keeps every ordered QR history on one
trajectory shard.

The implementation exposes these contracts directly in the canonical GPU engine.
\code{record\_observer} receives the ordered controller word, Born outcomes, branch
probabilities, feedback targets, and cycle/update/sample identifiers without changing the
dynamics.  \code{frozen\_schedule} together with \code{frozen\_outcomes} replays that exact
conditional branch.  \code{set\_controller\_twist(phi,gauge,seam\_y)} applies an absolute
rather than cumulative controller twist, with seam and uniform gauges used as mutual
controls.  \code{onsite\_phase\_noise\_sigma} inserts the M3 onsite phase kick once after
each complete adaptive cycle, while \code{noise\_observer} records per-cycle moments and a
SHA-256 digest of the realized phase field.  Exact regeneration uses the pre-run RNG state,
engine hash, and configuration; the full field is not copied to Drive.  Production
archives save these compact records and API conventions, not samplewise
covariance matrices.

M1/L1 has no fixed-schedule descendant, trajectory shard, CUDA branch, Drive archive, or
Colab runtime.  Its CPU runner constructs the exact canonical frame operators, solves the
continuous Lyapunov problem, and applies exact finite Gaussian affine channels by binary
composition.  Independent cases are process-parallel with explicit CPU affinity and
one-thread BLAS by default.  The selected-observable schema writes no covariance, and it
permits momentum-resolved arrays only for $n_{\rm shell}=\mathrm{None}$.

\section{Overlap-section winding and the occupied Bloch bundle}
\label{app:overlap-winding}

This appendix fixes the formal meaning and numerical implementation of the OW overlap
winding used in the B1 legacy summary.  Consider the two-band Bloch Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 h(\mathbf{k})&=\mathbf n(\mathbf{k})\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma,\\
 \mathbf n&=(\sin k_x,\sin k_y,\alpha-\cos k_x-\cos k_y),
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:overlap-qwz-model}
\end{equation}
with lower-band projector
$P_-(\mathbf{k})=[\mathbf{1}-\widehat{\mathbf n}(\mathbf{k})\cdot
\boldsymbol\sigma]/2$.  The fixed $X$-trial spinor is
$|\tau_X\rangle=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt2$.  For no truncation take
$|\phi_{\rm none}(\mathbf{k})\rangle=|\tau_X\rangle$.  A square finite-shell field is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 |\phi_n(\mathbf{k})\rangle
 &=\sum_{\mathbf r\in[-n,n]^2}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r}
 C_{\mathbf r}|\tau_X\rangle,\\
 C_{\mathbf r}
 &=\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d^2q}{(2\pi)^2}
 e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}P_-(\mathbf q).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:overlap-finite-shell-field}
\end{equation}

Let $L\to T^2$ be the occupied line bundle.  On momentum patches $U_i$, choose normalized
frames satisfying $|u_j\rangle=e^{i\chi_{ij}}|u_i\rangle$ on $U_i\cap U_j$.  The local
overlap representatives
\begin{equation}
 f_{n,i}(\mathbf{k})=\langle u_i(\mathbf{k})|\phi_n(\mathbf{k})\rangle
 \label{eq:overlap-local-representative}
\end{equation}
then satisfy $f_{n,j}=e^{-i\chi_{ij}}f_{n,i}$ and therefore define a section of $L^*$.

Here is a direct proof of the winding relation, requiring only Stokes' theorem.  Remove a
small counterclockwise disk $D_a$ around every isolated zero $\mathbf{k}_a$ and call the
remaining punctured Brillouin zone
$M=T^2\setminus\bigcup_aD_a$.  The normalized projected trial state is well defined and
single valued on $M$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 |v_n(\mathbf{k})\rangle
 &=\frac{P_-(\mathbf{k})|\phi_n(\mathbf{k})\rangle}
 {\|P_-(\mathbf{k})|\phi_n(\mathbf{k})\rangle\|}\\
 &=e^{i\vartheta_i(\mathbf{k})}|u_i(\mathbf{k})\rangle,\\
 \vartheta_i&=\arg f_{n,i}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:overlap-projected-global-frame}
\end{equation}
Although $|u_i\rangle$ and $\vartheta_i$ separately depend on the patch gauge, their
product does not: $\vartheta_j=\vartheta_i-\chi_{ij}$ precisely cancels
$|u_j\rangle=e^{i\chi_{ij}}|u_i\rangle$.  Define
$A_i=-i\langle u_i|d u_i\rangle$ and
$A_v=-i\langle v_n|d v_n\rangle$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:overlap-projected-global-frame}
immediately gives
\begin{equation}
 A_v=A_i+d\vartheta_i,
 \qquad dA_v=dA_i\equiv F.
 \label{eq:overlap-connection-shift}
\end{equation}
Because $A_v$ is a single connection one-form throughout $M$, Stokes' theorem gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  2\pi C
  &=\lim_{D_a\to0}\int_M F
    =\lim_{D_a\to0}\oint_{\partial M}A_v\\
  &=-\sum_a\lim_{D_a\to0}\oint_{\gamma_a}
    \bigl(A_i+d\vartheta_i\bigr)
    =-2\pi\sum_a\nu_a.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:overlap-stokes-proof}
\end{equation}
The minus sign is purely an orientation sign: each hole is traversed clockwise as part of
$\partial M$, whereas $\gamma_a=\partial D_a$ is counterclockwise in the definition of
winding.  The integral of the smooth local $A_i$ vanishes with the disk radius, leaving
$\oint_{\gamma_a}d\vartheta_i=2\pi\nu_a$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \sum_a\nu_a
 =\sum_a\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\gamma_a}d\arg f_{n,i}
 =-C.}
 \label{eq:overlap-index-theorem}
\end{equation}
This is the zero-index statement specialized and proved for the present overlap problem.
Consequently, every smooth global trial field has total overlap vorticity $-C$ against a
Chern band, even when that trial field spans a trivial bundle.  In particular,
$n_{\rm shell}=0$ has compact-spinor Chern number zero but its overlap still has total
winding $-1$ when the target band has $C=+1$.  In a trivial target band, isolated
zero--antizero pairs may occur, but their total index must be zero.

The zero search is gauge independent because it begins from
\begin{equation}
 |f_n(\mathbf{k})|^2=
 \langle\phi_n(\mathbf{k})|P_-(\mathbf{k})|\phi_n(\mathbf{k})\rangle.
 \label{eq:overlap-gauge-invariant-magnitude}
\end{equation}
The implementation evaluates Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-gauge-invariant-magnitude} on a
periodic $181\times181$ grid, selects all discrete local minima, refines the lowest 32 by
periodic two-dimensional Nelder--Mead minimization, and merges points separated by less
than $10^{-5}$ in wrapped momentum distance.  A residual below $10^{-6}$ is declared a
zero.  For each such point, choose the reference spinor $|r_a\rangle$ maximizing
$\|P_-(\mathbf{k}_a)|r_a\rangle\|$ from a fixed four-spinor set, and hold it fixed around
the loop.  This defines
\begin{equation}
 |u_a^{\rm loc}(\mathbf{k})\rangle=
 \frac{P_-(\mathbf{k})|r_a\rangle}
 {\|P_-(\mathbf{k})|r_a\rangle\|}.
 \label{eq:overlap-local-frame}
\end{equation}
On the radius-$0.02$ circle
$\mathbf{k}(\theta)=\mathbf{k}_a+0.02(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, 721 equally spaced angles
are used to compute
\begin{equation}
 \nu_a=\operatorname{round}\!\left\{
 \frac{1}{2\pi}\left[
 \operatorname{unwrap}\arg
 \langle u_a^{\rm loc}(\mathbf{k}(\theta))|\phi_n(\mathbf{k}(\theta))\rangle
 \right]_{\theta=0}^{2\pi}\right\}.
 \label{eq:overlap-numerical-winding}
\end{equation}
The same $181\times181$ mesh independently computes the target-band FHS number using
normalized links
$U_\mu(\mathbf{k})=\langle u(\mathbf{k})|u(\mathbf{k}+\hat\mu)\rangle/
|\langle u(\mathbf{k})|u(\mathbf{k}+\hat\mu)\rangle|$ and
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm FHS}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}
 \operatorname{Arg}\!\left[
 U_x(\mathbf{k})U_y(\mathbf{k}+\hat x)
 U_x(\mathbf{k}+\hat y)^*U_y(\mathbf{k})^*\right].
 \label{eq:overlap-fhs-check}
\end{equation}
This independent surface calculation gives $C=+1$ at $\alpha=1,1.5$ and $C=0$ at
$\alpha=3,30$.  The corresponding overlap windings and positive trivial-phase gaps are
reported in Eq.~\eqref{eq:b1-overlap-winding-sweep}.  The critical value $\alpha=2$ is
excluded because the Bloch gap closes and the isolated-band Chern number is undefined.

\section{Frozen-record \texorpdfstring{$U(1)$}{U(1)} twist family and wall spectral flow}
\label{app:frozen-twist}

This appendix defines the branchwise twist used in Campaign~H3.  It is an auxiliary
spectral-flow construction for a single realized Born record.  It is not a physical
monitored-time pump, and its integer is not called a quantized transport coefficient.

\subsection{Boundary holonomy and twisted controller projectors}

Let $y\in\mathbb Z_{N_y}$ denote the periodic direction and let $\phi$ be the flux through
the cylinder.  A one-particle $U(1)$ connection $A^{(\phi)}_{ij}$ is chosen so that the
oriented product of Peierls phases around one complete $y$ cycle is
\begin{equation}
 \prod_{(ij)\in S_y^1}e^{\ii A^{(\phi)}_{ij}}=e^{\ii\phi}.
 \label{eq:twist-holonomy}
\end{equation}
In a seam gauge all links are untwisted except those crossing a declared bond
$y=N_y-1\to0$, which carry $e^{+\ii\phi}$ in the positive orientation and
$e^{-\ii\phi}$ in the reverse orientation.  A uniform gauge distributes the same
holonomy as a phase $e^{\ii\phi/N_y}$ per positive-$y$ step.  The two descriptions are
related by a one-particle gauge transformation $U_\phi$ and must give identical spectra.

For a normalized controller orbital
\begin{equation}
 |\chi_j(0)\rangle=\sum_i w_{ji}|i\rangle,
 \qquad P_j(0)=|\chi_j(0)\rangle\langle\chi_j(0)|,
\end{equation}
parallel transport each component from the declared controller center to site $i$ using
$A^{(\phi)}$.  This defines
\begin{equation}
 |\chi_j(\phi)\rangle
 =\sum_i e^{\ii\vartheta_{ji}(\phi)}w_{ji}|i\rangle,
 \qquad
 P_j(\phi)=|\chi_j(\phi)\rangle\langle\chi_j(\phi)|.
 \label{eq:twisted-controller-projector}
\end{equation}
The phase difference $\vartheta_{ji}-\vartheta_{jk}$ equals the line integral of the
connection between the two components.  This prescription matters for a controller whose
finite support straddles the periodic seam: multiplying the whole orbital by one common
phase would cancel from $P_j$ and would not insert a twist.  Construct the full twisted
controller frame first and only then apply the support mask.  Twisting after truncation can
omit seam-crossing components and defines a different protocol.

At $\phi=2\pi$ the seam-gauge projectors close directly.  In a uniform gauge they obey
\begin{equation}
 P_j(2\pi)=U_{2\pi}P_j(0)U_{2\pi}^\dagger,
 \label{eq:large-gauge-controller-closure}
\end{equation}
where $U_{2\pi}$ is the large gauge transformation.  Every covariance and overlap closure
test must therefore be performed in one common gauge, applying $U_{2\pi}$ where required.
Moving the seam is a gauge-relocation control rather than a new physical flux family.

In a translation-invariant equilibrium ribbon, the same holonomy shifts the allowed
momenta as $k_y\mapsto k_y+\phi/N_y$.  Every bulk band is periodic over the Brillouin zone,
so after $2\pi$ flux the bulk spectrum returns to itself as a set; this fact alone does not
mean that each eigenstate returns to the same label.  A chiral interface branch connects
the valence and conduction continua.  Under one flux quantum its levels undergo a cyclic
permutation across the reference energy: the branch need not close onto itself before its
label is exchanged with the neighboring level.  This spectral flow, rather than a failure
of bulk cosine periodicity, is the equilibrium analogue of the wall-resolved entanglement
flow measured here.  The two oppositely oriented interfaces carry opposite permutations,
so their net flow on the periodic sample vanishes.

\subsection{One record, one continuous conditional branch}

At $\phi=0$, independently prepare and evolve trajectory $s$ and save its complete
record
\begin{equation}
 \xi_s=\bigl[(j_1,m_1),(j_2,m_2),\ldots,(j_M,m_M)\bigr],
 \label{eq:frozen-record-word}
\end{equation}
including the external controller/channel word $j_e$, every Born outcome $m_e$, the
pre-run RNG state (or equivalent counter-based initial-state seed map), and the replay
hash.  Feedback targets and correction
rules remain those of the physical protocol.  For the twisted projectors define the
unnormalized branch operator and branch weight
\begin{align}
 K_{\xi_s}(\phi)
 &=K_{m_M|\xi_{s,<M}}(\phi)\cdots K_{m_1}(\phi),\label{eq:frozen-kraus-family}\\
 p_{\xi_s}(\phi)
 &=\qTr\!\left[K_{\xi_s}(\phi)\rho_0K_{\xi_s}^\dagger(\phi)\right].
 \label{eq:frozen-branch-weight}
\end{align}
Whenever $p_{\xi_s}(\phi)>0$, normalize this same branch and obtain
$C_{\xi_s}(\phi)$.  The physical sampling occurs only once, at $\phi=0$; all other twist
points are deterministic replays of that record.  The resulting family asks how one
conditional branch changes under boundary holonomy.  It does not assert that
$p_{\xi_s}(\phi)$ is uniform or that the frozen record is typical of the Born ensemble at
nonzero $\phi$.

Drawing a new outcome record at each $\phi$ would instead produce unrelated random
covariances $C_{\xi(\phi)}(\phi)$.  Sorting their spectra can manufacture apparent jumps or
crossings, but there is no continuous eigenbundle and hence no branchwise spectral-flow
integer.  Likewise, the external random-serial word is frozen along with the outcomes; a
new word at every $\phi$ changes the microscopic conditional map.  If
$p_{\xi_s}(\phi)=0$ anywhere on the circle, the normalized branch is undefined there and
record $s$ does not define the required flux family.  Such a failure is saved and counted;
the record may not be replaced after its twist behavior is known.

\subsection{Entanglement flow, wall resolution, and crossing count}

For the preregistered wall subsystem $A_w$, diagonalize the restricted covariance at every
twist point,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 C_{\xi_s,A_w}(\phi)u_{sa}(\phi)
 &=\nu_{sa}(\phi)u_{sa}(\phi),\\
 \varepsilon_{sa}(\phi)
 &=\log\frac{1-\nu_{sa}(\phi)}{\nu_{sa}(\phi)}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:twist-entanglement-spectrum}
\end{equation}
The reference level $\varepsilon=0$ is equivalent to $\nu=1/2$.  Between adjacent twist
points, form the overlap matrix
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal O_{ab}^{(m)}
 =\langle u_{sa}(\phi_m)|u_{sb}(\phi_{m+1})\rangle.
 \label{eq:twist-overlap-matrix}
\end{equation}
Track isolated levels by maximum absolute overlap.  For a near-degenerate cluster, track
the entire subspace by its singular values and polar transport before assigning individual
branches; an arbitrary basis rotation inside the cluster is not a physical crossing.
Save every overlap matrix, assignment, minimum tracking singular value, and ambiguity
flag.

Assign a tracked branch to wall $w$ using its preregistered wall-window weight or a
projected transverse-position operator.  A crossing between $\phi_m$ and
$\phi_{m+1}$ contributes the sign of the tracked spectral slope.  The wall-resolved flow
is
\begin{equation}
 \nu_{{\rm flow},w}(\xi_s)
 =\sum_{a,c\in w}
 \Sgn\!\left[\varepsilon_{sa}(\phi_{m_c+1})
             -\varepsilon_{sa}(\phi_{m_c})\right],
 \label{eq:wall-twist-flow-count}
\end{equation}
where $c$ runs over unambiguous zero crossings of tracked branch $a$ assigned to wall
$w$.  The global sign convention is fixed once using the B0 orientation labels.  Opposite
walls of the periodic domain-wall geometry should carry opposite flow, so the target is
$(-1,+1)$ up to the declared orientation convention and the net flow is zero.

Repeat the crossing count after changing the twist grid, moving the seam, varying the
tracking tolerance, and moving the reference level within the entanglement gap.  A valid
integer cannot depend on any of these choices.  Verify
\begin{equation}
 C_{\xi_s}(2\pi)
 \simeq U_{2\pi}C_{\xi_s}(0)U_{2\pi}^\dagger
 \label{eq:frozen-record-closure}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding subspace closure.  Failure of closure, an unresolved overlap
assignment, or a zero branch weight is not silently repaired by smoothing the spectrum.

Production uses $S=1$ preregistered Born-sampled record per protocol at $\phi=0$.  Each
record and its complete twist circle form one branch-level diagnostic unit.  Report its
signed wall flows, branch-weight and closure diagnostics, and ambiguity flags directly;
do not report a success fraction or infer typicality from $S=1$.  The 65 twist points, the
two walls, and multiple reference levels are correlated deterministic descendants and do
not increase $S$.

\section{Quenched finite-time tangent spectra and localization modes}
\label{app:quenched-tangent}

This appendix fixes the tangent-product convention used in Campaigns~T1 and P2.  It also
separates three objects that are easily conflated: the quenched Lyapunov spectrum, the
eigenmodes of a trajectory-averaged finite-time log-strain operator, and an annealed
second-moment spectrum.

\subsection{Cycle Jacobian, product, and time normalization}

Along realized trajectory $s$, let $\Phi_{s,t}$ denote one complete normalized circuit
cycle and let
\begin{equation}
 \delta C_{t+1}=J_{s,t}[\delta C_t],
 \qquad J_{s,t}=D\Phi_{s,t}\big|_{C_{s,t}},
 \label{eq:cycle-covariance-jacobian}
\end{equation}
after restriction to the declared physical tangent space.  The $T$-cycle product is
\begin{equation}
 M_s(T)=J_{s,T-1}\cdots J_{s,1}J_{s,0}.
 \label{eq:tangent-window-product}
\end{equation}
Here $T$ is the number of complete circuit cycles.  The finite-time singular exponents are
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{sa}(T)=\frac1T\log\sigma_a[M_s(T)].
 \label{eq:finite-time-singular-exponents}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, on the nonsingular physical subspace define the right log-strain operator
\begin{equation}
 L_{s,T}^{\rm R}
 =\frac1{2T}\log\!\left[M_s^\dagger(T)M_s(T)\right].
 \label{eq:right-log-strain-operator}
\end{equation}
Its eigenvalues are $\lambda_{sa}(T)$ and its eigenvectors are the right finite-time
singular vectors, namely input perturbations at the left endpoint of the window.  The
analogous left operator
\begin{equation}
 L_{s,T}^{\rm L}
 =\frac1{2T}\log\!\left[M_s(T)M_s^\dagger(T)\right]
 \label{eq:left-log-strain-operator}
\end{equation}
has the same finite eigenvalues and output singular vectors.  The normalization is
multiplication by $1/(2T)$, not division by $1/(2T)$.  Exact null directions have
$\log0=-\infty$ and are recorded in a null mask rather than replaced by an arbitrary
finite floor.

The reported unit is inverse circuit cycles.  The scaled variable $T/N_y$ may be used to
display convergence, and the size dependence may subsequently be fitted to
$\Delta(N_y)\sim N_y^{-z}$, but $N_y$ is not inserted into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-time-singular-exponents}.  Normalizing by elementary update count or
hardware layer depth defines a different clock and must be reported as a separate
conversion.

\subsection{Stabilized QR accumulation and finite-time singular vectors}

The dense product in Eq.~\eqref{eq:tangent-window-product} is not formed at production
sizes.  Strong contraction, exact projector nulls, and expanding directions make it
ill-conditioned.  Starting from an orthonormal rank-$r$ physical frame $Q_{s,0}$, iterate
\begin{equation}
 Y_{s,t}=J_{s,t}Q_{s,t},
 \qquad
 Y_{s,t}=Q_{s,t+1}R_{s,t},
 \label{eq:tangent-qr-step}
\end{equation}
with a fixed diagonal-sign convention for $R_{s,t}$.  After the declared alignment
interval, the QR estimator is
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{sa}(T)
 =\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log |(R_{s,t})_{aa}|.
 \label{eq:qr-lyapunov-estimator}
\end{equation}
The asymptotic limit gives the Oseledec exponents represented by the retained frame.  QR
vectors themselves are backward Lyapunov vectors and need not equal covariant Lyapunov
vectors or finite-window singular vectors, particularly inside a near-degenerate band.

To reconstruct finite-window singular modes, save block-$R$ factors together with their
diagonal log scales and the input/output frames.  A stabilized SVD of the coefficient-space
product
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_s(T)=R_{s,T-1}\cdots R_{s,0}
 =\widetilde U_s\Sigma_s\widetilde V_s^\dagger
 \label{eq:stabilized-r-product}
\end{equation}
gives retained input and output singular vectors
\begin{equation}
 v_{sa}^{\rm in}=Q_{s,0}\widetilde V_{sa},
 \qquad
 v_{sa}^{\rm out}=Q_{s,T}\widetilde U_{sa}.
 \label{eq:retained-singular-vectors}
\end{equation}
The rank-$r$ result is exact only within the propagated subspace.  Rank doubling and
independent initial frames test whether the near-zero band has been captured.

\subsection{Quenched spectrum, mean log strain, and annealed spectrum}

The primary quenched spectrum takes the logarithm trajectory by trajectory and then
averages continuously tracked indices,
\begin{equation}
 \overline\lambda_a^{\rm q}(T)
 =\frac1S\sum_{s=1}^S\lambda_{sa}(T)
 =\frac1{ST}\sum_{s=1}^S\log\sigma_a[M_s(T)].
 \label{eq:quenched-lyapunov-spectrum}
\end{equation}
The gap is formed only after this indexwise average.  Means and medians of the
per-trajectory minimum gap are retained as distinct sensitivity statistics.  Averaging
$M_s(T)$ before taking singular values is neither this quenched estimator nor the tangent
spectrum of a typical realized record.

If all right log-strain operators have been embedded or parallel transported into one
declared common physical tangent space, one may also define
\begin{equation}
 \overline L_T^{\rm R}
 =\frac1S\sum_{s=1}^S L_{s,T}^{\rm R}.
 \label{eq:mean-quenched-log-strain}
\end{equation}
This is a useful mean finite-time log-strain operator.  Its eigenvectors answer which
common directions are slow or expanding under the averaged logarithmic strain.  In
general, however,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{eig}\!\left(\overline L_T^{\rm R}\right)
 \ne \frac1S\sum_s\operatorname{eig}\!\left(L_{s,T}^{\rm R}\right),
 \label{eq:noncommuting-log-strain}
\end{equation}
because the sample log-strain operators do not commute.  Consequently there is no unique
single vector whose eigenvalue is the indexwise quenched average in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:quenched-lyapunov-spectrum}.  The eigenmodes of
$\overline L_T^{\rm R}$ may be plotted, but they are labelled ``mean quenched log-strain
modes,'' not ``the quenched Lyapunov modes.''  Since physical tangent spaces at different
covariances need not coincide, the embedding or transport convention and its residual are
part of this definition.

For comparison, the annealed second-moment operator is
\begin{equation}
 L_T^{\rm ann}
 =\frac1{2T}\log\!\left[\frac1S\sum_sM_s^\dagger(T)M_s(T)\right].
 \label{eq:annealed-second-moment-log-strain}
\end{equation}
It places enhanced weight on rare records with large stretch and is not substituted for
the quenched spectrum.  Equations~\eqref{eq:quenched-lyapunov-spectrum},
\eqref{eq:mean-quenched-log-strain}, and
\eqref{eq:annealed-second-moment-log-strain} are saved and named separately whenever more
than one is reported.

\subsection{Localization of a nonunique slow band}

For an isolated nondegenerate exponent one may plot its input or output singular-vector
density, provided the endpoint convention is stated.  Near degeneracy, individual vectors
rotate under numerical noise, QR gauge, and sample-to-sample wall exchange.  The stable
trajectory-level object is the projector onto the declared slow band $\mathcal L_s$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{\mathcal L,s}
 &=\sum_{a\in\mathcal L_s}|v_{sa}\rangle\langle v_{sa}|,\\
 \rho_s(x,y)
 &=\frac1{r_s}\sum_{a\in\mathcal L_s,\mu}|v_{sa,\mu}(x,y)|^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:slow-subspace-projector-density}
\end{equation}
Average $P_{\mathcal L,s}$ only after expressing it in the same ambient real-space basis;
otherwise average the gauge-invariant density $\rho_s$ and wall-window weights.  Resolve
the two walls by diagonalizing the projected transverse-position operator inside
$\mathcal L_s$ or by fixed left/right windows.  Do not average signed eigenvectors, select
the most localized QR column, or count a basis rotation inside a degenerate subspace as
motion of a physical mode.

When the tangent action factorizes as a one-leg congruence,
\begin{equation}
 \delta C\longmapsto K_s(T)\,\delta C\,K_s^\dagger(T),
 \label{eq:one-leg-tangent-congruence}
\end{equation}
and $K_s(T)$ has one-leg exponents $\ell_{sa}$, the full covariance-tangent exponents are
pair sums
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{s,ab}^{(C)}=\ell_{sa}+\ell_{sb},
 \label{eq:covariance-pair-sum-exponents}
\end{equation}
subject to the physical Hermiticity/projector tangent constraints.  A purification rate
must therefore be compared with the appropriate covariance pair-sum sector, not
automatically with the smallest one-leg gap.

For every trajectory save the QR log increments, block-$R$ factors, frame ranks, null and
gauge masks, input/output endpoint convention, slow-band membership, projector density,
wall weights, orthogonality residuals, and replay hash.  The independent sample count is
the number of Born trajectories $S$; tangent-frame repeats, QR checkpoints, and multiple
vectors within one slow band are correlated numerical diagnostics and do not increase
$S$.

\section{Charge full counting statistics and the level-one current algebra}
\label{app:fcs}

For a Gaussian trajectory with subsystem correlation matrix $C_{\xi,A}$, the quantum FCS
of $Q_A$ is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mc Z_{\xi,A}(\lambda)
 &=\det\!\left[\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A}+e^{\ii\lambda}C_{\xi,A}\right],\\
 \mc K_{\xi,A}(\lambda)&=\log\mc Z_{\xi,A}(\lambda).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
With $\kappa_{n,\xi}^{\rm q}=(-\ii\partial_\lambda)^n\mc K|_{\lambda=0}$,
\begin{align}
 \kappa_{1,\xi}^{\rm q}&=\qTr C_{\xi,A},\\
 \kappa_{2,\xi}^{\rm q}&=\qTr[C_{\xi,A}(\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A})],\\
 \kappa_{3,\xi}^{\rm q}&=\qTr[C_{\xi,A}(\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A})(\mathbf{1}_A-2C_{\xi,A})],\\
 \kappa_{4,\xi}^{\rm q}&=\qTr[C_{\xi,A}(\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A})
 (\mathbf{1}_A-6C_{\xi,A}+6C_{\xi,A}^2)].
\end{align}
The state-CFT observable is $\E[\kappa_{n,\xi}^{\rm q}]$.  It is distinct from the FCS of
the Born mixture,
\begin{equation}
 \mc Z_A^{\rm mix}(\lambda)=\E_\xi[\mc Z_{\xi,A}(\lambda)],
\end{equation}
whose logarithm includes classical trajectory fluctuations.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \Var_{\rm mix}(Q_A)=
 \E_\xi[\kappa_{2,\xi}^{\rm q}]
 +\Var_\xi(\kappa_{1,\xi}^{\rm q}).
 \label{eq:total-variance}
\end{equation}
Both terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:total-variance} are saved and plotted separately.

For a chiral $U(1)$ current, the normalization convention is fixed by
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 J(z)J(0)\sim\dfrac{k}{z^2},\\
 [J_m,J_n]=k\,m\,\delta_{m+n,0}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
A free chiral complex fermion has $k=1$.  A single wall contributes
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 X_L(\ell)&=\log\!\left[\frac{N_y}{\pi a}
 \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{N_y}\right)\right],\\
 \kappa_2^{(1w)}(\ell)&=\frac{k}{2\pi^2}X_L(\ell)+b+\cdots,
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
whereas two well-separated, oppositely oriented walls contribute
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_2^{(2w)}(\ell)=\frac{k}{\pi^2}X_L(\ell)+b+\cdots.
\end{equation}
The common correlation-spectrum derivation of the matching entropy coefficients and the
$\pi^2/3$ relation is given in Appendix~\ref{app:entropy-charge-spectrum}.
The charged primaries obey $h_q=q^2/(2k)$, giving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:G5-conformal-tower} at $k=1$ for the fixed-word operator spectrum.
The analysis must declare whether a subsystem contains one or two walls before converting a
fit coefficient to $k$ or $c_{\rm edge}$.  The coefficient $k$ identifies the current algebra;
by itself it does not determine chirality, which is tested only by Campaigns H1--H3.

Signed feedback-transfer FCS is a different classical observable.  If $Y_e\in\{-1,0,1\}$
is the system-layer charge transfer at event $e$, save
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{\rm rec}(R,T)&=\sum_{e\in R\times T}Y_e,\\
 \theta_R(s,T)&=\frac1T\log\E_\xi[e^{-sQ_{\rm rec}}].
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The empirical finite-time $\theta_R$ is not called a tilted-channel eigenvalue.  Use
log-sum-exp stabilization, trajectory bootstrap intervals, and a reweighting effective
sample-size flag.  Record FCS and quantum subsystem FCS may be compared only after their
distinct definitions are displayed.

\section{Common correlation-spectrum origin of entropy and charge fluctuations}
\label{app:entropy-charge-spectrum}

This appendix fixes the exact Gaussian relation between interval entanglement and quantum
charge fluctuations and then states the stronger CFT test used in Campaign~S1.  It applies
to a number-conserving Gaussian reduced state whether or not the reduced density matrix is
pure.  It does not identify the within-trajectory quantum variance with classical
trajectory-to-trajectory charge wandering.

\subsection{Spectral calculus of the restricted covariance}

Let
\begin{equation}
 C_{\xi,A}u_{\xi a}=\nu_{\xi a}u_{\xi a},
 \qquad 0\leq \nu_{\xi a}\leq1,
\end{equation}
and define the positive charge-susceptibility operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V_{\xi,A}
 =C_{\xi,A}(\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A}),
 \qquad
 q_{\xi a}=\nu_{\xi a}(1-\nu_{\xi a})\in[0,1/4].
 \label{eq:charge-susceptibility-operator}
\end{equation}
The interval quantum variance and von Neumann entropy are
\begin{align}
 F_{\xi,A}^{\rm q}
 &=\qTr\mathcal V_{\xi,A}
   =\sum_aq_{\xi a},\label{eq:variance-from-V}\\
 S_{\xi,A}
 &=-\qTr\!\left[
 C_{\xi,A}\log C_{\xi,A}
 +(\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A})\log(\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A})\right]\nonumber\\
 &=\sum_a h(\nu_{\xi a})
   =\qTr g(\mathcal V_{\xi,A}),\label{eq:entropy-from-V}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 h(\nu)&=-\nu\log\nu-(1-\nu)\log(1-\nu),\\
 g(q)&=h\!\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4q}}{2}\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:entropy-kernel-q}
\end{equation}
The branch of the square root is immaterial because $h(\nu)=h(1-\nu)$.  More generally,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 S_{\xi,A}^{(n)}&=\qTr g_n(\mathcal V_{\xi,A}),\\
 g_n(q)&=\frac{1}{1-n}
 \log\!\left[\nu(q)^n+(1-\nu(q))^n\right],
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:renyi-from-V}
\end{equation}
with $\nu(q)=(1+\sqrt{1-4q})/2$.  Thus entropy and variance are different spectral
functionals of the same operator.  The scalar $F_{\xi,A}^{\rm q}$ alone does not determine
$S_{\xi,A}$: distinct multisets $\{q_{\xi a}\}$ can have the same first moment.  The full
spectrum of $\mathcal V_{\xi,A}$ does determine every Gaussian R\'enyi entropy.

The corresponding single-particle modular Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
 h_{\xi,A}^{\rm mod}
 =\log\!\left[(\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A})C_{\xi,A}^{-1}\right],
 \qquad
 \nu_{\xi a}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\epsilon_{\xi a}}},
 \label{eq:modular-correlation-map}
\end{equation}
with zero and unit occupations understood by continuity.  In modular-energy variables,
\begin{align}
 q(\epsilon)&=\frac{1}{4\cosh^2(\epsilon/2)},\\
 s(\epsilon)&=
 \log\!\left[2\cosh\!\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right]
 -\frac{\epsilon}{2}\tanh\!\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right).
 \label{eq:modular-spectral-kernels}
\end{align}
If $\rho_{\xi,A}(\epsilon)=\sum_a\delta(\epsilon-\epsilon_{\xi a})$, then
\begin{equation}
 F_{\xi,A}^{\rm q}=\int d\epsilon\,\rho_{\xi,A}(\epsilon)q(\epsilon),
 \qquad
 S_{\xi,A}=\int d\epsilon\,\rho_{\xi,A}(\epsilon)s(\epsilon).
 \label{eq:modular-density-integrals}
\end{equation}
Both kernels select entanglement levels with $|\epsilon|=O(1)$, and
\begin{equation}
 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\epsilon\,q(\epsilon)=1,
 \qquad
 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\epsilon\,s(\epsilon)=\frac{\pi^2}{3}.
 \label{eq:kernel-integrals}
\end{equation}
Consequently a common slowly varying, logarithmically growing low-modular-energy density
produces $S=(\pi^2/3)F^{\rm q}+O(1)$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:kernel-integrals} explains the
spectral origin of the coefficient but is not an exact finite-size equality for an
arbitrary covariance spectrum.

\subsection{CFT coefficient and level-one test}

For the interval convention of Appendix~\ref{app:fcs}, one chiral wall contributes
\begin{align}
 S_n^{(1w)}(\ell)
 &=\frac{c}{12}\left(1+\frac1n\right)X_L(\ell)+b_{S_n}+\cdots,\\
 F_{\rm q}^{(1w)}(\ell)
 &=\frac{k}{2\pi^2}X_L(\ell)+b_F+\cdots,
 \label{eq:one-wall-entropy-variance}
\end{align}
whereas two well-separated oppositely oriented walls contribute twice these logarithmic
coefficients,
\begin{align}
 S_n^{(2w)}(\ell)
 &=\frac{c}{6}\left(1+\frac1n\right)X_L(\ell)+b_{S_n}'+\cdots,\\
 F_{\rm q}^{(2w)}(\ell)
 &=\frac{k}{\pi^2}X_L(\ell)+b_F'+\cdots.
 \label{eq:two-wall-entropy-variance}
\end{align}
The geometry factor cancels in the ratio.  Denoting the corresponding log-chord slopes by
$a_{S_n}$ and $a_F$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{a_{S_n}}{a_F}
 =\frac{\pi^2c}{6k}\left(1+\frac1n\right),
 \qquad
 \frac{a_S}{a_F}=\frac{\pi^2c}{3k}.
 \label{eq:general-entropy-variance-ratio}
\end{equation}
For a charge-one chiral complex fermion, $c=k=1$, and therefore
\begin{equation}
 a_S-\frac{\pi^2}{3}a_F=0,\qquad
 a_{S_n}-\frac{\pi^2}{6}\left(1+\frac1n\right)a_F=0.
 \label{eq:u1-entropy-variance-null}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 S(\ell)-\frac{\pi^2}{3}F^{\rm q}(\ell)
 =\text{constant}+\text{finite-size corrections}
 \label{eq:entropy-variance-constant-residual}
\end{equation}
has no logarithmic term.  This is a leading-scaling statement, not a claim that the two
spectral kernels agree mode by mode.

Under a unitary chiral-CFT interpretation and a fixed microscopic normalization of charge,
the $U(1)$ current generates a $c=1$ Sugawara sector.  The combination $c\simeq1$,
$k\simeq1$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:u1-entropy-variance-null}, and an independent H1--H3 chirality
test supports a minimal $U(1)_1$ charged wall without an additional neutral gapless
sector.  Entropy and variance alone do not determine the sign of the chiral central charge
or the direction of propagation.

\subsection{Cross-boundary coherence and intrinsic mixedness}

For a pure Slater trajectory the full correlation matrix is a projector.  With the spatial
block decomposition
\begin{equation}
 C_\xi=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 C_{\xi,A}&C_{\xi,AB}\\
 C_{\xi,BA}&C_{\xi,B}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad C_\xi^2=C_\xi,
\end{equation}
the $AA$ block of the projector identity gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V_{\xi,A}
 =C_{\xi,A}(\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A})
 =C_{\xi,AB}C_{\xi,BA}.
 \label{eq:pure-cross-boundary-identity}
\end{equation}
Hence the $q_{\xi a}$ are the squared singular values of the cross-boundary correlation
block,
\begin{equation}
 F_{\xi,A}^{\rm q}=\|C_{\xi,AB}\|_F^2,
 \qquad
 S_{\xi,A}=\qTr g(C_{\xi,AB}C_{\xi,BA}).
 \label{eq:cross-boundary-spectral-functionals}
\end{equation}
The variance is the total squared cross-boundary coherence, whereas the entropy retains
the complete singular-value distribution.

For a globally mixed Gaussian state, $0\leq C_\xi\leq\mathbf{1}$ but $C_\xi^2\ne C_\xi$, and the
exact decomposition becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V_{\xi,A}
 =C_{\xi,AB}C_{\xi,BA}+(C_\xi-C_\xi^2)_{AA}.
 \label{eq:mixed-cross-boundary-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Both terms are positive.  The first measures cross-boundary coherence and the second is
intrinsic mixedness already present in the global state.  Their traces add in the charge
variance, but the entropy is not generally the sum of separate functions of the two terms
because they need not commute.  Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed-cross-boundary-decomposition} is
the appropriate diagnostic during max-mix purification; Eq.~\eqref{eq:pure-cross-boundary-identity}
is used only after the pure-trajectory gate passes.

The shared natural orbitals also define matched real-space contours,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 f_{\xi,A}(i)&=\sum_a|u_{\xi a}(i)|^2q_{\xi a},\\
 s_{\xi,A}(i)&=\sum_a|u_{\xi a}(i)|^2h(\nu_{\xi a}),
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:matched-entropy-variance-contours}
\end{equation}
with the exact sum rules
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i\in A}f_{\xi,A}(i)=F_{\xi,A}^{\rm q},
 \qquad
 \sum_{i\in A}s_{\xi,A}(i)=S_{\xi,A}.
 \label{eq:matched-contour-sum-rules}
\end{equation}
Comparing these unnormalized contours tests whether the same wall-localized natural
orbitals carry both logarithms.  Each contour is averaged over trajectories only after its
per-trajectory construction.

\subsection{Full charge statistics and ensemble decomposition}

Writing $z=e^{\ii\lambda}$, the interval FCS polynomial factorizes as
\begin{equation}
 \chi_{\xi,A}(z)
 =\det[\mathbf{1}_A-C_{\xi,A}+zC_{\xi,A}]
 =\prod_a(1-\nu_{\xi a}+z\nu_{\xi a}).
 \label{eq:fcs-polynomial-factorization}
\end{equation}
Its zeros obey
\begin{equation}
 z_{\xi a}=-\frac{1-\nu_{\xi a}}{\nu_{\xi a}}
 =-e^{\epsilon_{\xi a}}.
 \label{eq:fcs-zero-entanglement-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Thus the exact full interval-charge distribution, whose probabilities are the coefficients
of $\chi_{\xi,A}(z)$, determines the single-particle entanglement spectrum up to
permutation and hence every Gaussian entropy.  Numerical inversion of high-degree noisy
counting polynomials is ill conditioned and is not a production gate; direct covariance
eigenvalues remain the primary simulated estimator.

Finally, if one first samples a trajectory and then measures its interval charge, the law
of total variance is
\begin{equation}
 \Var_{\rm total}(N_A)
 =\E_\xi\!\left[\qTr\mathcal V_{\xi,A}\right]
 +\Var_\xi\!\left[\qTr C_{\xi,A}\right].
 \label{eq:appendix-total-variance-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Only the first term is the quantum interval variance governed by the spectrum in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:charge-susceptibility-operator} and used to infer $k$.  Boundary localization
of the second term is meaningful dynamical information about the trajectory ensemble, but
it is not substituted for the current-algebra coefficient.

\subsection{Production estimator and acceptance rule}

Campaign~S1 computes $S_{\xi,A}$, $F_{\xi,A}^{\rm q}$, their contours, and the
$q_{\xi a}$ spectrum from the same restricted covariance for every declared interval.
Origin and stationary-snapshot averages are internal to a trajectory and are performed
identically for both observables.  The common linear fit then produces
$m_{1,\xi}$, $f_\xi$, and the paired difference $\delta_{SF,\xi}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:matched-entropy-variance-slope}.  Whole trajectories are resampled jointly,
preserving the covariance between the two slopes and among all interval lengths.

The primary test is the thermodynamic extrapolation of $\delta_{SF}^{\rm Born}$ and the
equivalent residual curve in Eq.~\eqref{eq:matched-entropy-variance-residual}.  The ratio
$a_S/a_F$ and the R\'enyi relations in Eq.~\eqref{eq:matched-renyi-variance-slope} are
secondary.  Repeat the complete paired analysis after each preregistered fit-window
deletion and on the exact-Hamiltonian, matched trivial, and one-versus-two-wall controls.
A nonzero residual logarithmic coefficient forbids the minimal $U(1)_1$ identification
even if entropy and variance separately admit logarithmic fits.  It does not erase the
separate estimates of $c$ and $k$, and it says nothing by itself about chirality.

\section{Circuit depth, Lu bound, wall hybridization, and fixed duration}
\label{app:depth}

For a product input and a depth-$D$ local circuit, the rank-entropy growth across region $A$
obeys the Lu--Lessa--Kim--Hsieh bound~\cite{LuLessaKimHsieh2022}
\begin{equation}
 S_0(A,T)-S_0(A,0)\lesssim D\,|\partial A|.
 \label{eq:lu-bound}
\end{equation}
For the full-width strip $A=[0,N_x)\times[0,\ell)$,
$|\partial A|\sim2N_x$.  A two-wall CFT contribution therefore requires parametrically
\begin{equation}
 \frac13\log N_y\lesssim \kappa D N_x.
 \label{eq:lu-cft}
\end{equation}
Here $D$ counts layers of mutually nonoverlapping local maps, not circuit sweeps.  The
production random-serial schedule visits overlapping orbitals sequentially, so one cycle
has a size-dependent implementation depth.  For $n_{\rm shell}=1$, an update centered at
$u=(x,y)$ has the periodic $3\times3$ cell support
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S_u=\{u+(\delta_x,\delta_y):\delta_x,\delta_y\in\{-1,0,1\}\}.
\end{equation}
The conflict graph joins $u$ and $v$ when $\mathcal S_u\cap\mathcal S_v\ne\varnothing$,
equivalently when their cyclic separations obey
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 d_{N_x}(x,x')&\le2,& d_{N_y}(y,y')&\le2,\\
 d_L(a,b)&=\min_{m\in\mathbb Z}|a-b+mL|.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
It has maximum degree at most $24$ and hence at most $25$ colors by a greedy bound.  If
$q_x\mid N_x$, $q_y\mid N_y$, and $q_x,q_y\ge3$, the explicit residue coloring
\begin{equation}
 c(x,y)=(x\bmod q_x,y\bmod q_y)
 \label{eq:residue-coloring}
\end{equation}
uses $q_xq_y$ colors and is proper: distinct equal-color centers have separation at least
three in one coordinate.  A commensurate $3\times3$ coloring therefore uses nine colors.
For every size in Eq.~\eqref{eq:primary-sizes}, the conservative $4\times4$ coloring wraps
cleanly and gives $\chi_{\rm conf}\le16$.

Let $d_{\rm inst}=O(1)$ denote the physical layer depth of one
occupation-measurement--feedforward--fresh-ancilla-fSWAP instrument block.  Keeping the four
controller channels as separate blocks gives
\begin{equation}
 d_{\rm cyc}\le4\chi_{\rm conf}d_{\rm inst}\le64d_{\rm inst},
 \qquad D=d_{\rm cyc}T_{\rm cyc}.
 \label{eq:colored-depth}
\end{equation}
This is an $O(1)$ bound on the physical depth of one colored sweep, not on the total
preparation depth.  The BPJ bulk can converge in $O(1)$ sweeps while the additional
critical wall requires a cycle count that grows with $N_y$; the two statements concern
different target entanglement structures.
Equation~\eqref{eq:lu-cft} gives only
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm cyc}\gtrsim
 \frac{\log N_y}{3\kappa d_{\rm cyc}N_x}.
 \label{eq:lu-cycles}
\end{equation}
The unknown coefficient makes this too weak to choose 10 versus 50 versus $2N_y$ cycles.
The production duration is therefore a declared numerical choice rather than a consequence
of this bound.

This coloring gives an optional quantum-simulation compilation.  Within a color, disjoint
local instruments commute, so simultaneous hardware readout and sequential numerical
evaluation define the same joint Born instrument even though its outcomes need not be
statistically independent.  Reordering different color blocks is not a parallel
reinterpretation of a saved random-serial trajectory because overlapping conditional maps
generally do not commute.  The production campaign therefore makes no
constant-depth-per-sweep claim for its random-serial word; it reports
Eq.~\eqref{eq:colored-depth} only as a realizable resource bound for the corresponding
color-block hardware schedule.

The circuit-specific operator-Schmidt-rank bounds are
\begin{equation}
 r(a_\chi),r(a_\chi^\dagger)\le2,
 \qquad r(n_\chi),r(\mathbf{1}-n_\chi)\le4.
\end{equation}
Along record $\xi$, a sharper bookkeeping bound is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta S_0^\xi
 \le 2\log2\,N_{=}^{\partial}(\xi)
 +\log2\,N_{\ne}^{\partial}(\xi),
\end{equation}
with the two counts distinguished according to the boundary-crossing branch type.  These
bounds assume an initially product state.  A random Slater determinant is already
entangled and cannot be used to validate a product-input depth bound.  The maximally mixed
state is treated separately as a purification problem beginning at $t=0$.

Finite transverse width produces an independent infrared scale.  If the opposing-wall
hybridization behaves as
\begin{equation}
 m(w)\sim m_0e^{-w/\xi_\perp},
\end{equation}
then one requires
\begin{equation}
 m(w)\ll\frac{2\pi v}{N_y},
 \qquad
 w\gtrsim\xi_\perp\log N_y
\end{equation}
up to nonuniversal constants.  B0 determines $\xi_\perp$ and W1 verifies the same condition
in the stochastic circuit.

Every production trajectory begins from its own independently sampled declared initial
state and runs for exactly $2N_y$ complete cycles.  There is no separate physical burn-in,
no shared equilibrated parent state, and no cloning of one evolved covariance into 100
continuations.  Time-resolved bulk markers, entropy coefficients, charge statistics, wall
profiles, purity diagnostics, and successive-covariance changes are retained as transient
checks inside this fixed window.

The three pure-state checkpoints used by S1 are $t=N_y,3N_y/2,2N_y$.  Agreement among them
is a sensitivity diagnostic, not a device for converting cycle snapshots into independent
samples.  If the late checkpoints are incompatible beyond the preregistered tolerance, the
relevant stationarity or temporal gate fails.  Production is not silently extended beyond
$2N_y$; a longer campaign requires a versioned amendment and a new output directory.

Max-mix purification likewise starts at $C=\mathbf{1}/2$ at $t=0$ and retains its complete
$2N_y$ clock.  T1 initializes its physical trajectory and tangent frame at $t=0$, uses the
first $N_y$ cycles for tangent-frame alignment only, resets only the QR log accumulator,
and accumulates exponents over the final $N_y$ cycles.  The physical trajectory is never
reset and the alignment interval is not called burn-in.  This procedure is tailored to the
production serial implementation and must be re-evaluated if the actual overlap-conflict
schedule changes.

\section{Campaign-card checklist}

Before a campaign is marked complete, its final note must answer all of the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Was the canonical GPU entry point recorded?
\item Were all 25 trajectories independently initialized and run for the declared total
      duration without cloning an evolved state?
\item Were max-mix and tangent clocks treated according to Appendix~\ref{app:depth}?
\item Can every plotted scalar be reconstructed from per-trajectory raw data?
\item Is the order of nonlinear evaluation, time/source averaging, and trajectory averaging explicit?
\item Are fit windows, excluded points, and correction models preregistered and saved?
\item Do paired controls share declared random streams without being called the same record?
\item Did every shard pass manifest, seed, replay/restart, and numerical-residual checks?
\item Does the caption state sample count, independent unit, uncertainty, and geometry?
\item If the gate failed, was the prescribed downgrade used without changing the estimator?
\end{enumerate}
