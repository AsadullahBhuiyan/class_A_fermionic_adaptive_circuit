\title{Perfect-correction Markov-channel and continuous Lindblad dynamics at a class-A domain wall}
\author{Internal campaign summary}
\affiliation{Class-A fermionic adaptive-circuit project}
\date{Production-results note; completed August 22, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We report a matched comparison between the deterministic trajectory-averaged
perfect-correction Markov channel and an infinitesimal continuous Lindblad evolution
built from identical overcomplete Wannier modes.  Both domain-wall support truncation
settings and two finite spatial shells are included.  The physical channel retains its
number-measurement dephasing; the continuous evolution is evaluated with and without
the corresponding number-dephasing term.  Because a random ordered sweep need not be
translation invariant along the wall, its momentum-resolved estimator is defined by a
late-time temporal average followed by an exact translation-group twirl.  This twirl is
a post-processing projection, not a symmetry of the ordered channel and not an average
over trajectories.  We also report the ascending global occupation spectrum of the raw,
untwirled late state.  All three
dynamics exhibit a wall-associated natural-occupation crossing while their bulk
occupations remain far from one half.  The crossing disappears across the parent-model
reference near $\alpha_{\rm run,in}=2$, but no relaxation-gap closure is observed.
The continuous density-kick response has opposite signed velocities on the two walls;
the ordered channel instead has poor linear fits and a common drift sign.  Thus the
campaign demonstrates mixed-state occupation spectral flow, not a gapless Lindbladian,
trajectory Lyapunov mode, or schedule-independent chiral transport.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Correction and scope}

The computed objects must be distinguished at the outset.  The first is the ordered
one-cycle correlation-matrix map produced by
\texttt{classA\_U1FGTN.run\_markov\_channel}.  It is the deterministic average of the
projective measurement and perfect-feedback protocol, not a sampled trajectory.  The
second is the continuous equation obtained from infinitesimal local channel increments.
The third is the older auxiliary Gaussian bath calculation.  At ancillary filling
$\bar n_{\mathrm a}=1/2$, its dephasing-free generator is exactly one half of the
perfect-correction dephasing-free continuous generator.  Its stationary correlation
matrix is therefore reusable, whereas its relaxation clock, response fits, and finite
Strang map are not.

No result below is called a trajectory Lyapunov spectrum, a Hamiltonian band, or a
Liouvillian many-body spectrum.  Natural occupations are eigenvalues of a two-point
correlation matrix.  A midpoint crossing can diagnose a state-level purity gap without
implying a vanishing relaxation rate.

\section{Common OW geometry}

We follow the notation of Bhuiyan, Pan, and Jian~\cite{Bhuiyan2026}.  Many-body
operators carry hats, $\mu=1,2$ labels physical orbitals, $\nu=A,B$ labels trial-orbital
families, and $\pm$ labels upper and lower parent bands.  The parent single-particle
matrix in the archived run convention is
\begin{align}
 h_{\alpha_{\rm run}}(\boldsymbol k)
 &=\widehat{\boldsymbol n}_{\alpha_{\rm run}}(\boldsymbol k)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma,
 \\
 \boldsymbol n_{\alpha_{\rm run}}(\boldsymbol k)
 &=\bigl(\sin k_x,\sin k_y,
 \alpha_{\rm run}-\cos k_x-\cos k_y\bigr),
 \label{eq:parent}
\end{align}
with $\alpha_{\rm PRR}=-\alpha_{\rm run}$.  It is used to construct the
\textit{overcomplete Wannier (OW) basis}; a spatial domain-wall Hamiltonian is not
diagonalized.

For center $\boldsymbol r$ the normalized state used by both dynamics is
\begin{equation}
 |\widetilde W_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}\rangle=
 \frac{M_{n_{\rm shell}}(\boldsymbol r'-\boldsymbol r)
 M_{\rm DW}(r'_x;r_x)|W_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}\rangle}
 {\|M_{n_{\rm shell}}M_{\rm DW}|W_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}\rangle\|}.
 \label{eq:ow}
\end{equation}
The shell mask depends on relative periodic displacement.  The optional hard-wall mask
allows support only in the domain containing the center.  All cases use
$N_x=20$, walls $x=5,15$, $\alpha_{\rm run,out}=30$, trial basis $X$, and a maximally
mixed initial state.  These explicit walls override incompatible historical defaults.

Define
\begin{align}
 \hat\chi_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}
 &=\sum_{\boldsymbol r',\mu}
 \widetilde W_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}(\boldsymbol r',\mu)
 \hat c_{\boldsymbol r',\mu},\\
 \hat N_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}
 &=\hat\chi_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}^{\dagger}
 \hat\chi_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm},
\end{align}
and the rank-one single-particle matrix
$P_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}=|\widetilde W_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}\rangle
\langle\widetilde W_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,\pm}|$.  Distinct OW stabilizers need not
commute.

\section{The two requested dynamics}

\subsection{Ordered perfect-correction channel}

The published correlation convention is
\begin{equation}
 G_{ij}=\Tr(\hat\rho\,\hat c_i^\dagger\hat c_j).
\end{equation}
The canonical class returns $Q=2G-\id$; the runner records this bridge and stores
physical $G$.  For a single mode $P$, let $\Diss^\dagger[\hat L]$ denote the
adjoint Lindblad action on $G$.  The dephasing-free loss and gain terms are
\begin{align}
 \Diss^\dagger[\hat\chi](G)&=-\tfrac12\{P,G\},\\
 \Diss^\dagger[\hat\chi^\dagger](G)&=P-\tfrac12\{P,G\}.
 \label{eq:localnodeph}
\end{align}
Thus $\mathcal I+\Diss^\dagger[\hat\chi]$ and
$\mathcal I+\Diss^\dagger[\hat\chi^\dagger]$ give the expressions sometimes called a
``no-dephasing unit-step channel.''  They are formal forward-Euler increments, not
physical local maps for overlapping OW modes at unit step.

Number measurement contributes
$\Diss^\dagger[\hat N](G)=PGP-\tfrac12\{P,G\}$.  The exact empty and filled reset
maps are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal R_{P,+}(G)&=G-\{P,G\}+PGP,\\
 \mathcal R_{P,-}(G)&=G+P-\{P,G\}+PGP,
 \label{eq:localdeph}
\end{align}
Writing $\mathcal I$ for the identity superoperator, these maps obey
\begin{align}
 \mathcal R_{P,+}-\mathcal I
 &=\Diss^\dagger[\hat\chi]+\Diss^\dagger[\hat N],\\
 \mathcal R_{P,-}-\mathcal I
 &=\Diss^\dagger[\hat\chi^\dagger]+\Diss^\dagger[\hat N].
 \label{eq:resetidentity}
\end{align}
Equivalently, each reset is exactly
$\mathcal R_a=\mathcal I+\mathcal L_a$, with $\mathcal L_a$ given by the
appropriate right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:resetidentity}.
At each visited unit cell the canonical order is $A+$ (loss), $A-$ (gain), $B+$
(loss), $B-$ (gain); cells are visited in the saved random order.  The full sweep is
the corresponding noncommuting product
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{\boldsymbol s}=
 \prod_{\boldsymbol r\in\boldsymbol s}^{\longleftarrow}
 \mathcal R_{P_{\boldsymbol r,B,-},-}
 \mathcal R_{P_{\boldsymbol r,B,+},+}
 \mathcal R_{P_{\boldsymbol r,A,-},-}
 \mathcal R_{P_{\boldsymbol r,A,+},+},
 \label{eq:orderedchannel}
\end{equation}
with schedule record $\boldsymbol s$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:orderedchannel} is the
production channel.  It is also exact to write
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{\boldsymbol s}=\mathcal I+\mathcal L_{{\rm cycle},\boldsymbol s},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L_{{\rm cycle},\boldsymbol s}
 =\mathcal E_{\boldsymbol s}-\mathcal I.
 \label{eq:cycleL}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal L_{{\rm cycle},\boldsymbol s}$ contains every schedule-ordered cross
term generated by the product; it is not $\sum_a\mathcal L_a$.  The literal
no-dephasing unit-step Euler product is excluded
because it is not positivity preserving for overlapping OW modes; it is retained only
as a labelled small-system diagnostic.

\subsection{Infinitesimal continuous equation}

Let $a=(\boldsymbol r,\nu,n)$, $P_a=P_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,n}$,
$s_a=1$ for $n=-$, and $s_a=0$ for $n=+$.  The continuous perfect-correction
action matched to the local increments above is
\begin{align}
 \frac{\mathrm dG}{\mathrm dt}
 &=\mathcal L_{\rm PC}^{(\zeta)}(G)\nonumber\\
 &=\sum_a\left[s_aP_a-\frac12\{P_a,G\}
 +\zeta P_aGP_a-\frac{\zeta}{2}\{P_a,G\}\right].
 \label{eq:continuous}
\end{align}
The $\zeta=0$ arm contains unit gain and loss.  The $\zeta=1$ arm additionally contains
unit number dephasing, equivalent at the many-body level to adding
$\sum_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,n}\Diss[\hat N_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,n}]$.  Although number
dephasing need not preserve Gaussian many-body states, Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuous} closes
exactly on $G$.  Consequently an entropy evaluated only from the natural occupations is
called a Gaussian entropy proxy whenever $\zeta=1$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:continuous} is the infinitesimal Poisson generator
$\sum_a\mathcal L_a$ (or its dephasing-deleted version), not the finite-sweep
$\mathcal L_{{\rm cycle},\boldsymbol s}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cycleL}.

The production integrator is complex128 RK4 with $\delta t=0.05$.  A separate
representative validation subset at $\delta t=0.1,0.05,0.025$ forms the
step-convergence gate; these are not additional production physics cases.  The file
\texttt{CI\_Lindblad\_DW.py} is an algebraic reference only; production consumes the
same OW vectors as the channel and does not inherit that file's distinct wall rule.

\section{Order, translation, and momentum estimators}

Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:ow} is translated identically at every $y$ and the wall mask
depends only on $x$,
\begin{equation}
 [T_y,\mathcal L_{\rm PC}^{(\zeta)}]=0.
\end{equation}
A translation-invariant initial state remains invariant under the continuous equation,
and $G(k_y)$ has exact $2N_x\times2N_x$ blocks.  A finite ordered product generally
does not commute with $T_y$, even if every local family is covariant.  We therefore do
not diagonalize the diagonal Fourier slices of the untwirled channel state and call
them bands.

For each independent schedule sample $s$, first form the approved late-cycle average
\begin{equation}
 \overline G_s=\frac1{N_y}\sum_{c=N_y+1}^{2N_y}G_s(c),
 \label{eq:timeaverage}
\end{equation}
then the exact spatial sliding estimator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_y[\overline G_s]
 =\frac1{N_y}\sum_{q=0}^{N_y-1}T_y^q\overline G_sT_y^{-q}.
 \label{eq:twirl}
\end{equation}
Its blocks define the channel's explicitly \emph{twirled} spectrum.  Every such plot is
accompanied by the untwirled residual
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_T=\frac{\|T_y\overline G_sT_y^{-1}-\overline G_s\|_F}
 {\|\overline G_s\|_F}.
 \label{eq:translationresidual}
\end{equation}
The map $\mathcal T_y$ is a nonunitary conditional expectation: in the $y$-momentum
basis it deletes every $k_y\ne k_y'$ coherence.  It can therefore change the natural
occupations, and its block eigenvalues must not be presented as eigenvalues of the raw
ordered-channel state.  Nor is Eq.~\eqref{eq:twirl} a trajectory or schedule average;
it is applied separately to the late average from each deterministic schedule sample.
The symmetry-free spectrum retained for comparison is instead
\begin{equation}
 \overline G_s|\psi_{sj}\rangle=\nu^{\rm raw}_{sj}|\psi_{sj}\rangle,
 \qquad
 \nu^{\rm raw}_{s1}\le\cdots\le\nu^{\rm raw}_{s,2N_xN_y}.
 \label{eq:rawspectrum}
\end{equation}
These global eigenvalues are well defined, but they carry no exact $k_y$ label.
For a complementary representation that makes no symmetry assumption, a global mode
$G|\phi_j\rangle=\nu_j|\phi_j\rangle$ carries momentum weight
\begin{equation}
 Z_j(k_y)=\sum_{x,\mu}|\langle x,k_y,\mu|\phi_j\rangle|^2,
 \qquad \sum_{k_y}Z_j(k_y)=1.
 \label{eq:momentumweight}
\end{equation}

\section{Occupation-spectrum results and estimators}

Let
\begin{equation}
 G(k_y)|\phi_j(k_y)\rangle=\nu_j(k_y)|\phi_j(k_y)\rangle
\end{equation}
denote either an exact continuous block or an explicitly twirled channel block.  With
$\mathcal W_a=\{x_a-1,x_a,x_a+1\}$,
\begin{equation}
 W_j^{(a)}(k_y)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal W_a,\mu}
 |\phi_{j,x\mu}(k_y)|^2.
\end{equation}
Eligible wall modes have $W_j^{(a)}\ge0.25$ and minimize
$|\nu_j-1/2|+0.02(1-W_j^{(a)})$ independently at each momentum.  The displayed slope
is an ordinary least-squares fit over
$|k_y|\le\max(2.5\,2\pi/N_y,0.22)$.  It is not overlap tracking or a topological index.

The global midpoint gap, bulk-excluded gap, Gaussian entropy proxy, Gaussian
charge-variance proxy, and midgap wall localization are
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{1/2}&=\min_j|\nu_j-\tfrac12|,\\
 \Delta_{\rm bulk}&=\min_{j:W_j^{\mathcal W}<0.25}|\nu_j-\tfrac12|,\\
 S_G&=-\sum_j[\nu_j\ln\nu_j+(1-\nu_j)\ln(1-\nu_j)],\\
 V_G&=\tr[G(\id-G)]=\sum_j\nu_j(1-\nu_j),\\
 \mathcal L_{\rm mid}&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal W}p_x,
\end{align}
where $p_x$ is averaged over the $2N_y$ modes closest to half occupation and normalized
to unit total weight.  For a non-Gaussian full Fock-space state, $V_G$ is not the true
charge variance; it is explicitly reported only as a two-point Gaussian proxy.  No
eigenvalue outside $[0,1]$ is clipped to manufacture an entropy; its physicality
violation is reported instead.

The occupation gap grows sharply through the unsampled $\alpha=2$ reference.  For the
$n_{\rm shell}=1$ dephasing-on continuous arm it changes from
$7.04\times10^{-4}$ at $\alpha=1.5$ to $5.75\times10^{-2}$ at $1.75$,

$1.00$ or larger without continuous dephasing, and $1.90$ or larger with it.  The
campaign therefore finds an occupation-spectrum transition but no dynamical gap
closing in the measured sector.

\section{Relaxation and density-kick response}

The affine continuous equation has a homogeneous covariance generator
$\mathbb L^{(\zeta)}$; the ordered sweep has a homogeneous map $\mathbb M_{\boldsymbol s}$.
Their comparable relaxation scales are
\begin{align}
 \gamma_{\rm cont}&=\min_{\ell\ne0}[-\operatorname{Re}\ell],\\
 \gamma_{\rm ch}&=\min_{0<|m|<1}[-\ln|m|],
\end{align}
with exact neutral sectors removed by a stated tolerance.  Complex and nonnormal
spectra are retained rather than replaced by the older $2d_{\min}$ formula.

A source at wall $a$ and coordinate $y_0$ is kicked by
\begin{equation}
 \hat U_\epsilon=\exp[-i\epsilon\hat n_{a,y_0}],\qquad
 \delta G(0)=i[P_{a,y_0},G_{\rm bg}].
\end{equation}
The same homogeneous channel or generator then propagates the tangent.  The wall profile
and positive-lobe center are
\begin{align}
 \chi_a^{\rm wall}(r,t)&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal W_a,\mu}
 [\delta G(t)]_{x,y_0+r,\mu;x,y_0+r,\mu},\\
 y_{+,a}(t)&=\frac{\sum_r\widetilde r[\chi_a^{\rm wall}(r,t)]_+}
 {\sum_r[\chi_a^{\rm wall}(r,t)]_+},
\end{align}
where $\widetilde r=((r+N_y/2)\bmod N_y)-N_y/2$.  Velocity is the OLS slope over
$2\le t\le0.375N_y$, subject to the saved response-norm threshold.  For the ordered
channel, all $y_0$ are retained before averaging, so the schedule-origin spread is a
deterministic systematic rather than a statistical confidence interval.

most of the nominal fit window.  We therefore do not interpret its fitted displacement
as a chiral velocity.

is evaluated.  This is expected because a unit sweep retains schedule-ordered cross
terms that are absent from the summed infinitesimal generator.

\section{Validation and interpretation rules}

All 105 cases completed and all immutable result hashes survived analysis.  The local
equations agree with the canonical channel on small systems;
the continuous action agrees with the derivative of weakened local maps; RK4 shows the
expected step convergence; the full-dephasing channel remains physical; continuous
translation residuals meet tolerance; cycle coordinates and sample IDs are complete;
and the response tangent agrees with a finite central difference.  Three stored
gauge-dependent raw-OW byte hashes differed across otherwise matched arms; fresh
construction reproduced one recorded realization in each group, so these are retained
as provenance warnings rather than misidentified as physical-projector mismatches.

The completed note may report stationary or late-state occupation flow, relaxation,
and damped directional response.  It may not infer a trajectory Lyapunov boundary mode,
quantized transport, or schedule-independent chirality.  The random ordered channel's
translation twirl is an estimator applied to its late state, not a symmetry retroactively
assigned to the underlying map.  Its raw ascending spectrum is reported separately and
is never labeled by $k_y$.
