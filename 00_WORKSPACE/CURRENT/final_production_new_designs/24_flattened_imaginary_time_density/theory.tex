\title{Imaginary-time density memory of a domain-wall parent ground state}
\author{Numerical benchmark notes}
\date{\today}
\begin{abstract}
We compute connected equilibrium density autocorrelations for the half-filled
ground state of the signed overcomplete-Wannier parent. The calculation is
deterministic and distinct from conditional purification or Born-averaged
adaptive-circuit dynamics. Both local density memory and column-charge memory
are obtained without many-body time stepping.
\end{abstract}

\section{Parent and state}
With an overcomplete Wannier (OW) basis, define the single-particle parent
\begin{equation}
 h_{\rm flat}=\sum_R(P_{A+}+P_{B+}-P_{A-}-P_{B-}),\qquad
 \hat H=\sum_{ij}(h_{\rm flat})_{ij}\hat c_i^\dagger\hat c_j.
\end{equation}
No subsequent replacement of energies by their signs is made. Distinct OW
projectors need not commute. We retain hard support truncation, $N_x=20$,
$N_y=30$, $\alpha_1=1$, $\alpha_2=30$, $n_{\rm shell}=1$, and trial orbital $X$.
The spatial benchmark's regulator transforms representative OW columns at
$k+10^{-7}/N_y$ but retains the periodic untwisted Bloch basis. This is the
occupation-regulator convention, not a flux-threading protocol. We diagonalize
the resulting regulated parent in thirty $40\times40$ blocks and fill the
lowest 600 modes. The same energies and eigenvectors define propagation.
Write the ground state as $\lvert\Omega\rangle\!\rangle$, its energy as $E_0$,
and $G_{ij}=\langle\!\langle\Omega\rvert\hat c_i^\dagger\hat c_j
\lvert\Omega\rangle\!\rangle$. In the code, the occupied projector is $P=G^{\mathsf T}$.

\section{Density fluctuations and spectral evaluation}
For a Hermitian single-particle matrix $D$, let
$\hat O=\sum_{ij}D_{ij}\hat c_i^\dagger\hat c_j$ and
$\delta\hat O=\hat O-\langle\hat O\rangle\hat{\mathbf{1}}$.
For $\tau\geq0$ the connected equilibrium correlator is
\begin{align}
 C_O(\tau)&=\langle\delta\hat O(\tau)\delta\hat O(0)\rangle,
 &\hat O(\tau)&=e^{\tau\hat H}\hat Oe^{-\tau\hat H},\\
 C_O(\tau)&=\langle\!\langle\Omega\rvert\delta\hat O
 e^{-\tau(\hat H-E_0\hat{\mathbf{1}})}\delta\hat O
 \lvert\Omega\rangle\!\rangle.
\end{align}
A one-body insertion creates only particle--hole excitations. With $a$ occupied
and $b$ empty this gives exactly
\begin{equation}
 C_O(\tau)=\sum_{a\in\mathrm{occ},b\in\mathrm{empty}}
 |u_b^\dagger D u_a|^2e^{-\tau(\epsilon_b-\epsilon_a)}.
\end{equation}
The weights and excitation energies are nonnegative. Therefore each
autocorrelation is nonnegative and nonincreasing. At contact,
\begin{equation}
 C_O(0)=\operatorname{tr}[PD(\mathbf{1}-P)D].
\end{equation}
For total charge $D=\mathbf{1}$ this vanishes, as it must for a fixed-number
Slater determinant. Imaginary time has inverse-parent-energy units; no
identification with circuit cycles is assumed.

\section{Local and column observables}
Let $\hat n_{x,y}=\sum_\mu\hat c_{x,y,\mu}^\dagger\hat c_{x,y,\mu}$.
We retain both
\begin{align}
 C_x^{\rm local}(\tau)&=\frac1{N_y}\sum_y
 \langle\delta\hat n_{x,y}(\tau)\delta\hat n_{x,y}(0)\rangle,\\
 C_x^{\rm column}(\tau)&=\frac1{N_y}
 \langle\delta\hat Q_x(\tau)\delta\hat Q_x(0)\rangle,
 &\hat Q_x&=\sum_y\hat n_{x,y}.
\end{align}
Translation symmetry makes the local origin average exact. Writing the Bloch
eigenvectors as $v_{ka}$, local transition weights at fixed $x$ are
$|\sum_\mu v_{kb,x\mu}^*v_{k'a,x\mu}|^2/N_y^2$.
Column weights instead conserve momentum and are
$\delta_{kk'}|\sum_\mu v_{kb,x\mu}^*v_{k'a,x\mu}|^2/N_y$.
The observables consequently probe different momentum sectors.

The existing spatial benchmark plots
$B_x(r)=\tfrac12\sum_{\mu\nu}|G_{(x,0,\mu),(x,r,\nu)}|^2$.
Wick contraction gives, for distinct cells,
$\langle\delta\hat n_{x,0}\delta\hat n_{x,r}\rangle=-2B_x(r)$.
At $r=0$ the contact term adds $\langle\hat n_{x,0}\rangle$.
Thus the positive spatial proxy must not be mistaken for the signed density
correlator, nor for the positive imaginary-time autocorrelation.

\section{Relation to filtering and numerical interpretation}
Normalized imaginary-time filtering evolves a state according to
\begin{equation}
 \hat\rho(\tau)=\frac{e^{-\tau\hat H}\hat\rho(0)e^{-\tau\hat H}}
 {\operatorname{Tr}[e^{-2\tau\hat H}\hat\rho(0)]}.
\end{equation}
This operation alone does not define the two density insertions above.
Furthermore, a finite ordered product of noncommuting postselected OW
measurements is not automatically $e^{-\tau\hat H}$. The present benchmark
contains neither such a measurement record nor an adaptive-channel average.

We save $\tau=0$ and 240 logarithmically spaced positive times from $10^{-3}$
to $10^3$, using double precision throughout. Ratios $C_x(\tau)/C_x(0)$ are
undefined when the equal-time variance is at most $10^{-12}$; raw data are
never floored. There is no sampling SEM. A finite system has a discrete
particle--hole spectrum, and a small gap matters only if its density matrix
element is appreciable. No power-law exponent is fitted automatically.
