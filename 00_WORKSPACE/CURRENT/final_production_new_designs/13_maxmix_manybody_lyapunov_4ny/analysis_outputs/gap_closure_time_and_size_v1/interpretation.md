The finite-time Lyapunov gap decreases approximately inversely with circumference at all three observation-time ratios. Its rescaled mean continues to increase between T=3Ny and 4Ny by 7–12%, so long-time convergence is not established.

The all-size linear extrapolations at T=3Ny and 4Ny have slightly negative intercepts, with 95% sampling intervals below zero. Such an extrapolation cannot represent a physical negative gap. Omitting Ny=20,24 or adding a 1/Ny^2 correction makes the intervals include zero, while increasing uncertainty. These results demonstrate sensitivity to finite-size modeling; they do not select a preferred fit because it contains zero.

The prior pure-power-law endpoint result is reproduced sample-wise. Its fitted exponent of about 1.11 and the current unconstrained linear-in-1/Ny fits use different finite-size models. The negative linear intercept is an extrapolation/model sensitivity, not a data or estimator regression.

Suggested claim: The observed finite-time gaps show finite-size softening approximately proportional to 1/Ny, compatible with a gapless limit. Residual time dependence and finite-size-model sensitivity prevent a definitive estimate of the asymptotic thermodynamic gap.
