Fitting the raw modular gap with a free time intercept yields later-window rates compatible across [2Ny,3Ny] and [3Ny,4Ny] for every measured size. Each paired 95% whole-trajectory bootstrap interval for their difference contains zero. The early window shows lower rates for several sizes. The errors on finite-window slopes are larger than on endpoint ratios, so consistency does not establish arbitrarily precise time convergence.

For the latest window [3Ny,4Ny], the mean rate decreases from 0.06534 +/- 0.00653 at Ny20 to 0.02158 +/- 0.00206 at Ny60 (one SEM), and Ny times the mean lies between about 1.21 and 1.38. This supports approximately inverse-circumference scaling.

An unconstrained weighted fit of latest-window mean rates to d0+a/Ny gives d0=-0.002267, with 95% bootstrap interval [-0.008597,0.004094]. Omitting Ny20,24 gives d0=-0.000139, interval [-0.009251,0.008980]. Both include zero, but also permit a small positive residual gap. The middle-window all-size linear intercept is below zero; its interval includes zero after omitting Ny20,24. All fit results are retained in finite_size_fits.csv.

The prior many-body first-level slope results reproduce these rates after dividing their gap1 by two, reflecting the log(sigma^2) convention. The comparison is on the same samples, time grids and windows. This agreement identifies the estimator conversion explicitly; it is not an independent new simulation.

Suggested scientific statement: The modular-gap growth rate decreases approximately inversely with circumference. Rates from the two latest fitting windows are statistically consistent, and the latest-window finite-size extrapolation is compatible with a vanishing gap. The accessible sizes and times do not exclude a small residual asymptotic gap.
