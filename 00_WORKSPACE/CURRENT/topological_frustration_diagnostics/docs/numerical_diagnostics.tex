\title{\texorpdfstring{Numerical Diagnostics for Topological Frustration in a\\
Domain-Wall-Truncated Adaptive Fermion Circuit}{Numerical Diagnostics for Topological Frustration in a Domain-Wall-Truncated Adaptive Fermion Circuit}}
\author{Asadullah Bhuiyan}
\affiliation{Department of Physics, Cornell University, Ithaca, New York 14853, USA}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We specify a CPU reference program that distinguishes three logically separate
features of a number-conserving adaptive Gaussian circuit: algebraic
compatibility of its target measurements, stochastic defect and transfer
activity along trajectories, and slow single-particle dynamics near a hard
interface.  The interface is produced by truncating every controller mode to
the sector containing its center and by updating only the active topological
slab.  It therefore does not require a physically trivial exterior mass.  The
reference implementation nevertheless retains the canonical geometry
parameters $\alpha_1=1$ and $\alpha_2=30$.  We define exact
Gaussian-completion tests, a weighted minimum-frustration problem, defect and
signed-transfer records, empirical full-counting statistics, two deliberately
distinct spectral proxies, and a paired local-response test for directional
wall propagation.  The note also states the numerical controls and the
inference licensed by each observable.  It is a methods and validation
document and contains no numerical claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Controller and hard-wall geometry}

The active single-particle Hilbert space has dimension
$D=2N_{\rm cell}$, with two complex orbitals per cell.  A covariance
$G=2C-\mathbf{1}$ is represented in terms of the one-body correlation matrix
$0\leq C\leq\mathbf{1}$.  A pure number-conserving Gaussian state at half
filling corresponds to a rank-$N$ projector $C$, where $N=D/2$.

At each cell $R=(R_x,R_y)$ the controller contains four normalized observable
Wannier vectors
\begin{equation}
 \ket{w_{aR}},\qquad a\in\{A+,A-,B+,B-\},
\end{equation}
with desired occupations
\begin{equation}
 s_{A+}=s_{B+}=0,\qquad s_{A-}=s_{B-}=1.
 \label{eq:targets}
\end{equation}
The untruncated vectors are obtained from the upper- and lower-band projectors
of the flattened class-A Hamiltonian at $\alpha=1$.  A finite support cutoff
$n_{\rm shell}$ is applied first.  For a domain-wall center $R$, the additional
binary mask is
\begin{equation}
 M_R(r)=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&r\text{ and }R\text{ lie in the same domain-wall sector},\\
  0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \ket{w^{\rm DW}_{aR}}=
 \frac{M_R\ket{w_{aR}}}{\lVert M_R\ket{w_{aR}}\rVert}.
 \label{eq:dw-mask}
\end{equation}
Only centers and one-body indices inside the slab $x_L\leq x\leq x_R$ are
retained by \texttt{meas\_slab\_only}.  Consequently, active controller vectors
are determined entirely by $\alpha_1$ and the mask in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dw-mask}.
Changing the unused exterior value $\alpha_2$ changes only excluded exterior
frames.  The production configuration is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_1=1,\qquad \alpha_2=30,\qquad
 \texttt{DW=True},\quad
 \texttt{dw\_truncation=True},\quad
 \texttt{meas\_slab\_only=True}.
 \label{eq:reference-geometry}
\end{equation}
An automated invariance test compares active frames at $\alpha_2=1$ and $30$;
this comparison is a software control, not a physics sweep.

\section{Static Gaussian completion}

Let $W_F$ contain the desired-filled vectors $A-,B-$ and $W_E$ the
desired-empty vectors $A+,B+$.  Rank-revealing singular-value decompositions,
with relative tolerance $10^{-10}$, define orthonormal bases $Q_F,Q_E$ and
subspaces $\cV_F,\cV_E\subset\cH$.  The singular values of
$Q_F^\dagger Q_E$ are $\cos\vartheta_j$.  We report
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{\max}=\max_j\cos\vartheta_j,
 \qquad
 \Phi=\Tr(P_FP_E)=\sum_j\cos^2\vartheta_j,
 \label{eq:angles}
\end{equation}
where $P_F=Q_FQ_F^\dagger$ and $P_E=Q_EQ_E^\dagger$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact completion]
A rank-$N$ projector $C$ satisfying
$C\ket{w}=\ket{w}$ for every filled constraint and
$C\ket{w}=0$ for every empty constraint exists if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \cV_F\perp\cV_E,\qquad
 \rank W_F\leq N\leq D-\rank W_E.
 \label{eq:completion}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Indeed, necessity follows by placing $\cV_F$ in $\operatorname{im}C$ and
$\cV_E$ in $\ker C$.  Conversely, under Eq.~\eqref{eq:completion}, augment
$\cV_F$ by any $N-\dim\cV_F$ directions in
$(\cV_F\oplus\cV_E)^\perp$ and project onto the resulting space.  The code
constructs this projector when possible and checks filled leakage, empty
leakage, and rank error.

When exact completion fails, define the weighted defect functional
\begin{equation}
 \cF[C]=\sum_a\gamma_a
 \left[s_a+(1-2s_a)\bra{w_a}C\ket{w_a}\right],
 \qquad \rank C=N,
 \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
with $\gamma_a=1$ by default.  Writing
\begin{equation}
 A=\sum_a\gamma_a(1-2s_a)\ket{w_a}\bra{w_a},
\end{equation}
Ky Fan's variational principle gives
\begin{equation}
 F_\star=\min_C\cF[C]
 =\sum_a\gamma_as_a+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_j^\uparrow(A).
 \label{eq:fstar}
\end{equation}
The minimizing eigenspace yields channel-resolved residuals
$f_{a\star}$ and the map $f_\star(x,y)$.  Equations~\eqref{eq:angles}--\eqref{eq:fstar}
answer whether the controller constraints admit a common Gaussian state and
where an irreducible static compromise resides.  They do not determine whether
trajectory activity survives at long times.

\section{Trajectory activity and counting statistics}

For the $m$th visited channel, let $n_{a,m}\in\{0,1\}$ be the sampled
Born-rule outcome and $n'_{a,m}$ the occupation imposed by any subsequent
feedback reset.  We record
\begin{equation}
 X_{a,m}=\lvert n_{a,m}-s_a\rvert,
 \qquad
 Y_{a,m}=n'_{a,m}-n_{a,m}\in\{-1,0,+1\}.
 \label{eq:xy}
\end{equation}
Thus $X$ is a wrong-outcome indicator, $\lvert Y\rvert$ is actual transfer
activity, and the sign of $Y$ distinguishes gain from loss.  Under perfect
correction, $X=\lvert Y\rvert$ event by event.  Under imperfect feedback they
need not agree.  Forced postselection does not produce a stochastic activity
record and is never encoded as a string of zero events.  The desired-outcome
Born probability is retained as a conditional frustration diagnostic.

The interface region contains the active columns within
$w_{\rm int}=n_{\rm shell}+1$ of either hard wall; the remaining active cells
form the interior.  For a region $R$ and observation window $T$, define
\begin{equation}
 K_R^X(T)=\sum_{m\in R,T}X_m,\qquad
 K_R^Y(T)=\sum_{m\in R,T}\lvert Y_m\rvert.
\end{equation}
The code reports rates, spatial profiles, local inter-event survival
probabilities, and the first three cumulants divided by $T$.  Its finite-time,
empirical scaled cumulant-generating function is
\begin{equation}
 \theta_R(s,T)=\frac{1}{T}\log
 \left\langle e^{-sK_R(T)}\right\rangle_{\rm traj}.
 \label{eq:scgf}
\end{equation}
Evaluation uses log-sum-exp stabilization on 41 fields in
$-0.2\leq s\leq0.2$, with bootstrap intervals and a reweighting effective
sample fraction.  Points below $0.1$ effective sample fraction are flagged.
Equation~\eqref{eq:scgf} measures finite-time fluctuations and intermittency;
it is not the leading eigenvalue of an explicitly constructed tilted channel,
and finite-size curvature is not a proof of a dynamical singularity.

\section{Slow-mode proxies}

\subsection{Lyapunov tangent cocycle}

Along a sampled trajectory, the existing engine propagates a one-body tangent
frame $V_t$ through the differential of every measurement and reset.  A QR
factorization after each cycle gives
\begin{equation}
 V_t=Q_tR_t,\qquad
 \lambda_j(t)=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\log\lvert(R_\tau)_{jj}\rvert.
\end{equation}
We retain the full active-space spectrum on small systems and define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{\rm L}(t)=\min_j\lvert\lambda_j(t)\rvert.
\end{equation}
The final minimum-$\lvert\lambda\rvert$ vector is characterized by its
interface weight, left/right-wall weights, inverse participation ratio, and
transverse profile.  This diagnoses marginal directions of the implemented
single-particle tangent cocycle, not the full nonlinear stochastic channel.

\subsection{Regularized-Choi particle transfer}

A separate run propagates the regularized Gaussian Choi covariance.  On its
finite sector, the particle transfer matrix is
\begin{equation}
 T_p=-(\mathbf{1}+\Sigma_{LL})^{-1}\Sigma_{LR}.
\end{equation}
For a pure Choi covariance, diagonalizing the Hermitian block
$\Sigma_{LL}$ with eigenvalues $a_j\in[-1,1]$ gives finite transfer exponents
\begin{equation}
 \xi_j(t)=\frac{1}{2t}
 \left[\log(1-a_j)-\log(1+a_j)\right],
 \qquad
 \Delta_{\mathrm{Choi}}(t)=\min_{j\,{\rm finite}}\lvert\xi_j(t)\rvert.
 \label{eq:choi}
\end{equation}
Endpoint zeros and poles, spectral-interval violations, eigensystem residuals,
and near-gap eigenvectors are saved explicitly.  If no finite sector remains,
$\Delta_{\mathrm{Choi}}$ is undefined rather than zero; the latest finite
near-gap vectors and their observation cycle are retained.  The Lyapunov and
Choi gaps
are compared side by side but never merged: they answer different linearized
questions and neither is labeled the exact Markov-channel gap.

\section{Paired directional response}

After $2N_y$ equilibration cycles, select a unit cell $(x_w,y_0)$ on a wall and
let $P$ project onto its two onsite orbitals.  From the same equilibrated
covariance construct
\begin{equation}
 G^+=QGQ+P,\qquad G^-=QGQ-P,\qquad Q=\mathbf{1}-P.
 \label{eq:reset}
\end{equation}
The pair is evolved with identical per-sample random streams and identical
randomized schedules under perfect correction.  Because this protocol consumes
one Born variate per channel and no stochastic correction variate, the common
stream remains aligned even when paired outcomes differ.  The normalized
response is
\begin{equation}
 \delta n(x,y,t)=\frac{n^+(x,y,t)-n^-(x,y,t)}{2},
 \qquad \sum_{x,y}\delta n(x,y,0)=1.
\end{equation}
After projecting onto each wall strip, the code records the response norm,
absolute width, wall weight, and periodic signed moment
\begin{equation}
 M_1(t)=\frac{\sum_y d_y\,\delta n_{\rm wall}(y,t)}
 {\sum_y\lvert\delta n_{\rm wall}(y,t)\rvert},
 \qquad d_y\in[-N_y/2,N_y/2).
 \label{eq:first-moment}
\end{equation}
An early-time slope estimates directed drift before periodic wraparound.
Random update order is primary; raster order quantifies ordering bias.  A
credible chiral response requires wall localization, opposite drift signs at
the two oppositely oriented walls, stability under randomized order, and no
analogous stable signal in the uniform controller.

The smoke suite uses $4\times6$ uniform and hard-wall systems.  The reference
activity grid fixes $N_x=12$, takes $N_y=16,24,32$, uses 64 trajectories and
$4N_y$ cycles, and discards the first $2N_y$.  Spectral reference runs use
$N_x=8$, $N_y=8,12,16$, eight trajectories, and $2N_y$ cycles.  Response runs
use the activity geometries, 64 paired trajectories, $2N_y$ equilibration
cycles, and an $N_y/2$ response horizon.  The default cutoff is
$n_{\rm shell}=1$.  Runs at $n_{\rm shell}=2$ are explicit dynamical
convergence controls, and the untruncated frame is an explicit static-only
control; neither is launched by default.

Every output stores the canonical entry point, geometry, active basis,
tolerances, root seed, derived sample seeds, protocol, and update order.
Equilibration observers stream charge, charge variance, Gaussian entropy,
purity defect, and successive covariance change.  No full covariance history
is required.  Independent geometries and trajectories execute in CPU process
workers, with numerical-library threads capped per worker.  Worker seeds are
derived before dispatch, results are merged in task order, and only the parent
process writes files.  The run record includes the CPU and memory budget,
chosen worker and thread counts, process identifiers, and task timing.
Production characterization begins only after unit tests, $\alpha_2$
invariance, serial--parallel reproducibility, the full small-system CLI smoke
campaign, and a measured parallel-utilization benchmark pass.  The launcher
then runs static, activity, and response pilots before their production stages;
any failed gate terminates the sequence without a success marker.

\section{Interpretive limits}

The hard mask deliberately studies a controller boundary rather than a smooth
material interface.  Smooth walls and mixed topological/trivial projector
families are outside the present scope.  A nonzero $F_\star$ establishes an
algebraic compromise but does not force a nonzero stationary rate.  A finite
empirical SCGF samples trajectory fluctuations but does not replace a tilted
operator.  Closing Lyapunov or Choi proxies are evidence for slow one-body
sectors only after localization and finite-size checks.  Directional response
becomes evidence for chirality only after update-order and uniform controls.
These separations are part of the numerical specification, not merely
terminological cautions.
