\title{Mismatch-Click Defects and Replicated Update Maps in an Adaptive
Class-A Fermion Circuit: A Working Technical Note}
\author{Asadullah Bhuiyan}
\affiliation{Department of Physics, Cornell University, Ithaca, New York 14853, USA}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We investigate whether the binary signs in the perfect-correction covariance
map admit a useful dynamical-defect or statistical-mechanics description.  The
target sign is fixed by the controller band, while the Born sign defines a
binary mismatch click
\(d=(1-\eta_m\eta_s)/2\).  We show that this click is an exact centered
Bernoulli innovation driving a rank-at-most-one covariance kick.  The measured orbital
is healed after either outcome; for a pure Slater trajectory, the branch label
instead flips one state-dependent partner orbital.  This yields an exact
positive Perron--Frobenius transfer operator on the full covariance manifold, a
Gaussian-closed tilted channel for the full counting statistics of mismatch
activity, and a local replicated density-operator transfer whose trace is
\(\sum_\xi p_\xi^n\).  Two marked replicas represent the Born-averaged squared
correlator and intrinsic charge variance.  The one-copy covariance average
closes affinely, whereas its exact fluctuation equation contains a nonlinear
shot-noise source and generates an infinite moment hierarchy.  In contrast to
the classical cellular automaton of O'Dea \emph{et al.}, overlapping
noncommuting controller projectors do not preserve a common diagonal algebra;
the click record alone is therefore not generically an autonomous local
Markov process.  We finish with a testable narrow-distribution closure and a
retarded effective click action whose kernel is a linearized covariance Green
function.  This is a working note: exact
identities, proposed approximations, and conjectural wall physics are labeled
separately.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, answer, and status of the construction}
\label{sec:question}

The microscopic covariance update in the current class-A circuit
contains two signs~\cite{BhuiyanPanJian2026},
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{m|s}^{(j)}(G)
 =Q_j\bigl(\Id+\eta_mGP_j\bigr)^{-1}GQ_j+\eta_sP_j.
 \label{eq:starting-map}
\end{equation}
Here \(\eta_m=2m-1\) is the sampled occupation sign and
\(\eta_s=2s-1\) is the target sign.  For the controller convention of the
main manuscript, the lower-band mode has \(s=1\), hence \(\eta_s=+1\), and
the upper-band mode has \(s=0\), hence \(\eta_s=-1\).  Thus \(\eta_s\) is a
fixed background attached to the scheduled projector, while \(\eta_m\) is the
physical Born-random variable.

The natural binary field is their relative sign,
\begin{equation}
 d=\frac{1-\eta_m\eta_s}{2}\in\{0,1\}.
 \label{eq:defect-definition-intro}
\end{equation}
It records whether the measurement disagrees with the target and therefore
whether the fresh-ancilla correction fires.  The provisional answer developed
in this note has four levels.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Exact classical hidden-state process.}  The covariance \(G\) is a
 classical continuous state, and \(d\) is a binary emission from a positive
 Markov kernel on the covariance manifold.
 \item \emph{Exact one-copy closure.}  After Born averaging, \(G\) obeys a
 deterministic affine reset map.  The mean mismatch rate is consequently
 one-copy closed.
 \item \emph{Exact replica transfer.}  Shared outcomes couple density-operator
 replicas.  The resulting local transfer gives record R\'enyi sums and marked
 formulas for squared correlators and quantum density fluctuations.
 \item \emph{No generic click-only cellular automaton.}  The click is healed at
 the measured orbital, and its effect propagates through a state-dependent
 partner mode.  Eliminating \(G\) produces retarded many-body interactions
 among clicks rather than a finite-range autonomous bit rule.
\end{enumerate}
The fourth point is not a failure of the statistical-mechanics idea.  It says
that the exact theory is either a positive infinite-dimensional classical
transfer problem or a local interacting replicated-fermion tensor network.
A local scalar defect theory, if present at the wall, must emerge after a
controlled coarse graining.

\section{Microscopic instrument and sign dictionary}
\label{sec:instrument}

Consider a parity-even, number-conserving Gaussian state of \(N\) complex
fermion modes.  We use
\begin{equation}
 C_{ij}=\Tr(\rho c_i^\dagger c_j),
 \qquad G=2C-\Id,
 \qquad -\Id\leq G\leq\Id.
 \label{eq:covariance-definition}
\end{equation}
For a normalized controller orbital
\(a_\chi=\sum_i\chi_i c_i\), define
\begin{equation}
 n_\chi=a_\chi^\dagger a_\chi,
 \quad P=\chi\chi^\dagger,
 \quad Q=\Id-P,
 \quad g=\chi^\dagger G\chi.
 \label{eq:local-definitions}
\end{equation}
The measurement projectors are \(\Pi_1=n_\chi\) and
\(\Pi_0=\Id-n_\chi\).  For a target \(s\in\{0,1\}\), write
\begin{equation}
 \tau\equiv\eta_s=2s-1.
 \label{eq:tau-definition}
\end{equation}
The perfect-correction Kraus operators can then be organized by the mismatch
click rather than by the raw outcome:
\begin{align}
 K_{0|s}&=\Pi_s,
 &K_{1|s}&=(a_\chi^\dagger)^s a_\chi^{1-s},
 \label{eq:defect-kraus}\\
 K_{d|s}^\dagger K_{d|s}&=\Pi_{s\oplus d}.
 \nonumber
\end{align}
The raw measurement sign is recovered from
\begin{equation}
 \eta_m=\tau(-1)^d.
 \label{eq:eta-from-defect}
\end{equation}
The two branch probabilities are
\begin{equation}
 p_0(G)=\frac{1+\tau g}{2},
 \qquad
 p_1(G)=\frac{1-\tau g}{2}.
 \label{eq:defect-probabilities}
\end{equation}
We will repeatedly use the \emph{soft mismatch propensity}
\begin{equation}
 q_s(G)\equiv p_1(G)=\frac{1-\tau\chi^\dagger G\chi}{2}.
 \label{eq:soft-defect}
\end{equation}
The hard click is a Bernoulli sample \(d\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q_s(G))\).

The correction has a signed charge.  With \(\hat N=\sum_i c_i^\dagger c_i\),
\begin{equation}
 [\hat N,K_{d|s}]=\tau d\,K_{d|s}.
 \label{eq:kraus-charge}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for an input branch of definite particle number,
\begin{equation}
 N_T-N_0=\sum_{t=1}^{D}\tau_t d_t.
 \label{eq:charge-accounting}
\end{equation}
A lower-band mismatch is a particle injection and an upper-band mismatch is a
hole injection.  For number-indefinite mixed inputs, Eq.~\eqref{eq:kraus-charge}
remains the operator statement, but conditioning can also change the mean
charge of the unmeasured sector; Eq.~\eqref{eq:charge-accounting} should then
not be applied as a branchwise expectation-value identity.

\section{The mismatch click as an exact covariance innovation}
\label{sec:innovation}

Define two matrices built from the incoming covariance,
\begin{align}
 B(G)&=QGQ+\tau P,
 \nonumber\\
 R(G)&=QGPGQ=(QG\chi)(QG\chi)^\dagger.
 \label{eq:B-R-definitions}
\end{align}
For \(|g|<1\), the rank-one resolvent in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:starting-map} gives
\begin{align}
 \Phi_0(G)&=B(G)-\frac{\tau R(G)}{1+\tau g},
 \nonumber\\
 \Phi_1(G)&=B(G)+\frac{\tau R(G)}{1-\tau g}.
 \label{eq:two-defect-branches}
\end{align}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \Phi_d(G)=B(G)
 +\frac{2\tau\,[d-q_s(G)]}{1-g^2}R(G).}
 \label{eq:innovation-map}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
 \xi_d\equiv d-q_s(G),
 \qquad
 \E[\xi_d|G]=0,
 \qquad
 \E[\xi_d^2|G]=\frac{1-g^2}{4}
 \label{eq:martingale-innovation}
\end{equation}
is an exact martingale innovation.  For every physical covariance,
\begin{equation}
 \Tr R=\chi^\dagger GQG\chi\leq1-g^2.
 \label{eq:R-bound}
\end{equation}
At \(|g|=1\), one branch has zero probability, \(R=0\), and the unique
realized update is \(\Phi=B\).  The normalized counterfactual branch is not
defined.  Born-weighted expressions containing
\(R^{\otimes2}/(1-g^2)\) have the unique zero limit by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:R-bound}, whereas \(R/(1-g^2)\) and its orientation need not
have a path-independent limit.

The click is not a persistent defect of the just-measured mode.  Every
nonzero-probability branch satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_d(G)\chi=\tau\chi,
 \qquad
 \chi^\dagger\Phi_d(G)\chi=\tau.
 \label{eq:measured-mode-healed}
\end{equation}
The mode \(\chi\) factorizes with its target occupation immediately after the
event.  Away from the deterministic endpoints, the record survives in the
orthogonal complement through the rank-at-most-one matrix \(R\).

\subsection{Pure-state partner orbital}

For a pure Slater trajectory, \(G^2=\Id\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:R-bound} is saturated.
Whenever \(|g|<1\), define
\begin{equation}
 u=\frac{QG\chi}{\sqrt{1-g^2}},
 \qquad U=uu^\dagger.
 \label{eq:partner-orbital}
\end{equation}
Then \(u\) is normalized, orthogonal to \(\chi\), and
\begin{equation}
 R=(1-g^2)U,
 \qquad QGQ\,u=-g\,u.
 \label{eq:pure-R}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:innovation-map} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_d(G)=B(G)+2\tau[d-q_s(G)]U.
 \label{eq:pure-innovation-map}
\end{equation}
On the two-dimensional active subspace,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_d(G)\chi=\tau\chi,
 \qquad
 \Phi_d(G)u=\tau(2d-1)u.
 \label{eq:partner-eigenvalues}
\end{equation}
All one-particle directions outside this active plane are identical in the two
branches.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Phi_1(G)-\Phi_0(G)=2\tau U.}
 \label{eq:branch-difference}
\end{equation}
Microscopically, the correction heals \(\chi\), while \(d=1\) flips exactly one
state-dependent Schmidt-partner orbital relative to the matched branch.  This
is the most literal meaning of a dynamical particle/hole defect available at
the Gaussian level.  A worldline description would have to track the evolving
orientation \(u_t\), not only the bits \(d_t\).

\section{How a click influences the next controller}
\label{sec:propagation}

Assume \(|g|<1\), and let the next measured orbital be \(\psi\), with target
sign \(\tau'\).  Its
mismatch propensity after the present event is
\begin{equation}
 q'_{d}=\frac{1-\tau'\psi^\dagger\Phi_d(G)\psi}{2}.
 \label{eq:next-q-definition}
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{align}
 q'_B&=\frac{1-\tau'\psi^\dagger B(G)\psi}{2},
 \nonumber\\
 w_{\psi\leftarrow\chi}(G)
 &=\frac{\psi^\dagger R(G)\psi}{1-g^2}
 =\frac{|\psi^\dagger QG\chi|^2}{1-g^2}.
 \label{eq:propagation-weight}
\end{align}
The bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:R-bound} implies \(0\leq w\leq1\).  Direct
substitution gives the exact propagation law
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 q'_d=q'_B-\tau\tau'[d-q_s(G)]w_{\psi\leftarrow\chi}(G).}
 \label{eq:next-defect-rule}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 q'_1-q'_0=-\tau\tau'w_{\psi\leftarrow\chi}(G).
 \label{eq:branching-sign}
\end{equation}
A mismatch suppresses the next mismatch for equal target signs and enhances it
for opposite target signs.  For a pure incoming state,
\(w=|\langle\psi|u\rangle|^2\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:next-defect-rule} looks
like a two-state branching rule, but its rate depends on the full incoming
covariance through both \(u\) and \(g\).  After marginalizing over \(G\), the
click string is therefore generally non-Markovian.

\subsection{A geometric frustration floor}

The nonorthogonality of opposite-target orbitals provides an outcome-independent
source of future clicks.

\begin{proposition}[Consecutive opposite-target bound]
Suppose an update has just reset \(\chi\) to sign \(\tau\), so that
\(G\chi=\tau\chi\).  Let the immediately following controller orbital
\(\psi\) have target \(\tau'=-\tau\).  Then
\begin{equation}
 q_{\psi,\tau'}(G)\geq|\langle\chi|\psi\rangle|^2.
 \label{eq:overlap-floor}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:geometric-bound}.  This bound suggests a
direct controller diagnostic: construct the directed conflict graph of the
actual chronological word, weight an edge \(j\to k\) by
\(|\langle\chi_j|\chi_k\rangle|^2\) when \(\tau_j\tau_k=-1\), and compare its
large weights with the observed mismatch-click profile.  The theorem supplies
a lower bound for consecutive updates; it does not assert that every
nonconsecutive pair contributes additively.

A covariance with zero mismatch propensity for every controller would have to
satisfy
\begin{equation}
 G_\star\chi_j=\tau_j\chi_j
 \quad\text{for every }j.
 \label{eq:common-dark-condition}
\end{equation}
Since eigenvectors of a Hermitian matrix with eigenvalues \(+1\) and \(-1\)
are orthogonal, Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-dark-condition} is impossible whenever
two nonorthogonal controller modes carry opposite targets.  Thus cross-target
overlap is a precise obstruction to a common zero-click state.  Whether this
obstruction is present, and where it is localized, must be evaluated for the
actual finite-support controller; it is not inferred here from topology alone.

\section[Positive covariance-manifold transfer]
{Exact positive transfer on the covariance manifold}
\label{sec:covariance-transfer}

Fix the complete chronological schedule
\((\chi_t,\tau_t)_{t=1}^{D}\).  The covariance is a classical hidden state and
the mismatch click is its binary emission:
\begin{equation}
 d_t\sim p_{t,d}(G_{t-1}),
 \qquad
 G_t=\Phi_{t,d_t}(G_{t-1}).
 \label{eq:hidden-Markov-process}
\end{equation}
The corresponding Markov kernel is
\begin{equation}
 \cL_{t,1}(G',G)
 =\sum_{d=0,1}p_{t,d}(G)
 \delta\!\left(G'-\Phi_{t,d}(G)\right).
 \label{eq:physical-PF-kernel}
\end{equation}
It is normalized in \(G'\) and defines an exact Perron--Frobenius pushforward
on probability measures over the admissible covariance manifold \(\cX\).
The dual Koopman operator acts on an observable \(f(G)\) as
\begin{equation}
 (\cK_t f)(G)
 =\sum_{d=0,1}p_{t,d}(G)f\!\left(\Phi_{t,d}(G)\right).
 \label{eq:Koopman-map}
\end{equation}

The complete click-string probability is
\begin{equation}
 p[\bm d]
 =\prod_{t=1}^{D}p_{t,d_t}\!\left(G_{t-1}[d_1,\ldots,d_{t-1}]\right).
 \label{eq:defect-string-probability}
\end{equation}
This already defines a positive classical statistical weight.  A particularly
transparent Ising form follows from
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_t=(-1)^{d_t}=\eta_{m_t}\tau_t,
 \qquad
 h_t=\operatorname{arctanh}(\tau_t g_t).
 \label{eq:ising-variables}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 p(d_t|G_{t-1})
 =\frac{e^{h_t\sigma_t}}{2\cosh h_t},
 \label{eq:causal-Ising-weight}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 -\log p[\bm d]
 =\sum_t\left\{\log[2\cosh h_t[\bm d_{<t}]]
 -h_t[\bm d_{<t}]\sigma_t\right\}.
 \label{eq:causal-Ising-action}
\end{equation}
It is literally an Ising history in a causal self-consistent field.  The price
of eliminating \(G\) is that \(h_t\) is a nonlinear functional of all earlier
spins.  The band signs \(\tau_t\) form a fixed, generally staggered spacetime
background.

For real \(n>0\), define the positive replica-tilted covariance kernel
\begin{equation}
 \cL_{t,n}(G',G)
 =\sum_{d=0,1}p_{t,d}(G)^n
 \delta\!\left(G'-\Phi_{t,d}(G)\right).
 \label{eq:replica-PF-kernel}
\end{equation}
Its total mass after the full word is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \cZ_n=\langle 1|\cL_{D,n}\cdots\cL_{1,n}|G_0\rangle
 =\sum_{\bm d}p[\bm d]^n.}
 \label{eq:PF-Zn}
\end{equation}
At \(n=1\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:replica-PF-kernel} is stochastic.  At
\(n\neq1\), it is positive but not mass preserving.  Normalizing
\(p_d(G)^n\) independently at each time does \emph{not} produce the global
replica-biased process, because the future weight depends on the present
covariance.  A normalized dynamics requires the usual backward harmonic
function or Doob transform of the complete tilted transfer.

For a causal record the Shannon entropy also obeys the exact chain rule
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm rec}
 =-\left.\partial_n\log\cZ_n\right|_{n=1}
 =\sum_t\E\!\left[h_2(q_t)\right],
 \label{eq:shannon-chain-rule}
\end{equation}
where \(h_2(q)=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q)\).  Since \(h_2\) is nonlinear,
\(H_{\rm rec}\) requires the distribution of the propensities, not merely
their mean.

\section{What closes after physical Born averaging}
\label{sec:mean-and-fluctuation}

The innovation form makes the exceptional simplicity of \(n=1\) explicit:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\E_d[\Phi_d(G)|G]=B(G)=QGQ+\tau P.}
 \label{eq:one-copy-closure}
\end{equation}
The averaged density-matrix channel is the fermionic replacement channel
\begin{equation}
 \cE_s(\rho)
 =\sum_dK_{d|s}\rho K_{d|s}^\dagger
 =\proj{s}_\chi\widehat\otimes\Tr_\chi\rho,
 \label{eq:replacement-channel}
\end{equation}
and its covariance action is Eq.~\eqref{eq:one-copy-closure}.  Because \(B\)
is affine,
\begin{equation}
 \avg G_t=Q_t\avg G_{t-1}Q_t+\tau_tP_t
 \label{eq:mean-covariance-recursion}
\end{equation}
for a fixed schedule.  In particular, the mean mismatch rate is one-copy
closed:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \E d_t
 =\frac{1-\tau_t\chi_t^\dagger\avg G_{t-1}\chi_t}{2}.}
 \label{eq:mean-defect-rate}
\end{equation}
The mean signed correction rate is
\begin{equation}
 \E(\tau_td_t)
 =\frac{\tau_t-\chi_t^\dagger\avg G_{t-1}\chi_t}{2}
 =\frac{\Tr\avg G_t-\Tr\avg G_{t-1}}{2}.
 \label{eq:mean-signed-rate}
\end{equation}
Hence its sum over a stationary complete cycle vanishes, even though the
unsigned activity can remain finite.

\subsection{Exact covariance-fluctuation equation}

The conditional second moment is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \E_d[\Phi_d(G)^{\otimes2}|G]
 &=B(G)^{\otimes2}\\
 &\quad+\frac{R(G)^{\otimes2}}{1-g^2}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:two-copy-covariance-identity}
\end{equation}
Thus a mismatch click is a rank-at-most-one shot-noise source.  To write a
positive matrix covariance, use column-stacking vectorization of one-body
operators and define
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_t
 =\E\!\left[
 |\delta G_t\rangle\!\rangle
 \langle\!\langle\delta G_t|
 \right],
 \qquad
 \delta G_t=G_t-\avg G_t.
 \label{eq:Sigma-definition}
\end{equation}
Let \(\mathcal A_t(H)=Q_tHQ_t\) and
\(\widehat{\mathcal A}_t=Q_t^*\otimes Q_t\).  Since the innovation has zero
conditional mean, all cross terms with the past vanish and one obtains
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \Sigma_t
 =\widehat{\mathcal A}_t\Sigma_{t-1}
  \widehat{\mathcal A}_t^\dagger
 +\E\!\left[
 \frac{|R_t\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle R_t|}{1-g_t^2}
 \right].}
 \label{eq:exact-Sigma-update}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-Sigma-update} is the exact averaged replica-like
update sought for density fluctuations.  It is not a finite closure: the
source is quartic and rational in the random incoming covariance.  On any
interior domain \(|g|\leq1-\epsilon\), expanding
\((1-g^2)^{-1}=\sum_{k\geq0}g^{2k}\) displays dependence on moments of
arbitrarily high order.  The expansion is not uniform at the endpoints, but
the source itself has the regular zero limit because
\(\|R\|_{\rm HS}^2/(1-g^2)\leq1-g^2\).  Special twirls or large-flavor limits
may close this hierarchy, but generic Gaussian purity does not.

For a region \(A\), the within-trajectory quantum charge variance is
\begin{equation}
 F_{A,\xi}^{(q)}
 =\tr[C_{\xi,A}(\Id_A-C_{\xi,A})]
 =\frac{|A|-\tr G_{\xi,A}^2}{4}.
 \label{eq:charge-variance-G}
\end{equation}
It is therefore fixed by a contraction of the second covariance moment, not
by \(\avg G\).  Likewise, for \(i\neq j\),
\begin{equation}
 \avg{\langle\delta n_i\delta n_j\rangle_\xi}
 =-\avg{|C_{\xi,ij}|^2}.
 \label{eq:density-correlation-square}
\end{equation}
The distinction between \(\avg{|C_{ij}|^2}\) and
\(|\avg C_{ij}|^2\) is precisely the trajectory-fluctuation contribution.
Charge cumulants and free-fermion fluctuation--entanglement relations provide
the broader context for these observables~\cite{KlichLevitov2009,SongFlindtRachel2011}.

\section[Tilted channel for click activity]
{A Gaussian-closed tilted channel for click activity}
\label{sec:activity-tilt}

The full counting statistics of the mismatch clicks has a simpler exact
construction than same-record nonlinear state observables.  Give a click a
fugacity \(x\) and define
\begin{equation}
 \cE_{s,x}(\rho)
 =\sum_{d=0,1}x^dK_{d|s}\rho K_{d|s}^\dagger.
 \label{eq:tilted-channel-definition}
\end{equation}
In the Fock factorization associated with \(\chi\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \cE_{s,x}(\rho)
 &=\proj{s}_\chi\widehat\otimes
 \Tr_\chi\!\left[W_s(x)\rho\right],\\
 W_s(x)&=\Pi_s+x\Pi_{1-s}=x^{\Pi_{1-s}}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:tilted-block-identity}
\end{equation}
For \(x>0\), \(W_s(x)\) is the exponential of a quadratic number operator.
Products and partial traces of fermionic Gaussian operators remain Gaussian;
therefore the normalized version of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tilted-channel-definition}
closes exactly on a covariance, despite being non-trace-preserving before
normalization.

For a normalized incoming Gaussian state, the one-step normalization is
\begin{equation}
 z_x(G)=p_0(G)+xp_1(G)
 =\frac{1+\tau g+x(1-\tau g)}{2}.
 \label{eq:tilted-normalization}
\end{equation}
The normalized tilted covariance is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \Psi_{s,x}(G)
 =B(G)+\tau
 \frac{x-1}{1+\tau g+x(1-\tau g)}R(G).}
 \label{eq:tilted-covariance-map}
\end{equation}
The limits provide useful checks:
\(x=1\) gives the unconditional mean map, \(x=0\) gives the matched branch,
and \(x\to\infty\) gives the mismatch branch, provided the selected branch
has nonzero Born weight.  A normalized zero-probability branch is undefined.

For event-dependent sources \(x_t\), initialize \(Z_0=1\) and the physical
\(G_0\), then iterate
\begin{equation}
 Z_t=Z_{t-1}z_{x_t}(G_{t-1}^{\bm x}),
 \qquad
 G_t^{\bm x}=\Psi_{s_t,x_t}(G_{t-1}^{\bm x}).
 \label{eq:tilted-gaussian-recursion}
\end{equation}
This gives exactly
\begin{equation}
 Z_D(\bm x)=\sum_{\bm d}p[\bm d]\prod_tx_t^{d_t}.
 \label{eq:defect-generating-function}
\end{equation}
Taking \(x_t=e^{\lambda_t}\) generates all multitime click cumulants.  Taking
\(x_t=e^{\lambda+\ii\theta\tau_t}\) jointly counts unsigned activity and
signed correction charge.  This is the natural dynamical-activity ensemble of
quantum-trajectory large-deviation theory~\cite{GarrahanLesanovsky2010,Landi2024}.
For complex sources the tilted Gaussian operator and its covariance are
generally complex; the normalized recursion is an analytic continuation valid
away from zeros of the intermediate normalization, while the unnormalized
generating polynomial remains the primary object.
For a periodically repeated autonomous cycle, the leading eigenvalue of the
unnormalized cycle superoperator gives the scaled cumulant-generating
function.  A randomly changing schedule requires a separate quenched or
annealed convention.

\section{Replicated density transfer and marked observables}
\label{sec:replica-transfer}

The activity tilt weights a physical record by \(p_\xi\).  Record R\'enyi sums
and squared conditional observables require shared-record replicas.  Define
the unnormalized branch state
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\rho_\xi=K_\xi\rho_0K_\xi^\dagger,
 \qquad
 p_\xi=\Tr\widetilde\rho_\xi,
 \qquad
 \rho_\xi=\frac{\widetilde\rho_\xi}{p_\xi}.
 \label{eq:unnormalized-branch}
\end{equation}
For integer \(n\), introduce
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_n(\bm x)
 =\sum_\xi\left(\prod_tx_t^{d_t(\xi)}\right)
 \widetilde\rho_\xi^{\otimes n}.
 \label{eq:Omega-n-definition}
\end{equation}
Here replica Hilbert spaces are distinguishable and use the ordinary tensor
product; \(\widehat\otimes\) is reserved for fermionic mode factorizations
within one replica.
Let \(\cK_{t,d}(X)=K_{t,d}XK_{t,d}^\dagger\).  The exact local replicated
update is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \cT_{t}^{(n)}(x_t)
 =\cK_{t,0}^{\otimes n}+x_t\cK_{t,1}^{\otimes n},
 \qquad
 \Omega_{n,t}=\cT_t^{(n)}(x_t)\Omega_{n,t-1}.}
 \label{eq:replicated-local-transfer}
\end{equation}
The same click \(d_t\) is shared by every replica.  Summing the outcome
independently in each replica would instead construct powers of the
unconditional state and would erase the same-record correlations.  The trace
is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \cZ_n(\bm x)=\Tr\Omega_n(\bm x)
 =\sum_\xi p_\xi^n\prod_tx_t^{d_t(\xi)}.}
 \label{eq:replicated-partition-function}
\end{equation}
At \(\bm x=1\), this is the record partition function used in transfer-matrix
studies of monitored criticality~\cite{JianYouVasseur2020,BaoChoiAltman2020,
Zabalo2022}.  In vectorized form, each event is a local tensor
\begin{equation}
 T_t^{(n)}(x)
 =\sum_{d=0,1}x^d
 \left(K_{t,d}^*\otimes K_{t,d}\right)^{\otimes n}.
 \label{eq:vectorized-replica-tensor}
\end{equation}
Thus \(d_t\) is a spacetime Ising variable coupled to replicated fermions.
Integrating out the fermions gives the positive weight \(p[\bm d]^n\), but
generically produces a nonlocal many-body action for \(\bm d\).

The special cancellation at \(n=1\) can be seen locally for one normalized
incoming Gaussian state.  Let
\(a=p_0=(1+\tau g)/2\) and \(b=p_1=(1-\tau g)/2\).  Then
\begin{align}
 \sum_dp_d^n\Phi_d
 &=(a^n+b^n)B
 +\frac{\tau}{2}(b^{n-1}-a^{n-1})R,
 \label{eq:n-local-first}\\
 \sum_dp_d^n\Phi_d^{\otimes2}
 &=(a^n+b^n)B^{\otimes2}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad
 +\frac{\tau}{2}(b^{n-1}-a^{n-1})
 (B\otimes R+R\otimes B)
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad+\frac14(a^{n-2}+b^{n-2})R^{\otimes2}.
 \label{eq:n-local-second}
\end{align}
Only at \(n=1\) does the first-moment \(R\) term vanish.  These are local
conditional sums, not an update rule for a general correlated
\(\Omega_{n,t-1}\) produced by earlier shared outcomes; future tilted weights
still enter the normalized global \(n\)-ensemble.  When \(a\) or \(b\)
vanishes, the complete Born-weighted expressions---not their individual
negative-power factors---are taken to the endpoint.

\subsection{Two marked replicas}

Set \(\bm x=\mathbf{1}\) in this subsection and abbreviate
\(\Omega_n=\Omega_n(\mathbf{1})\).
For integer \(n\geq k\), insert observables on \(k\) distinguished replicas
and leave the remaining replicas unmarked.  One obtains
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Tr[(O_1^{(1)}\cdots O_k^{(k)})\Omega_n]}{\cZ_n}
 =\frac{\sum_\xi p_\xi^n\prod_{a=1}^{k}\langle O_a\rangle_\xi}
 {\sum_\xi p_\xi^n}.
 \label{eq:marked-replica-master}
\end{equation}
For a finite record space, the right-hand side defines a canonical real-\(n\)
function near \(n=1\).  The replica prescription recovers the physical Born
ensemble by continuing the integer-replica transfer to \(n\to1\).  In
particular,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \avg{|C_{\xi,ij}|^2}
 =\lim_{n\to1}
 \frac{\Tr[(c_i^\dagger c_j)^{(1)}
 (c_j^\dagger c_i)^{(2)}\Omega_n]}{\cZ_n}.}
 \label{eq:marked-squared-correlator}
\end{equation}
The within-trajectory quantum charge variance is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \avg{F_{A,\xi}^{(q)}}
 =\lim_{n\to1}
 \frac{\Tr\!\left[
 \bigl((N_A^2)^{(1)}-N_A^{(1)}N_A^{(2)}\bigr)\Omega_n
 \right]}{\cZ_n}.}
 \label{eq:marked-charge-variance}
\end{equation}
Likewise,
\begin{equation}
 \avg{\langle\delta n_i\delta n_j\rangle_\xi}
 =\lim_{n\to1}
 \frac{\Tr\!\left[
 \bigl((n_in_j)^{(1)}-n_i^{(1)}n_j^{(2)}\bigr)\Omega_n
 \right]}{\cZ_n}.
 \label{eq:marked-density-correlation}
\end{equation}
What is formal is reconstructing that continuation from integer-replica data,
especially after taking a large-depth or thermodynamic limit; a transfer
spectrum, field theory, or independent estimator must fix it.  Also note that
\(\Omega_2\) carries \(p_\xi^2\), not \(p_\xi\): simply simulating two replicas
at \(n=2\) does not directly give the physical Born-averaged squared
correlator.

\section{Why the O'Dea classical automaton does not transfer directly}
\label{sec:odea-comparison}

O'Dea, Morningstar, Gopalakrishnan, and Khemani obtain a classical stochastic
process for diagonal elements of the trajectory-averaged density matrix in
their adaptive spin circuits~\cite{ODeaMorningstarGopalakrishnan2024}.  Their
doubled Hilbert space is the bra/ket vectorization of an \(n=1\) channel, not
the same-record replica in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Omega-n-definition}.  A constrained
Haar average preserves a common diagonal algebra and kills the off-diagonal
block after a unitary layer.  The surviving populations are therefore genuine
classical bitstrings whose down spins are time-slice configuration defects
carried between updates.  In their
cluster-state construction, a finite-depth Clifford transformation first maps
commuting stabilizer violations to onsite bits.

There is a concise general criterion.  Let \(\Delta\) dephase in a proposed
product basis and let \(\cE\) be a quantum channel.  Its diagonal populations
obey an autonomous classical update exactly when
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Delta\cE=\Delta\cE\Delta.}
 \label{eq:classical-criterion}
\end{equation}
If \(\{K_a\}\) are Kraus operators, Eq.~\eqref{eq:classical-criterion} is
equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 \sum_a(K_a)_{xy}(K_a)_{xz}^*=0
 \quad\text{for every }x\text{ and }y\neq z.
 \label{eq:coherence-feed-criterion}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 T_{xy}=\sum_a|(K_a)_{xy}|^2\geq0,
 \qquad \sum_xT_{xy}=1
 \label{eq:classical-transition-matrix}
\end{equation}
is a stochastic matrix.  A record-resolved classical instrument needs the
stronger condition \(\Delta\cE_d=\Delta\cE_d\Delta\) for every outcome \(d\).

The natural target-violation operators of the fermion controller are
\begin{equation}
 \widehat D_j
 =\frac{\Id-\tau_j(2n_{\chi_j}-\Id)}{2}.
 \label{eq:violation-operators}
\end{equation}
They do not define simultaneous bits because
\begin{equation}
 [n_{\chi_j},n_{\chi_k}]
 =c^\dagger[P_j,P_k]c.
 \label{eq:number-commutator}
\end{equation}
Two rank-one projectors commute only when their orbitals are orthogonal or
collinear.  The finite-support controller is built from overlapping,
nonorthogonal modes, so successive resets are classical in different bases.
Coherences in one basis feed populations in the next.

A two-mode counterexample makes the problem explicit.  Let
\begin{equation}
 \chi=\frac{a+b}{\sqrt2},
 \qquad
 \varphi=\frac{a-b}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:two-mode-counterexample}
\end{equation}
The one-particle states \(\proj{\chi}\) and \(\proj{\varphi}\) have the same
diagonal in the site basis.  Resetting \(\chi\) empty sends the first state to
vacuum but leaves the second state occupied in \(\varphi\).  Therefore the
site-basis diagonal does not determine the future diagonal.  A delocalized
exact band basis may diagonalize the target, but it forfeits the local
cellular-automaton structure.  For a nonzero Chern band, the absence of a
translation-covariant exponentially localized orthonormal Wannier basis makes
this locality--diagonality tension structural; it is not by itself a no-go
theorem for every possible invariant diagonal algebra.

The conclusion is narrower than a no-go theorem for every conceivable
classical representation.  It rules out the direct O'Dea reduction to a local
common-basis bitstring for the generic overlapping controller.  The exact
positive covariance-manifold transfer in Eq.~\eqref{eq:replica-PF-kernel}
remains valid.

\section{Toward an effective wall defect theory}
\label{sec:effective-theory}

This section deliberately changes status from exact identities to candidate
approximations.  The purpose is to identify calculations that could turn the
formal defect field into a useful low-energy theory.

\subsection{Narrow-distribution closure}

The simplest closure replaces the nonlinear noise source in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-Sigma-update} by its value on the deterministic mean orbit.
Here and below,
\begin{equation}
 \bar g_t=\chi_t^\dagger\bar G_{t-1}\chi_t,
 \qquad
 \bar R_t=R_t(\bar G_{t-1}).
 \label{eq:mean-orbit-local-data}
\end{equation}
Thus the candidate closure is
\begin{equation}
 \E\!\left[
 \frac{|R_t\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle R_t|}{1-g_t^2}
 \right]
 \simeq
 \frac{|\bar R_t\rangle\!\rangle
 \langle\!\langle\bar R_t|}{1-\bar g_t^2}.
 \label{eq:narrow-source-closure}
\end{equation}
The result is a closed discrete Lyapunov equation for \(\Sigma_t\).  It is not
controlled merely because the incoming state is Gaussian.  It becomes
plausible when the trajectory distribution is demonstrably narrow, or in a
modified many-flavor model with a saddle suppressing collective fluctuations.
The approximation can be tested directly by measuring both sides of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:narrow-source-closure}.

\subsection[Retarded click interaction]
{Retarded click interaction from linearized covariance response}

Assume \(|\bar g_t|<1\) and define
\(\bar W_t=\bar R_t/(1-\bar g_t^2)\).  We now make a distinct weak-noise
closure: freeze the nonlinear kick matrix \(W_t(G)=R_t(G)/(1-g_t^2)\) on the
mean orbit and neglect its derivative.  Retaining the first-order state
dependence of the propensity gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J_t(H)
 =Q_tHQ_t+\bar W_t\bigl(\chi_t^\dagger H\chi_t\bigr),
 \quad
 \bar q_t=\frac{1-\tau_t\bar g_t}{2}.
 \label{eq:linearized-definitions}
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\(d_t-q_t=d_t-\bar q_t
+(\tau_t/2)\chi_t^\dagger\delta G_{t-1}\chi_t\), so the fluctuation recursion
in this closure is
\begin{equation}
 \delta G_t\simeq\mathcal J_t(\delta G_{t-1})
 +2\tau_t(d_t-\bar q_t)\bar W_t.
 \label{eq:linearized-shot-noise}
\end{equation}
For a single Bernoulli click, the discarded derivative of \(W_t\) is not
parametrically small; Eq.~\eqref{eq:linearized-shot-noise} becomes controlled
only if coarse graining, weak overlap, or a large-flavor construction supplies
a small-noise parameter.

Let \(\mathcal J_{t-1:u+1}\) denote the chronological product propagating a
matrix from just after event \(u\) to just before event \(t\), and define
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_{tu}
 =\chi_t^\dagger
 \mathcal J_{t-1:u+1}(\bar W_u)\chi_t\geq0.
 \label{eq:retarded-kernel}
\end{equation}
The inequality follows because \(\mathcal J_t\) maps positive matrices to
positive matrices.  Within the stated closure, the first-order response is
\begin{equation}
 q_t\simeq\bar q_t
 -\sum_{u<t}\tau_t\tau_u\Gamma_{tu}(d_u-\bar q_u).
 \label{eq:q-retarded-response}
\end{equation}
If \(\bar q_t\) stays bounded away from \(0\) and \(1\), and the induced
propensity shifts are small, expanding the Bernoulli log likelihood around
\(\bar q_t\) gives
\begin{align}
 \log p[\bm d]
 &\simeq\sum_t\left[d_t\log\bar q_t
 +(1-d_t)\log(1-\bar q_t)\right]
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad-\sum_{t>u}
 \frac{\tau_t\tau_u\Gamma_{tu}}
 {\bar q_t(1-\bar q_t)}
 (d_t-\bar q_t)(d_u-\bar q_u)+\cdots .
 \label{eq:retarded-click-action}
\end{align}
The complete-history quadratic form can be symmetrized algebraically over
unordered spacetime pairs.  Its ``retarded'' character refers instead to its
causal construction, temporal anisotropy, and generic lack of time-reversal
symmetry.  Near \(\bar q_t=0\) or \(1\), a separate rare-click expansion is
required.  Nonlinear kick dependence and partner-orientation fluctuations
generate higher-body terms.

Equation~\eqref{eq:retarded-click-action} suggests a concrete wall mechanism.
If the homogeneous linearized response map contracts rapidly in the bulk,
\(\Gamma_{tu}\) is short ranged there.  A near-marginal wall mode makes the
kernel long lived and possibly directional along the interface.  A defect
statistical mechanics could then emerge on the wall even though no local
click-only rule exists microscopically.  This is a conjecture until
\(\Gamma_{tu}\) is measured and shown to predict click correlations.

\subsection{Weak-overlap kinetics and deliberate twirls}

There is also a geometric weak-overlap expansion.  Suppose \(\chi_j\) has just
been reset to \(\tau_j\), and then the \emph{unconditional} reset of
\(\chi_k\) to \(\tau_k\) is applied.  With
\(\alpha=\langle\chi_k|\chi_j\rangle\) and
\(h=\chi_k^\dagger G\chi_k\), the mean re-created mismatch propensity at
\(j\) is
\begin{equation}
 q'_j
 =|\alpha|^2\left[1-\frac{\tau_j}{2}(h+\tau_k)\right].
 \label{eq:recontamination-rate}
\end{equation}
Thus nonorthogonality can re-create mismatch propensity at order
\(|\alpha|^2\), while the
coefficient still remembers the hidden covariance.  This identity motivates a
kinetic approximation but also displays exactly why overlap weights alone do
not close it.

An exact finite classical process can be manufactured by imposing a common
dephasing channel, an appropriate Haar twirl, or a commuting/orthogonal
controller limit.  Those are modified models.  They may destroy the coherent
class-A wall and must not be presented as derivations of the original circuit.
A large-flavor generalization is another possible route: replica interactions
can admit a saddle and organize corrections in inverse flavor number, but
independent uncoupled copies do not by themselves control the single-flavor
problem.

\section{Concrete diagnostics and falsification tests}
\label{sec:tests}

The defect proposal becomes useful only if it produces tests beyond a relabeling
of the measurement record.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Exact small-system enumeration.}  Enumerate every click string for
 small Fock-space systems.  Compare \(\cZ_n\), the activity generating
 function, and integer-replica insertions with
 Eqs.~\eqref{eq:defect-generating-function} and
 \eqref{eq:replicated-partition-function}.
 \item \emph{Controller conflict graph.}  Measure
 \([P_j,P_k]\), cross-target overlaps, and the directed weights relevant to
 the chronological BPJ word.  Test the floor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-floor}
 event by event.
 \item \emph{Partner-mode propagation.}  On pure trajectories, construct
 \(u_t\) and test Eq.~\eqref{eq:next-defect-rule} without fitted parameters.
 This directly asks whether click correlations follow the state-dependent
 partner overlap.
 \item \emph{Exact fluctuation source.}  Measure the source term in
 Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-Sigma-update} and compare it with the mean-orbit closure
 in Eq.~\eqref{eq:narrow-source-closure}.  This quantifies rather than assumes
 moment closure.
 \item \emph{Retarded kernel.}  Compute \(\Gamma_{tu}\) from the linearized
 response map and compare Eq.~\eqref{eq:q-retarded-response} with conditional
 click rates.  Separate wall-on, wall-off, and homogeneous controls.
 \item \emph{Dynamical activity.}  Use the exact tilted Gaussian recursion to
 obtain the activity SCGF, susceptibility, and signed-charge cumulants.  Look
 for wall-excess finite-size structure only after subtracting matched bulk
 update counts.
 \item \emph{Replica fluctuation check.}  Reconstruct the full second moment
 \(M=\Sigma+|\bar G\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\bar G|\), and compare its
 contractions with direct trajectory estimates of \(\avg{|C_{ij}|^2}\) and
 \(\avg{F_A^{(q)}}\).  Keep these distinct from quantities evaluated on
 \(\avg C\).
\end{enumerate}

Directed-percolation language is not justified by the existence of a binary
click alone.  It would require a genuine absorbing configuration, a closed
short-range scalar defect kinetics, and the absence of a relevant conservation
law or symmetry.  The generic compact noncommuting controller may instead have
intrinsic mismatch activity.  Its signed activity obeys the exact charge
accounting in Eq.~\eqref{eq:charge-accounting} and has zero mean over a
stationary cycle, but this is not itself a conservation law for the reduced
system or the click kinetics.  The appropriate starting hypothesis is
therefore a driven shot-noise field coupled to slow wall covariance modes, not
directed percolation by default.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The mismatch-click variable does organize the physics, but in a more nuanced
way than a classical occupation defect.  At one event it is an exact binary
innovation.  In a pure trajectory it flips one state-dependent partner mode,
giving a precise particle/hole interpretation and exact signed-charge
accounting.  Across events it is emitted by a hidden Markov process whose state
is the covariance matrix.  The corresponding positive transfer operator
already supplies a legitimate classical statistical mechanics, while the
replicated density transfer supplies a local fermionic tensor-network version.

The one-copy Born average is unusually simple because the nonlinear recoil
cancels between the two branches.  That cancellation does not survive generic
replica tilts or same-record nonlinear observables.  The exact fluctuation
equation identifies the missing piece as rank-at-most-one covariance shot
noise.  This
is why the averaged covariance can look smooth or gapped while squared
trajectory correlators retain substantial wall structure.

The comparison with O'Dea \emph{et al.} isolates the structural question.
Their averaged dynamics preserves a common diagonal defect algebra, so a local
classical cellular automaton is exact.  Here the compact class-A controller
uses overlapping noncommuting orbitals.  A local common diagonal algebra is not
available generically, and the correction event is immediately healed at its
own orbital.  The plausible emergent theory is consequently a wall-localized
activity field with retarded interactions mediated by covariance modes, or an
interacting replicated fermion theory with a defect insertion, rather than a
microscopic reaction--diffusion process assumed from the outset.

The next decisive calculation is therefore not to fit an arbitrary classical
model to click rasters.  It is to evaluate the controller conflict graph, the
exact partner-mode propagation weights, the nonlinear fluctuation source, and
the linearized response Green function on the same wall and wall-off
geometries.  Those
objects determine whether the retarded action truncates to a useful local wall
theory or whether the full replicated fermionic transfer must be retained.

 K_{d|s}\rho K_{d|s}^\dagger

 \label{eq:app-block-branch}

\section{Proof of the consecutive overlap bound}
\label{app:geometric-bound}

Suppose \(G\chi=\tau\chi\) and decompose
\begin{equation}
 \psi=\alpha\chi+w,
 \qquad \langle\chi|w\rangle=0,
 \qquad \|w\|^2=1-|\alpha|^2.
 \label{eq:app-psi-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Hermiticity gives \(\langle\chi|Gw\rangle=0\), so
\begin{equation}
 \psi^\dagger G\psi
 =\tau|\alpha|^2+w^\dagger Gw.
 \label{eq:app-gpsi}
\end{equation}
For \(\tau'=-\tau\) and \(-\Id\leq G\leq\Id\),
\begin{equation}
 \tau'\psi^\dagger G\psi
 \leq-|\alpha|^2+(1-|\alpha|^2)
 =1-2|\alpha|^2.
 \label{eq:app-alignment-bound}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 q_{\psi,\tau'}(G)
 =\frac{1-\tau'\psi^\dagger G\psi}{2}
 \geq|\alpha|^2,
 \end{equation}
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-floor}.

For two marks,
\begin{align}
 \Tr[O_1^{(1)}O_2^{(2)}\Omega_n]
 &=\sum_\xi
 \Tr(O_1\widetilde\rho_\xi)
 \Tr(O_2\widetilde\rho_\xi)
 (\Tr\widetilde\rho_\xi)^{n-2}
 \nonumber\\
 &=\sum_\xi p_\xi^n
 \langle O_1\rangle_\xi\langle O_2\rangle_\xi.
 \label{eq:app-two-marks}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:marked-squared-correlator}--
\eqref{eq:marked-density-correlation} follow by choosing the marked
operators and continuing the normalized ratio to \(n=1\).
