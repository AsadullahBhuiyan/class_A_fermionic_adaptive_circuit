\title{Entangling Capacity of a Local Adaptive Fermion Circuit}
\author{Class-A Fermionic Adaptive-Circuit Project}
\noaffiliation
\date{Internal technical note --- August 29, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Local measurements and feedforward can evade correlation-spreading bounds that
constrain unitary dynamics, but they do not provide unlimited entangling
capacity.  We give a branchwise proof that a local adaptive circuit increases
the Hartley entropy across a spatial cut by at most the sum of the logarithmic
operator-Schmidt ranks of the operations crossing that cut.  For bounded-range
circuit layers this reproduces the Lu--Lessa--Kim--Hsieh bound
$\Delta S_0=O(D_{\rm circ}|\partial A|)$.  We then specialize the result to a
postselection-free class-A fermion circuit with overcomplete-Wannier occupation
measurements and fresh-ancilla fSWAP correction.  Its four conditional branches
have operator-Schmidt rank at most four or two, yielding an explicit
trajectory-record bound.  Applied to a critical interface embedded in two
dimensions, the result requires $D_{\rm circ}N_x=\Omega(\log N_y)$ rather than
$D_{\rm circ}=\Omega(\log N_y)$.  The extra transverse boundary is therefore a
resource allowed by the theorem, not a violation of it.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Question and result}

Adaptive circuits combine local quantum operations with classical control
conditioned on earlier measurement outcomes.  They can prepare long-range
entangled states faster than local unitary circuits because the retained
classical record invalidates a unitary Lieb--Robinson argument.  Nevertheless,
Lu, Lessa, Kim, and Hsieh proved that their entanglement production remains
bounded~\cite{LuLessaKimHsieh2022}.  The purpose of this note is to state that
bound with all branchwise assumptions visible and apply it to the local
fermionic controller of Bhuiyan, Pan, and Jian (BPJ)~\cite{BhuiyanPanJian2026}.

Two points will be central.  First, the bound concerns the Hartley entropy, or
logarithm of Schmidt rank.  It survives projective measurements, singular
conditional operators, and arbitrary classical feedforward.  Second, its
geometric factor is the boundary of the subsystem in the dimension in which
the circuit is local.  A one-dimensional critical interface embedded in a
two-dimensional circuit therefore does not inherit the $O(1)$ cut boundary of
an intrinsically one-dimensional preparation problem.  Figure~\ref{fig:cuts}
contrasts the abstract cut with the strip used for the interface observable.

\section{Branchwise entangling-capacity theorem}

Fix a spatial bipartition of a finite fermionic lattice,
$\mathcal H=\mathcal H_A\widehat\otimes\mathcal H_{\Abar}$, where
$\widehat\otimes$ denotes the graded tensor product.  For a pure state
$\ketket{\psi}$, let $\chi_A(\psi)$ be its Schmidt rank across this cut and
define its Hartley entropy by
\begin{equation}
 S_0(A;\psi)=\log\chi_A(\psi).
 \label{eq:S0}
\end{equation}
All logarithms are natural.  Once a graded factorization is fixed, define the
operator-Schmidt rank $r(\hat O)$ as the smallest $r$ for which
\begin{equation}
 \hat O=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}
 \sigma_\alpha\hat O^A_\alpha\otimes\hat O^{\Abar}_\alpha .
 \label{eq:operator-schmidt}
\end{equation}
Parity strings required by an odd fermionic operator are included in one of
the two factors.

For a fixed adaptive record
$\boldsymbol m=(m_1,\ldots,m_M)$, the unnormalized branch state is
\begin{equation}
 \ketket{\widetilde\psi_{\boldsymbol m}}
 =\hat O_M(\boldsymbol m_{<M})\cdots
  \hat O_1\ketket{\psi_{\rm in}}.
 \label{eq:branch-word}
\end{equation}
The choice of $\hat O_j$ may depend arbitrarily on the preceding classical
record $\boldsymbol m_{<j}$.  We restrict attention to nonzero branches,
$\braketket{\widetilde\psi_{\boldsymbol m}}
 {\widetilde\psi_{\boldsymbol m}}>0$, so normalization is defined.

\begin{theorem}[Branchwise entangling capacity]
Let $\mathcal C_\partial(\boldsymbol m)$ be the operations in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch-word} whose support intersects both $A$ and $\Abar$, and
write $r_j=r(\hat O_j)$.  Every nonzero adaptive branch obeys
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 S_0^{\rm out}(A)-S_0^{\rm in}(A)
 \leq \sum_{j\in\mathcal C_\partial(\boldsymbol m)}\log r_j .}
 \label{eq:exact-bound}
\end{equation}
No invertibility or lower bound on the branch probability is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write an input of Schmidt rank $\chi$ as
\begin{equation}
 \ketket{\psi}=\sum_{i=1}^{\chi}\sqrt{p_i},
 \ketket{\phi_i}_A\otimes\ketket{\theta_i}_{\Abar}.
\end{equation}
Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:operator-schmidt} gives
\begin{equation}
 \hat O\ketket{\psi}
 =\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{\chi}
 \sigma_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_i},
 \hat O^A_\alpha\ketket{\phi_i}_A\otimes
 \hat O^{\Abar}_\alpha\ketket{\theta_i}_{\Abar},
 \label{eq:rank-product}
\end{equation}
where scalar coefficients have been absorbed into either factor except for
the displayed $\sigma_\alpha$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:rank-product} is a sum of
at most $r\chi$ product vectors, so the output Schmidt rank is no greater
than $r\chi$.  If $\hat O$ is supported entirely within $A$ or $\Abar$, it
has a one-term product representation and cannot increase $\chi$.  Applying
this statement successively along the realized word gives
$\chi_A^{\rm out}\leq\chi_A^{\rm in}
\prod_{j\in\mathcal C_\partial}r_j$.  Taking the logarithm proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-bound}.  Dividing a nonzero branch by the square root of
its Born probability is multiplication by a scalar and leaves its rank
unchanged.  The proof is therefore independent of the outcome-dependent
choice of later operators.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local depth bound]
Suppose a depth-$D_{\rm circ}$ circuit consists of nonoverlapping operations
in each layer, with fixed support diameter, fixed onsite Hilbert-space
dimension, and bounded lattice coordination.  Then a geometry-dependent
constant $\kappa$ independent of system size and depth exists such that
\begin{equation}
 S_0^{\rm out}(A)-S_0^{\rm in}(A)
 \leq \kappa D_{\rm circ}|\partial A|.
 \label{eq:Lu-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

Indeed, each layer has at most $\nu|\partial A|$ crossing operations and
each has $r_j\leq r_*$, with $\nu,r_*=O(1)$; hence
$\kappa=\nu\log r_*$.  For a pure product input,
$S_0^{\rm in}=0$, and monotonicity of R\'enyi entropies in their index gives
\begin{equation}
 S_n^{\rm out}(A)\leq S_0^{\rm out}(A)
 \leq \kappa D_{\rm circ}|\partial A|,
 \qquad n>0,
 \label{eq:renyi-bound}
\end{equation}
including the von Neumann limit $n\to1$.

The product-input qualification cannot be replaced by a bound on the initial
von Neumann entropy alone.  Let $f\subset A$ be a local flag and choose a
volume-law state $\ketket{\Phi_L}$ across the remaining cut, with its
$\Abar$ support orthogonal to a product state $\ketket{0}$.  The state
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{1-\epsilon}\ketket{0}_f\ketket{0}
 +\sqrt{\epsilon}\ketket{1}_f\ketket{\Phi_L}
 \label{eq:filter-example}
\end{equation}
has $S_1=H_2(\epsilon)+\epsilon S_1(\Phi_L)=O(1)$ when
$\epsilon=O(L^{-1})$, but projecting the flag onto $1$ produces
$\ketket{\Phi_L}$ with $S_1=\Theta(L)$.  Its initial Hartley entropy was
already extensive, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-bound} remains intact.  This is why
the robust statement is about Schmidt rank and why
Eq.~\eqref{eq:renyi-bound} uses a product input.

\section{Corrected fermionic occupation measurements}

We now specialize the theorem to one finite-range overcomplete-Wannier (OW)
controller mode in the BPJ circuit.  Suppressing its position, family, and
band labels, let
\begin{equation}
 \hat a_\chi\equiv\hat\chi_{\boldsymbol r,\nu,n}
 =\sum_{x\in X}\chi_x\hat c_x,
 \qquad \sum_{x\in X}|\chi_x|^2=1,
 \label{eq:ow-mode}
\end{equation}
where the fixed patch $X$ is obtained by truncating and renormalizing an OW
state.  The algebra is
$\{\hat c_x^\dagger,\hat c_y\}=\delta_{xy}$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \hat N_\chi=\hat a_\chi^\dagger\hat a_\chi,
 \qquad \hat\Pi_1=\hat N_\chi,
 \qquad \hat\Pi_0=\One-\hat N_\chi.
 \label{eq:projectors}
\end{equation}
The lower-band target is $s=1$ and the upper-band target is $s=0$.  Measuring
$\hat N_\chi$ gives $m\in\{0,1\}$, after which perfect correction has the
system Kraus operators
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &m=0&m=1\\ \hline
 s=0&\hat K_{0|0}=\hat\Pi_0&\hat K_{1|0}=\hat a_\chi\\[1mm]
 s=1&\hat K_{0|1}=\hat a_\chi^\dagger&\hat K_{1|1}=\hat\Pi_1
\end{array}.
\label{eq:kraus-table}
\end{equation}
They satisfy
\begin{align}
 \hat K_{m|s}^\dagger\hat K_{m|s}&=\hat\Pi_m,
 &\hat\Pi_s\hat K_{m|s}&=\hat K_{m|s},\nonumber\\
 \sum_m\hat K_{m|s}^\dagger\hat K_{m|s}&=\One.&&
 \label{eq:kraus-checks}
\end{align}
Thus the Born outcome remains random while every nonzero branch leaves the
measured mode at its target occupation.  On a mismatch, a colocated fresh
ancilla $\hat d$ prepared in $\ketket{s}_d$ supplies the dilation
\begin{equation}
 \fSWAP(\hat a_\chi,\hat d):
 \ketket{m}_\chi\ketket{s}_d
 \longmapsto\ketket{s}_\chi\ketket{m}_d .
 \label{eq:fswap}
\end{equation}
The outgoing ancilla is a known factorized mode and may be discarded; no
ancilla outcome is postselected.  Taking its appropriate matrix element
reproduces the odd Kraus operators in Eq.~\eqref{eq:kraus-table}.

To calculate the entangling capacity, split the OW mode across the cut.  In a
fixed graded factorization,
\begin{equation}
 \hat a_\chi=\hat a_A\otimes\One_{\Abar}
 +\hat P_A\otimes\hat a_{\Abar},
 \qquad \hat P_A=(-1)^{\hat F_A}.
 \label{eq:graded-split}
\end{equation}
This is already a two-term operator-Schmidt representation, and the same is
true of its adjoint.  Multiplying the two representations gives
\begin{align}
 \hat N_\chi={}&
 \hat a_A^\dagger\hat a_A\otimes\One_{\Abar}
 +\hat a_A^\dagger\hat P_A\otimes\hat a_{\Abar}
 \nonumber\\
 &+\hat P_A\hat a_A\otimes\hat a_{\Abar}^\dagger
 +\One_A\otimes\hat a_{\Abar}^\dagger\hat a_{\Abar}.
 \label{eq:number-split}
\end{align}
Similarly, $\One-\hat N_\chi$ is the sum of the four product terms obtained
by replacing the first factor in the first term by
$\One_A-\hat a_A^\dagger\hat a_A$ and changing the signs of the remaining
three terms.  We have therefore proved the following circuit-specific ranks.

\begin{proposition}[OW branch ranks]
For an OW mode crossing the cut,
\begin{equation}
 r(\hat a_\chi),r(\hat a_\chi^\dagger)\leq2,
 \qquad
 r(\hat N_\chi),r(\One-\hat N_\chi)\leq4.
 \label{eq:ow-ranks}
\end{equation}
Consequently every nonzero trajectory record $\boldsymbol m$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \Delta S_0^{\boldsymbol m}(A)
 \leq2\log2\,N_=^\partial(\boldsymbol m)
 +\log2\,N_{\ne}^\partial(\boldsymbol m).}
 \label{eq:record-bound}
\end{equation}
Here $N_=^\partial$ counts crossing branches with $m=s$, whose Kraus
operator is a rank-at-most-four projector, and $N_{\ne}^\partial$ counts
crossing branches with $m\ne s$, whose Kraus operator is
$\hat a_\chi$ or $\hat a_\chi^\dagger$.
\end{proposition}

The ranks in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ow-ranks} are upper bounds: they can decrease for
special orbitals or cuts.  Equation~\eqref{eq:record-bound}, however, is
deterministic once the physical record and cut are fixed.  It requires
neither averaging nor a minimum Born probability and can be checked directly
along any pure trajectory.

\section{Two-dimensional interface application}

The finite-range class-A controller acts on an $N_x\times N_y$ torus.  Its
two periodic domain walls run in the $y$ direction.  The entropy diagnostic
uses the full-width strip
\begin{equation}
 A_\ell=\{(x,y):0\leq x<N_x,\ 0\leq y<\ell\}.
 \label{eq:strip}
\end{equation}
As Fig.~\ref{fig:cuts}(b) shows, $\partial A_\ell$ has two transverse
components of length $N_x$:
\begin{equation}
 |\partial A_\ell|=2N_x.
 \label{eq:strip-boundary}
\end{equation}
Localizing an entanglement contour near a wall does not change this
subsystem or turn Eq.~\eqref{eq:strip-boundary} into an $O(1)$ boundary.

Suppose a pure product input is prepared by a local circuit into states whose
strip entropy has the proposed two-wall conformal form
\begin{equation}
 S_1(A_\ell)=b(N_x)+\frac{c_L+c_R}{6}
 \log\!\left[
  \frac{N_y}{\pi a}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{N_y}\right)
 \right]+o(1),
 \label{eq:cft-entropy}
\end{equation}
where $a$ is a microscopic cutoff~\cite{CalabreseCardy2004}.  At
$\ell=N_y/2$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:renyi-bound} and
\eqref{eq:strip-boundary} imply, conditional on this CFT scaling,
\begin{gather}
 2\kappa D_{\rm circ}N_x
 \geq\frac{c_L+c_R}{6}\log N_y-O(1),\nonumber\\[-1mm]
 \boxed{D_{\rm circ}N_x=\Omega(\log N_y).}
 \label{eq:2d-resource}
\end{gather}
For fixed $N_x$ this recovers a logarithmic depth requirement.  When the
transverse resource $N_x$ grows, however, the Lu--Lessa--Kim--Hsieh theorem
permits smaller depth, including size-independent depth when the boundary
capacity is already at least logarithmic.  This is the direct reason that a
critical interface in a two-dimensional local circuit does not contradict
the intrinsically one-dimensional conclusion.

The record-resolved statement is sharper.  Combining
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:record-bound} and \eqref{eq:cft-entropy} at half circumference
gives, for every trajectory that individually exhibits the stated entropy,
\begin{equation}
 2N_=^\partial(\boldsymbol m)+N_{\ne}^\partial(\boldsymbol m)
 \geq\frac{c_L+c_R}{6\log2}\log N_y-O(1).
 \label{eq:record-resource-general}
\end{equation}
For two oppositely oriented complex-fermion walls,
$c_L=c_R=1$, so
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 2N_=^\partial(\boldsymbol m)+N_{\ne}^\partial(\boldsymbol m)
 \geq\frac{\log N_y}{3\log2}-O(1).}
 \label{eq:record-resource}
\end{equation}
If only the Born average $\overline{S_1}$ is known to have the logarithmic
coefficient, averaging the branchwise inequality yields the corresponding
bound on
$2\overline{N_=^\partial}+\overline{N_{\ne}^\partial}$; it does not by itself
establish Eq.~\eqref{eq:record-resource} for every record.  Conversely, the
fact that the output is an ensemble supplies no loophole: the underlying
upper bound, Eq.~\eqref{eq:record-bound}, holds before averaging for every
nonzero trajectory.

\section{Assumptions, scope, and conclusion}

The conclusions above use the following assumptions explicitly.

\emph{Pure nonzero branches.---}The Schmidt-rank theorem concerns normalized
pure trajectories with nonzero Born probability.  It does not bound an
entanglement measure of the discarded-record mixed state.

\emph{Bounded quantum locality.---}The depth corollary assumes fixed onsite
dimension, bounded coordination, bounded operation diameter, and only a
constant local ancillary overhead.  It applies directly to OW modes truncated
at fixed $n_{\rm shell}$.  Exact OW modes with exponential tails are not
strictly bounded-support operations; a range that grows with system size
must be retained in the constants instead of being called $O(1)$.

\emph{Input state.---}Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-bound} applies to any pure
input, but the preparation lower bounds
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:2d-resource}--\eqref{eq:record-resource} use
$S_0^{\rm in}=0$.  A generic random Slater determinant is already spatially
entangled.  A maximally mixed state is not a pure input; applying a pure-state
bound requires a specified purification and a cut that includes the retained
reference modes.

\emph{Physical depth and encoding.---}$D_{\rm circ}$ denotes actual layers
of mutually nonoverlapping bounded-support quantum operations, not an
algorithmic update counter.  The locality statement is native to the graded
fermionic tensor product.  A qubit realization must separately include the
locality and depth overhead of its fermion-to-qubit encoding.

Within these assumptions, the logical status is unambiguous.  The general
bound is branchwise; the OW Kraus operators satisfy it with the explicit ranks
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ow-ranks}; and the full-width strip supplies a boundary of
size $2N_x$.  The circuit therefore obeys the Lu--Lessa--Kim--Hsieh bound.
What is not proved here is that the wall realizes the conformal form
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft-entropy}, how rapidly it converges, or that it is prepared in
constant total depth.  Those are independent physical questions.  This note
establishes only the entangling-capacity theorem, its record-resolved fermionic
refinement, and the conditional resource constraint that any such interface
state must satisfy.
