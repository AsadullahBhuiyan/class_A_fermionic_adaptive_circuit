\affiliation{Repository methods note}
\date{September 1, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We give a self-contained account of interval R\'enyi entropy and charge full counting
statistics for Born-conditioned, number-conserving Gaussian trajectories.  The restricted
correlation spectrum determines both observables, while their logarithmic chord-length
coefficients measure different infrared data: the total conformal entanglement content
seen by the cut and the level of the physical $U(1)$ current algebra.  We fix all
one-wall and two-wall factors by introducing explicit branch sums, derive the cylinder
charge variance from the Kac--Moody correlator, and formulate a paired null test for a
minimal $U(1)_1$ sector.  We carefully distinguish Born-averaged conditional entropies,
annealed replica moments, the entropy of the Born mixture, within-trajectory quantum
charge variance, and record-to-record charge wandering.  Finally, we turn the formalism
into a reanalysis-first validation program.  The program begins with the accepted exact
domain-wall spectra and archived stochastic covariance snapshots, invokes a fixed-record
engine comparison only if old and current outputs must be pooled, and permits new dynamics
only when archived data are statistically inconclusive.  Static entropy and charge
fluctuations never determine chirality; that claim requires a separate signed propagation
or flux diagnostic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose, scope, and hierarchy of claims}
\label{sec:scope}

The objective is not to infer a conformal field theory (CFT) from one successful entropy
fit.  It is to separate four logically distinct questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does the interval entanglement have a stable logarithmic coefficient compatible
with a specified total central charge?
\item Does the \emph{within-trajectory} interval charge variance have the coefficient of
a level-one $U(1)$ Kac--Moody current?
\item Do the entropy and charge coefficients obey the paired relation expected when the
same minimal charged sector accounts for both signals?
\item Is that sector chiral, and if so what is its handedness?
\end{enumerate}
The first three questions are static and unsigned.  The fourth is dynamical or topological
and requires an independent orientation-sensitive packet, response, or flux experiment.
The note therefore uses the following claim hierarchy.

The formal development treats arbitrary R\'enyi order $n>0$.  Numerical validation is
restricted to $n=1,2,3$.  Charged or symmetry-resolved R\'enyi entropies and paired/Nambu
Gaussian states are outside the present scope.

\section{Conditioned Gaussian trajectories and interval geometry}
\label{sec:gaussian-setup}

\subsection{Canonical correlation matrix and archive crosswalk}

Let the complex fermions obey
\begin{equation}
 \{\hat\psi_i^\dagger,\hat\psi_j\}=\delta_{ij},\qquad
 \{\hat\psi_i,\hat\psi_j\}=0 .
\end{equation}
For a normalized many-body density operator $\hat\rho_\xi$ conditioned on a complete
Born-sampled record $\xi$, the canonical single-particle correlation matrix is
\begin{equation}
 G_{\xi,ij}=\Tr(\hat\rho_\xi\hat\psi_i^\dagger\hat\psi_j),
 \qquad 0\leq G_\xi\leq\Id .
 \label{eq:G-definition}
\end{equation}
The record label $\xi$ is not an additional quantum degree of freedom.  It indexes the
classical ensemble generated by the adaptive free-fermion protocol of
Ref.~\cite{BhuiyanPanJian2026}.

This note reserves $G$ for the occupation correlation matrix in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:G-definition}.  Many archived analyses call the same object $C$; after
conversion to the occupation convention their symbol $C_{\xi,A}$ is exactly our
$G_{\xi,A}$.  Some older raw products instead store a centered covariance and sometimes
its transpose.  Those files require the metadata-declared affine/transpose conversion
before the present formulas apply.  In particular, the analysis must never guess the
meaning of a stored array from the variable name alone.  The provenance manifest records
the source convention and the explicit map to $G$ for every input.

For a spatial region $A$, let $P_A$ be its one-body projector and
\begin{equation}
 G_{\xi,A}=P_AG_\xi P_A\big|_A,
 \qquad
 G_{\xi,A}u_{\xi a}=\nu_{\xi a}u_{\xi a},
 \qquad 0\leq\nu_{\xi a}\leq1.
 \label{eq:restricted-spectrum}
\end{equation}
All nonlinear observables below are evaluated on $G_{\xi,A}$ before the Born average.
Replacing $G_\xi$ by $\overline G=\E_\xi[G_\xi]$ changes the observable and is not an
allowed shortcut.

\subsection{Cylinder interval, chord coordinate, and branch count}

The stochastic wall geometries are periodic along $y$ with circumference $N_y$.  A
full-transverse strip of length $\ell$ and origin $y_0$ is
\begin{equation}
 A(y_0,\ell)=[0,N_x)\times
 \{y_0,y_0+1,\ldots,y_0+\ell-1\},
 \label{eq:strip-region}
\end{equation}
with $y$ understood modulo $N_y$.  The logarithmic cylinder coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 X_{N_y}(\ell)=
 \log\!\left[\frac{N_y}{\pi a}
 \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{N_y}\right)\right],
 \qquad 0<\ell<N_y,
 \label{eq:chord-coordinate}
\end{equation}
where the microscopic cutoff $a$ changes the intercept but not the slope.

Let $w$ label the independent gapless wall branches intersected by the entanglement cut,
and define the explicit sums
\begin{equation}
 c_\Sigma=\sum_w c_w,
 \qquad
 k_\Sigma=\sum_w k_w .
 \label{eq:branch-sums}
\end{equation}
These definitions prevent the most common factor-of-two error.  A single chiral branch
has $c_\Sigma=c_w$ and $k_\Sigma=k_w$.  A full strip crossing two well-separated,
oppositely oriented but otherwise identical walls has $c_\Sigma=2c_w$ and
$k_\Sigma=2k_w$.  Orientation does not change either unsigned sum.

Within a trajectory, all periodic origins and all retained stationary snapshots are
correlated internal averages.  The trajectory, not an origin or snapshot, is the
independent sampling unit.

\section{Reduced Gaussian state and R\'enyi entropies}
\label{sec:renyi}

\subsection{Natural-orbital factorization}

Number conservation and Gaussianity imply that the reduced state factorizes in the
natural-orbital basis of Eq.~\eqref{eq:restricted-spectrum}~\cite{Peschel2003}:
\begin{align}
 \hat\rho_{\xi,A}
 &=\frac{\exp[-\sum_a\epsilon_{\xi a}
 \hat f_{\xi a}^\dagger\hat f_{\xi a}]}{Z_{\xi,A}},
 &
 \epsilon_{\xi a}&=\log\frac{1-\nu_{\xi a}}{\nu_{\xi a}},
 \label{eq:rhoA-modular}\\
 \hat\rho_{\xi,A}
 &=\bigotimes_a\left[(1-\nu_{\xi a})\lvert0_a\rangle\!\rangle
 \langle\!\langle0_a\rvert+\nu_{\xi a}\lvert1_a\rangle\!\rangle
 \langle\!\langle1_a\rvert\right].
 \label{eq:rhoA-product}
\end{align}
The one-body modular matrix is
\begin{equation}
 h^{\mathrm{mod}}_{\xi,A}
 =\log[(\Id_A-G_{\xi,A})G_{\xi,A}^{-1}],
 \label{eq:modular-matrix}
\end{equation}
with eigenvalues $\epsilon_{\xi a}$ and endpoints interpreted by continuity.

For $n>0$, $n\neq1$, the trajectory R\'enyi entropy is
\begin{align}
 S_{n,\xi}(A)
 &=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\Tr(\hat\rho_{\xi,A}^{\,n})
 \nonumber\\
 &=\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_a
 \log[\nu_{\xi a}^{\,n}+(1-\nu_{\xi a})^{n}].
 \label{eq:renyi-spectrum}
\end{align}
The continuous $n\to1$ limit is the von Neumann entropy
\begin{equation}
 S_{1,\xi}(A)=-\sum_a\left[
 \nu_{\xi a}\log\nu_{\xi a}
 +(1-\nu_{\xi a})\log(1-\nu_{\xi a})\right].
 \label{eq:von-neumann-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:renyi-spectrum} and \eqref{eq:von-neumann-spectrum} are exact for every
number-conserving Gaussian reduced state, whether the global Gaussian state is pure or
mixed.
For a globally pure conditioned trajectory they quantify bipartite entanglement.  For a
globally mixed conditioned Gaussian state they are reduced-state entropies; an
entanglement/CFT interpretation requires the separately declared purity or purification
gate.

\subsection{Replica coefficient on the cylinder}

In a chiral CFT, the replica twist field for one branch has scaling dimension
\begin{equation}
 h_n=\frac{c_w}{24}\left(n-\frac1n\right).
\end{equation}
The two-point function of the twist endpoints on the cylinder therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 S_n^{(w)}(\ell)=
 \frac{c_w}{12}\left(1+\frac1n\right)X_{N_y}(\ell)
 +b_{w,n}+\cdots ,
 \label{eq:single-branch-renyi}
\end{equation}
where the ellipsis contains irrelevant-operator, lattice, wall-overlap, and finite-size
corrections~\cite{CalabreseCardy2004,CalabreseCardy2009}.  Summing independent branches,
\begin{equation}
 \overline{S_n(\ell)}=
 a_{S_n}X_{N_y}(\ell)+b_{S_n}+\cdots,
 \qquad
 \boxed{a_{S_n}=\frac{c_\Sigma}{12}
 \left(1+\frac1n\right)}.
 \label{eq:renyi-slope}
\end{equation}
For two identical wall branches this yields the per-wall estimator
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{c_{w,n}=\frac{6a_{S_n}}{1+1/n}}.
 \label{eq:per-wall-c}
\end{equation}
For $n=1$, $c_{w,1}=3a_{S_1}$.  Agreement among $n=1,2,3$ is stronger than one
successful von Neumann fit because the three kernels probe the same correlation spectrum
differently.

\section{Charge full counting statistics}
\label{sec:fcs}

Let the physical charge in $A$ be
\begin{equation}
 \hat Q_A=\sum_{i\in A}\hat\psi_i^\dagger\hat\psi_i .
\end{equation}
Klich's trace identity gives the trajectory-resolved characteristic function
\cite{Klich2003}
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Z_{\xi,A}(\lambda)
 =\Tr(\hat\rho_{\xi,A}\e^{\ii\lambda\hat Q_A})
 =\det[\Id_A-G_{\xi,A}+\e^{\ii\lambda}G_{\xi,A}]
 =\prod_a(1-\nu_{\xi a}+\e^{\ii\lambda}\nu_{\xi a}).
 \label{eq:fcs-determinant}
\end{equation}
With
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_{j,\xi}(A)=(-\ii\partial_\lambda)^j
 \log\mathcal Z_{\xi,A}(\lambda)\big|_{\lambda=0},
\end{equation}
the first four cumulants are
\begin{align}
 \kappa_{1,\xi}&=\tr G_{\xi,A},
 \label{eq:kappa1}\\
 \kappa_{2,\xi}&=\tr[G_{\xi,A}(\Id_A-G_{\xi,A})],
 \label{eq:kappa2}\\
 \kappa_{3,\xi}&=\tr[G_{\xi,A}(\Id_A-G_{\xi,A})(\Id_A-2G_{\xi,A})],
 \label{eq:kappa3}\\
 \kappa_{4,\xi}&=\tr[G_{\xi,A}(\Id_A-G_{\xi,A})
 (\Id_A-6G_{\xi,A}+6G_{\xi,A}^2)].
 \label{eq:kappa4}
\end{align}
We write
\begin{equation}
 F_{\xi,A}\equiv\kappa_{2,\xi}(A)
 =\langle(\hat Q_A-\langle\hat Q_A\rangle_\xi)^2\rangle_\xi
 \label{eq:quantum-variance}
\end{equation}
for the \emph{within-trajectory quantum variance}.  It is this quantity, averaged after
evaluation, that probes the current algebra.

\subsection{Five inequivalent ensemble constructions}
\label{sec:ensemble-constructions}

The following objects must not be conflated:
\begin{align}
 \overline{S_n(A)}
 &=\frac{1}{1-n}\E_\xi[\log\Tr(\hat\rho_{\xi,A}^{\,n})],
 &&\text{Born-averaged conditional entropy},
 \label{eq:quenched-renyi}\\
 S_n^{\mathrm{ann}}(A)
 &=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\E_\xi[\Tr(\hat\rho_{\xi,A}^{\,n})],
 &&\text{annealed replica moment},
 \label{eq:annealed-renyi}\\
 S_n^{\mathrm{mix}}(A)
 &=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\Tr[(\overline{\hat\rho_A})^n],
 &&\overline{\hat\rho_A}=\E_\xi[\hat\rho_{\xi,A}],
 \label{eq:mixture-renyi}\\
 \overline{F_A}
 &=\E_\xi[F_{\xi,A}],
 &&\text{conditional quantum charge variance},
 \label{eq:mean-quantum-variance}\\
 F_A^{\mathrm{rec}}
 &=\Var_\xi(\tr G_{\xi,A}),
 &&\text{record-to-record charge wandering}.
 \label{eq:record-wandering}
\end{align}
For $n>1$, Jensen's inequality generally separates
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:quenched-renyi} and \eqref{eq:annealed-renyi}.  The Born mixture in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture-renyi} is generally non-Gaussian even when every conditional state
is Gaussian; its entropy is not obtained by inserting $\overline G$ into the Gaussian
formula.  Finally, the physical two-stage experiment that first draws a record and then
measures $\hat Q_A$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 \Var_{\mathrm{two\ stage}}(Q_A)=\overline{F_A}+F_A^{\mathrm{rec}}.
 \label{eq:total-variance}
\end{equation}
Only the first term belongs in the Kac--Moody estimator.
Relations between free-fermion charge statistics, bipartite fluctuations, and entanglement
are developed in Refs.~\cite{KlichLevitov2009,SongFlindtRachel2011,
SongRachelFlindtKlichLaflorencieLeHur2012}; the conditional-versus-record distinctions
above are essential when importing those relations into a monitored ensemble.

\section{The level of the \texorpdfstring{$U(1)$}{U(1)} current algebra}
\label{sec:kac-moody}

\subsection{Normalization and cylinder correlator}

We normalize the holomorphic current by
\begin{equation}
 J(z)J(0)\sim\frac{k_w}{z^2},
 \qquad
 J(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}J_mz^{-m-1},
 \qquad
 [J_m,J_n]=k_wm\,\delta_{m+n,0}.
 \label{eq:km-algebra}
\end{equation}
The microscopic charge unit is held fixed throughout.  A charge-one chiral complex
fermion has $k_w=1$.  In this convention the Sugawara stress tensor of one Abelian
current is
\begin{equation}
 T_J(z)=\frac{1}{2k_w}:J(z)J(z):,
 \label{eq:sugawara}
\end{equation}
and carries central charge one; a theory may nevertheless contain additional neutral
gapless sectors~\cite{DiFrancescoMathieuSenechal1997}.
For an integer physical charge sector $Q$, the pure $U(1)$ charged primary has
$h_Q=Q^2/(2k_w)$; a neutral sector can add to this dimension.  Subsystem FCS fixes the
current coefficient but does not by itself resolve this operator tower.

To fix the numerical coefficient rather than absorb it into the definition of $k_w$,
write the cylinder coordinate as $w=\tau+\ii y$ and map it to the plane by
$z=\exp(2\pi w/N_y)$.  A weight-one current transforms as
\begin{equation}
 J_{\mathrm{cyl}}(w)=\frac{2\pi}{N_y}zJ(z),
 \qquad
 \hat\rho_w(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}J_{\mathrm{cyl}}(\ii y),
 \label{eq:cylinder-current-map}
\end{equation}
where the second equality fixes the microscopic unit-charge normalization.  Since
$z/(z-1)^2=-[4\sin^2(\pi y/N_y)]^{-1}$ at equal time, the noncoincident connected
density correlator of one branch is
\begin{equation}
 \langle\hat\rho_w(y)\hat\rho_w(0)\rangle_c
 =-\frac{k_w}{4\pi^2}
 \left(\frac{\pi}{N_y}\right)^2
 \csc^2\!\left(\frac{\pi y}{N_y}\right),
 \qquad y\neq0,
 \label{eq:density-correlator}
\end{equation}
up to the contact term that fixes the ultraviolet charge variance.  Double integration
over an interval, or equivalently the derivation in Appendix~\ref{app:cylinder}, gives
\begin{equation}
 F_w(\ell)=\frac{k_w}{2\pi^2}X_{N_y}(\ell)+b_{F,w}+\cdots .
 \label{eq:single-wall-F}
\end{equation}
Independent branches add, hence
\begin{equation}
 \overline{F(\ell)}=a_FX_{N_y}(\ell)+b_F+\cdots,
 \qquad
 \boxed{a_F=\frac{k_\Sigma}{2\pi^2}}.
 \label{eq:F-slope}
\end{equation}
For two identical wall branches,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{k_w=\pi^2a_F}.
 \label{eq:per-wall-k}
\end{equation}
The coefficient determines the level only after the microscopic charge normalization and
the number of intersected branches have been declared.  It does not determine the sign of
a propagation velocity or a chiral central charge.

\section{Shared susceptibility spectrum}
\label{sec:shared-spectrum}

Define the positive single-particle susceptibility operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V_{\xi,A}=G_{\xi,A}(\Id_A-G_{\xi,A}),
 \qquad
 v_{\xi a}=\nu_{\xi a}(1-\nu_{\xi a})\in[0,1/4].
 \label{eq:susceptibility}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 F_{\xi,A}&=\tr\mathcal V_{\xi,A}=\sum_av_{\xi a},
 \label{eq:F-from-V}\\
 S_{n,\xi}(A)&=\tr g_n(\mathcal V_{\xi,A}),
 \label{eq:S-from-V}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
 g_n(v)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log[\nu(v)^n+(1-\nu(v))^n],
 \qquad
 \nu(v)=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4v}}{2}.
 \label{eq:gn-v}
\end{equation}
The branch choice in $\nu(v)$ is irrelevant because the entropy kernel is symmetric under
$\nu\leftrightarrow1-\nu$.  The scalar $F$ is only the first moment of
$\{v_a\}$ and does not determine $S_n$ at finite size; the full susceptibility spectrum
does determine every Gaussian R\'enyi entropy.
The map $\nu_a\mapsto v_a$ discards the branch information
$\nu_a\leftrightarrow1-\nu_a$.  Consequently $\mathcal V_A$ determines $F$, every
$S_n$, and even cumulants such as $\kappa_4$ that are symmetric under this exchange, but
it does not determine the mean charge $\kappa_1$ or odd cumulants such as $\kappa_3$.

In modular-energy variables,
\begin{align}
 \nu(\epsilon)&=\frac{1}{1+\e^\epsilon},
 &
 f(\epsilon)&=\nu(1-\nu)=\frac{1}{4\cosh^2(\epsilon/2)},
 \label{eq:f-kernel}\\
 s_n(\epsilon)&=\frac{
 \log[2\cosh(n\epsilon/2)]-n\log[2\cosh(\epsilon/2)]}{1-n},
 \quad &(n\neq1),
 \label{eq:sn-kernel}\\
 s_1(\epsilon)&=\log[2\cosh(\epsilon/2)]
 -\frac{\epsilon}{2}\tanh(\epsilon/2).
 \label{eq:s1-kernel}
\end{align}
If $\rho_{\xi,A}(\epsilon)=\sum_a\delta(\epsilon-\epsilon_{\xi a})$, then
\begin{equation}
 F_{\xi,A}=\int\dd\epsilon\,\rho_{\xi,A}(\epsilon)f(\epsilon),
 \qquad
 S_{n,\xi}(A)=\int\dd\epsilon\,\rho_{\xi,A}(\epsilon)s_n(\epsilon).
 \label{eq:kernel-integrals-density}
\end{equation}
The exact kernel integrals are
\begin{equation}
 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\epsilon\,f(\epsilon)=1,
 \qquad
 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\epsilon\,s_n(\epsilon)
 =\frac{\pi^2}{6}\left(1+\frac1n\right),
 \label{eq:kernel-integrals}
\end{equation}
with the $n=1$ value $\pi^2/3$.  Thus a common slowly varying low-modular-energy density
produces the universal ratio of \emph{leading logarithmic coefficients}.  Equation
\eqref{eq:kernel-integrals} is not a mode-by-mode identity and does not imply
$S_n(\ell)\propto F(\ell)$ at each finite $\ell$.

\section{Paired minimal-\texorpdfstring{$U(1)_1$}{U(1)1} test}
\label{sec:paired-test}

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:renyi-slope} and \eqref{eq:F-slope}, define
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\delta_n=
 a_{S_n}-\frac{\pi^2}{6}\left(1+\frac1n\right)a_F}
 =\frac{c_\Sigma-k_\Sigma}{12}\left(1+\frac1n\right).
 \label{eq:delta-n}
\end{equation}
For a charge-one free complex-fermion branch, $c_w=k_w=1$, so $\delta_n=0$ for every
$n$.  The strongest numerical test is paired: $S_n$ and $F$ are extracted from the same
trajectory, snapshot, origin-averaging prescription, interval set, and regression design.
Whole-trajectory resampling then retains their covariance.  Testing two independently
estimated slopes and discarding their covariance is unnecessarily weak.

The correct inference ladder is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Estimate $c_{w,n}$ and $k_w$ separately, with branch count fixed by geometry.
\item Test $\delta_n=0$ jointly for $n=1,2,3$ using the paired bootstrap covariance.
\item Test window, endpoint, size, construction, and matched-trivial stability.
\item Invoke ``minimal $U(1)_1$'' only if $c_{w,n}\simeq k_w\simeq1$ and the null test
passes without a resolved neutral excess.
\item Invoke ``chiral'' only after a separate signed diagnostic passes orientation
reversal and wall-off controls.
\end{enumerate}
A failed $\delta_n$ blocks the minimal-sector statement but leaves independently valid
$c$ and $k$ estimates intact.

\section{Numerical validation ladder}
\label{sec:validation-ladder}

\subsection{Tier 0: immutable archived-data reanalysis}

The first tier performs no dynamics and never edits source campaigns.  The committed
builder has completed the following steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Recomputed $F,S_1,S_2,S_3$, $c_{w,n}$, $k_w$, and $\delta_n$ from the accepted
B0 spectra and reproduced Table~\ref{tab:exact-b0} within numerical tolerance.
\item Resolved the raw inputs for atlas Result 21 from its source manifest, reproduced the
four $k_w$ values and their per-trajectory distributions, and computed $S_2,S_3$ from the
same restricted spectra without treating origins as independent samples.
\item Reduced the 160 complete prior-production trajectories for the larger-size paired
$S_1$--$F$ test and matched-trivial null.  Preserve construction, system size, and sample
axes.  Do not silently pool the explicit and support-terminated interfaces.
\item Recorded input paths, byte counts, SHA-256 digests, stored-array conventions,
conversion to canonical $G$, available engine/source identity, geometry, initialization,
cycle, sample ID, fit window, censor flags, and numerical status; unavailable legacy
seed and engine identities are marked explicitly rather than inferred.
\end{enumerate}

The primary regression design is ordinary least squares with a free intercept and a fixed,
prespecified fit window:
\begin{align}
 \bar S_{n,\xi}(\ell)&=a_{S_n,\xi}X_{N_y}(\ell)+b_{S_n,\xi},
 \\
 \bar F_{\xi}(\ell)&=a_{F,\xi}X_{N_y}(\ell)+b_{F,\xi}.
 \label{eq:paired-regression}
\end{align}
The same design matrix is used for every trajectory in a configuration.  Because the
slope estimator is linear, the slope of the ensemble-mean curve equals the mean of the
trajectory slopes when the window and weights are shared; displaying both nevertheless
tests fit shape and trajectory typicality.  Confidence intervals and joint tests resample
whole trajectories.

Sensitivity analyses are declared rather than selected from the data: move the lower
endpoint by the B0 increments, delete the largest included interval, fit the two
complementary branches separately for pure snapshots, and compare the log-chord model to
the declared noncritical alternative.  A trivial control must not acquire a stable
critical coefficient merely because a short curve admits a high $R^2$.

\subsection{Tier 1: fixed-record engine compatibility}

Tier 1 is invoked only if old- and current-engine observables must be pooled into one
numerical estimate.  Select one fixed geometry and seed, replay the identical ordered
record with both engine revisions, and compare:
\begin{equation}
 \{\nu_a(\ell),S_1(\ell),S_2(\ell),S_3(\ell),F(\ell),
 a_{S_n},a_F,c_{w,n},k_w,\delta_n\}
\end{equation}
for every declared interval.  Record array norms and phase/transpose conventions before
comparing spectra.  A mismatch blocks pooling and is classified as a source-version or
protocol discrepancy; it does not retroactively invalidate the internally consistent
legacy evidence.

\subsection{Tier 2: targeted top-up only after a statistical failure}

New trajectories are authorized only when Tier 0 passes formula, provenance, geometry,
and fit-stability checks but its whole-trajectory confidence intervals are too broad.
The fixed top-up design is:
\begin{itemize}
\item primary explicit-interface wall and a separately run, geometry- and update-count-
matched trivial control;
\item $N_x=20$, $N_y=40,50,60$, $n_{\mathrm{shell}}=1$;
\item pure initialization, random-serial schedule, and total evolution $T=2N_y$;
\item first increase each configuration to $S=25$ independent trajectories;
increase to $S=50$ only if central values and fit stability pass while the target joint
confidence interval remains unresolved;
\item save per-trajectory origin-averaged $S_1,S_2,S_3,\kappa_2$, fit diagnostics,
purity metadata, and optionally $\kappa_3,\kappa_4$---not dense covariance histories that
are unnecessary for the declared estimator.
\end{itemize}
A systematic failure of the log-chord form, branch counting, matched control, or
$\delta_n$ is not cured by increasing $S$.  It triggers a claim downgrade or a separately
versioned protocol investigation.

\section{Acceptance criteria and failure semantics}
\label{sec:acceptance}

The implementation is accepted only if all applicable checks below pass.
\begin{enumerate}
\item On small systems, direct Fock-space traces, correlation-eigenvalue formulas, trace
polynomials, and determinant FCS agree to absolute error $10^{-10}$.
\item The fast origin-average estimator agrees with explicit restricted-submatrix
construction at representative intervals in every source convention.
\item Eigenvalues near zero and one are evaluated stably.  Roundoff clipping, purity
deficits, non-Hermiticity, and out-of-range counts are reported rather than hidden.
\item Exact B0 values in Table~\ref{tab:exact-b0} reproduce to their locked numerical
tolerance, and the four Result-21 levels reproduce with their declared formulas.
\item Wall data prefer stable log-chord scaling over the declared alternative while
matched trivial controls do not show a false stable coefficient.
\item Whole trajectories are the bootstrap unit.  Origins, intervals, and stationary
snapshots never inflate $S$.
\item $c_{w,n}$, $k_w$, and $\delta_n$ are tested jointly under the fixed-window and
endpoint-deletion analyses.  The report includes their paired covariance.
\item A failed $\delta_n$ blocks ``minimal $U(1)_1$'' but preserves separately supported
central-charge and current-level statements.
\item Every generated figure and table is traceable to the analysis manifest, and every
source file is hashed before reading.
\end{enumerate}

The result vocabulary is fixed in advance:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Formula failure}: an implementation error; no physics result is reported.
\item \emph{Provenance or convention failure}: the affected source is quarantined from
pooling until its mapping to $G$ is resolved.
\item \emph{Fit instability}: report qualitative logarithmic tendency or no CFT
coefficient, according to the control comparison.
\item \emph{Separate $c$ and $k$ success but paired-null failure}: report the two
coefficients separately and do not identify a minimal $U(1)_1$ sector.
\item \emph{Static success without signed propagation}: report $U(1)_1$-consistent wall
state data, not chirality.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}

For number-conserving Gaussian trajectories, R\'enyi entropy and conditional quantum
charge variance are exact spectral functionals of one restricted correlation matrix.
Their common microscopic origin does not make them redundant: R\'enyi entropies weight the
entire susceptibility spectrum, whereas the variance measures its first moment, and their
CFT slopes determine $c_\Sigma$ and $k_\Sigma$ separately.  Explicit branch sums remove
the one-wall/two-wall ambiguity, and the paired residual $\delta_n$ turns the proposed
minimal-$U(1)_1$ identification into a falsifiable same-ensemble test.

The completed hashed, read-only Tier-0 reanalysis validates the formulas, provenance,
near-one entropy and charge coefficients, and wall log-chord preference.  The
matched-trivial coefficients remain inside the declared numerical-null band, although
their tiny numerical-floor curves can still prefer a log model by AIC; the literal
``trivial controls do not prefer log'' criterion is therefore not met.  Several
prespecified paired intervals also exclude $\delta_n=0$, so the strict minimal-$U(1)_1$
license is blocked at this stage even though separate $c$ and $k$ evidence remains.  A
systematic paired-null or control-model tension is not cured merely by adding
trajectories; it calls for the declared protocol, finite-size, and engine-compatibility
investigation.  No static value of $c$, $k$, or $\delta_n$ supplies handedness; chirality
remains an independent experimental claim.

\section{Cylinder integration of the current correlator}
\label{app:cylinder}

For one branch, define the regulated interval charge
\begin{equation}
 \hat Q_{[0,\ell]}=\int_0^\ell\dd y\,\hat\rho_w(y).
\end{equation}
Translation invariance gives
\begin{equation}
 F_w(\ell)=\int_0^\ell\dd y\int_0^\ell\dd y'\,
 \langle\hat\rho_w(y)\hat\rho_w(y')\rangle_c.
 \label{eq:F-double-integral}
\end{equation}
Away from the contact point,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd^2F_w}{\dd\ell^2}
 =2\langle\hat\rho_w(\ell)\hat\rho_w(0)\rangle_c
 =-\frac{k_w}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{\pi}{N_y}\right)^2
 \csc^2\!\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{N_y}\right).
\end{equation}
Integrating twice yields
\begin{equation}
 F_w(\ell)=\frac{k_w}{2\pi^2}
 \log\!\left[\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{N_y}\right)\right]
 +A\ell+B.
\end{equation}
Charge conservation and complement symmetry remove the nonuniversal linear term in the
periodic ground-state/conditioned fixed-charge setting.  Restoring the ultraviolet scale
inside the logarithm gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:single-wall-F}; the contact term changes only the
additive constant.  Summing uncorrelated branches gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:F-slope}.

\section{Stable spectral evaluation}
\label{app:stable-evaluation}

Hermitize the restricted matrix before diagonalization,
\begin{equation}
 G_{\xi,A}\leftarrow\frac12(G_{\xi,A}+G_{\xi,A}^\dagger),
\end{equation}
and report the pre-hermitization residual.  Eigenvalues outside $[0,1]$ by more than the
declared tolerance are failures.  Values outside only by roundoff may be clipped, but the
count and maximum correction enter the manifest.

For $S_1$, evaluate $-x\log x$ with its continuous value zero at $x=0$.  For $n=2,3$,
evaluate
\begin{equation}
 \log[\nu^n+(1-\nu)^n]
 =\operatorname{logaddexp}(n\log\nu,n\log(1-\nu))
\end{equation}
to avoid underflow.  Compute $F=\sum_a\nu_a(1-\nu_a)$ and compare with the trace formula
as an internal check.  The determinant FCS is evaluated through eigenvalue log sums near
$\lambda=0$ rather than a raw determinant when conditioning is poor.

For a direct small-system test, construct Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhoA-product} explicitly in its
$2^{|A|}$-dimensional Fock basis and compare $\Tr(\hat\rho_A^n)$ and
$\Tr(\hat\rho_A\e^{\ii\lambda\hat Q_A})$ against
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:renyi-spectrum} and \eqref{eq:fcs-determinant}.

\section{Origin-averaged block estimator}
\label{app:origin-average}

Group the full correlation matrix into $y$ blocks of dimension $d=2N_x$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \bar n=\frac1{N_y}\tr G_\xi,
 \qquad
 K_r=\frac1{N_y}\sum_{y=0}^{N_y-1}
 \lVert G_{\xi;y,y+r}\rVert_F^2 .
\end{equation}
Expanding $F=\tr G_A-\tr G_A^2$ and counting the number of ordered block pairs at each
separation gives
\begin{equation}
 \overline F_\xi(\ell)=
 \ell(\bar n-K_0)
 -\sum_{r=1}^{\ell-1}(\ell-r)(K_r+K_{-r}).
 \label{eq:fast-origin-average}
\end{equation}
The builder compares Eq.~\eqref{eq:fast-origin-average} with the explicit mean of
$\tr[G_{\xi,A(y_0,\ell)}(\Id_A-G_{\xi,A(y_0,\ell)})]$ at three or more interval lengths
for every convention and configuration.  Entropies still require the restricted spectra;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fast-origin-average} is not an entropy estimator.

\section{Pure versus mixed global Gaussian states}
\label{app:pure-mixed}

For a globally pure Slater determinant, $G_\xi^2=G_\xi$.  In the $A\oplus B$ block form,
the $AA$ projector identity gives
\begin{equation}
 G_{\xi,A}(\Id_A-G_{\xi,A})=G_{\xi,AB}G_{\xi,BA}.
 \label{eq:pure-cross-block}
\end{equation}
Thus $v_a$ are the squared singular values of the cross-boundary block and
\begin{equation}
 F_{\xi,A}=\lVert G_{\xi,AB}\rVert_F^2.
\end{equation}
For a globally mixed Gaussian state,
\begin{equation}
 G_{\xi,A}(\Id_A-G_{\xi,A})
 =G_{\xi,AB}G_{\xi,BA}+(G_\xi-G_\xi^2)_{AA}.
 \label{eq:mixed-cross-block}
\end{equation}
The second term is intrinsic global mixedness restricted to $A$.  A max-mix trajectory
must therefore pass its purification/stationarity gate before its interval coefficient is
interpreted as the same wall-entanglement observable as a pure trajectory.

\section{Estimator pseudocode and provenance contract}
\label{app:pseudocode}

For each immutable input trajectory, the analysis follows this order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item hash and identify the source before opening numerical arrays;
\item read metadata and convert the stored covariance convention to canonical $G$;
\item validate Hermiticity, spectral bounds, trace/charge, and purity status;
\item for every declared snapshot, interval, and periodic origin, restrict $G$ and compute
$\nu_a$, $S_1,S_2,S_3$, and $F$;
\item average origins and snapshots inside the trajectory;
\item apply the common fixed-window linear estimators to obtain trajectory slopes;
\item convert slopes using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:per-wall-c} and \eqref{eq:per-wall-k}, and form
$\delta_n$ from the paired slopes;
\item bootstrap complete trajectory IDs, preserving all observables and sizes selected in
the declared resample;
\item write machine-readable scalar rows, curves, diagnostics, figures, tables, and an
analysis manifest without modifying an input path.
\end{enumerate}
The manifest collectively records source paths, SHA-256 digests, byte counts, archive
members where applicable, stored and canonical conventions, available engine/source
hashes, case/sample/seed information, geometry, initialization, cycle and snapshot
aggregation, fit windows, branch counts, clipping and censor diagnostics, extracted
values, and output hashes.  Fields absent from a legacy source---notably Result-21 seed
and engine identities---are explicitly marked unavailable; they are not fabricated on
every source row.
